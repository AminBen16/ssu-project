# GEOGRAPHY SYLLABUS

**Subject:** Geography  
**Level:** O-Level (Lower Secondary)  
**Years:** Senior 1-4  
**Source:** NCDC Uganda, 2019  

---

## SENIOR 1

### THEME 1: INTRODUCTION TO GEOGRAPHY

#### Topic 1.1: Introduction (3 periods)
**Competency:** Understand and appreciate the importance of studying geography.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Use fieldwork to observe, name and classify human and physical features
- Know the meaning of geography and environment
- Understand that geography is the study of the relationship between people and their environment
- Appreciate that studying geography helps us understand how our lives are affected by the environment

#### Topic 1.2: Showing the local area on a map (4 periods)
**Competency:** Know the main physical and human features of the local area and how these can be shown on a map.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know what a map is and how it can be used to show places
- Understand the difference between a map and a photograph
- Understand that maps are representations of the world at different scales
- Be able to draw a sketch map of the school and/or local area
- Be able to use and interpret symbols and identify features on a map using a key
- Be able to identify directions on a map using basic compass points
- Be able to follow routes on a map

#### Topic 1.3: Maps and their uses (6 periods)
**Competency:** Know the main features of a map and understand the differences between a map and reality.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Draw a sketch map of the school and/or local area
- Use and interpret symbols and identify features on a map using a key
- Identify directions on a map using basic compass points
- Follow routes on a map
- Draw a map using a simple scale and use scale on a map
- Use letter and number coordinates or bearings and directions to locate places on a map
- Locate places on an atlas map using latitude and longitude
- Understand the difference between a map and a photograph
- Understand that there are many types of maps on different scales
- Use a linear scale and representative fraction to estimate distance, area and size of features on a map

#### Topic 1.4: Ways of studying geography (6 periods)
**Competency:** Understand and be able to study geography through the use of field work and through studying photographs.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know what is meant by field work
- Understand how to use and apply different techniques used in field work
- Use observation, interviews, questionnaires, drawings and photographs in field work
- Use maps, aerial images, photographs, reports, graphs and charts to communicate data
- Analyse and present statistics gathered in fieldwork
- Write conclusions to summarise field work
- Know the three different angles from which photographs can be taken
- Know the terms used to describe the different parts of a photograph
- Appreciate the effect of perspective on oblique photographs
- Differentiate between photographs and maps
- Describe an area seen on a photograph
- Make a sketch of an area from a photograph
- Appreciate that field work and photographs are important because geography is the study of the real world

#### Topic 1.5: The Earth and its movements (9 periods)
**Competency:** Understand the relationship between the Earth and the sun and the effects this has on our lives.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the relationship between the Earth and the sun and how this affects temperatures and seasons
- Draw diagrams to show the relationship between the Earth and the sun's rays and the causes of temperature variations
- Understand how the rotation causes day and night
- Know how we can locate places on a globe by using a grid including the use of latitude and longitude
- Use and measure latitude and longitude
- Calculate time using longitude
- Appreciate how the movement of the Earth in relation to the Sun affects the way people live

#### Topic 1.6: Weather and climate (8 periods)
**Competency:** Know and understand the main elements of the weather, their causes and how to measure them.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the differences between weather and climate
- Understand the elements of weather and how they are measured
- Carry out a project to observe, measure and record the elements of weather, make suitable instruments and visit a weather station
- Know the names of the main instruments used for recording the different elements of the weather and how each one is used
- Know what a Stevenson screen looks like, how it is built and why it is used
- Know the terms used for plotting weather on maps
- Know the names and characteristics of the main kinds of clouds and rainfall
- Appreciate that people's lifestyles are influenced by the type of weather and climate
- Understand the positive and negative effects of the weather on their own lives and those of their communities
- Be able to draw climate graphs of local and other areas and describe climates using these

### THEME 2: INTRODUCTION TO EAST AFRICA

#### Topic 2.1: Location, size, and relief regions of East Africa (3 periods)
**Competency:** Know the countries making up East Africa; their comparative sizes in area and population; and the main relief regions they can be divided into.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Use maps, statistics, graphs and diagrams to analyse population
- Appreciate that East African countries vary greatly in area and population
- Know the East African countries, their approximate population and area
- Use contours to show physical features on maps and draw cross-sections from simple contour maps

#### Topic 2.2: Formation of major landforms and drainage in East Africa (16 periods)
**Competency:** Explain how each of the main types of landforms in East Africa was formed, the main types of rocks and the main features of the drainage.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the main types of landforms and drainage features of East Africa
- Understand how igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks are formed, they are made up of minerals, and that some minerals are useful
- Understand the process of weathering and how weathered rock particles form the basis of soil
- Understand how each of the main types of landform was formed: by rocks themselves or by the rocks being worn away or eroded away
- Understand the characteristics of the main types of rocks and their effect on soils
- Understand the relationship between drainage the landforms
- Recognize the landforms on photographs
- Locate the examples of landforms on maps of East Africa
- Appreciate that the landforms, rocks and drainage all affect the way people live
- Understand the main concepts of plate tectonics and how this has led to the formation of the main physical features of East Africa
- Understand the characteristics of important kinds of physical features in East Africa, including mountain ranges, volcanoes, plateaus, basins and rift valleys
- Study through field work any of the above physical features in the local area
- Draw a map to show the main relief regions of East Africa
- Recognize physical features from photographs
- Understand how their own lives and the lives of their communities are affected by physical features, including natural hazards
- Understand through case studies how the physical features affect the lives of people in selected areas of East Africa
- Draw diagrams to show the formation of important physical features

#### Topic 2.3: Climate and natural vegetation of East Africa (8 periods)
**Competency:** Describe and explain the main types of climate in East Africa and how the climate influences the vegetation, appreciating this as a natural resource which can be used to benefit the present and future generations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the characteristics of the climates of Uganda and the rest of East Africa and the factors influencing them
- Understand through fieldwork the characteristics of the vegetation and how vegetation is affected by the climates
- Draw graphs to show the different climates
- Draw a map showing the climates and vegetation of East Africa
- Recognize and describe a climate from a graph
- Recognize and describe types of vegetation from photographs
- Understand through case studies how selected climates and types of vegetation affect the way of life of the people in those areas

#### Topic 2.4: Climate change in East Africa and the world (10 periods)
**Competency:** Understand what is meant by climate change, what is causing it and its effects in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the concept of climate change and its indicators and be able to draw diagrams to show the causes of climate change
- Understand the possible causes of climate change
- Understand the political and other difficulties faced in addressing climate change and carrying out measures to reduce its effects
- Understand how climate change may affect Uganda and the rest of East Africa
- Understand possible ways in which local communities and government can reduce the effects of climate change
- Use two case studies to examine the possible effects of climate change in other areas of Africa and the world
- Form opinions about measures to reduce or mitigate climate change
- Participate in tree planting and other activities which might alleviate the effects of climate change in their own communities/school
- Appreciate the need for people and governments to take actions to help reduce climate change, and change their lives to mitigate or reduce the effects of climate change

### THEME 3: WORLD CLIMATES; INTRODUCTION TO NORTH AMERICA; AGRICULTURE IN EAST AFRICA AND NORTH AMERICA

#### Topic 3.1: Major climatic zones of the world (8 periods)
**Competency:** Locate and describe the major climatic zones of the world and understand the relationship between climate and human activities and the lifestyles in each zone.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the names of some of the major climatic regions of the world
- Understand the main characteristics of the climates and how each affects the vegetation of the area
- Understand the factors influencing these characteristics
- Understand how people's ways of life are influenced by the climate in each region
- Plot the major climatic regions on a world map
- Recognize each type of region on photographs
- Understand how the traditional farming and way of life was influenced by the climate
- Appreciate that people's lifestyles are influenced by the type of weather and climate
- Appreciate that this influence is becoming less as technology helps people to overcome difficulties of their climate and as people move into urban areas

#### Topic 3.2: Geographical regions of North America (6 periods)
**Competency:** Know the main physical features, drainage, and climates of North America and brief history of North America.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the countries of North America
- Understand the main physical features of North America
- Know the main climatic regions of North America
- Understand how the size and position of North America makes it different from East Africa
- Understand how and why North America has developed differently from East Africa
- Draw a sketch map showing the countries and main regions of North America
- Appreciate the usefulness of learning about areas outside East Africa or Africa to contrast with East Africa
- Appreciate that all areas of the world are different, depending on their position and history
- Describe some climatic regions of North America, using climate graphs and compare these to East Africa

#### Topic 3.3: Development of agriculture in East Africa (14 periods)
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Carry out a field study to find out the nature of the land and water resources in the local area and the use of land and water resources for agriculture
- Understand the main types of agriculture in Uganda and the rest of East Africa and the factors influencing them
- Appreciate the importance of land and water as a source of food, livelihood and income for families, clans and communities
- Understand the differences between subsistence and commercial agriculture, and small scale and large-scale agriculture
- Understand the advantages and disadvantages of each of the main types of arable agriculture
- Know the characteristics of traditional pastoral farming
- Understand the advantages and disadvantages of commercial pastoral farming
- Form opinions about the dangers of over-use of land and water resources and the need to conserve these in the local area
- Recognize different types of farming from photographs
- Draw diagrams to show seasonal activities on farms
- Draw maps to show different types of farming
- Appreciate the importance of agriculture in the economies of East Africa
- Appreciate the need for government policies which assist farmers and develop farming
- Appreciate that caring for and preserving resources in the local environment, community and country are signs of love for one's country
- Understand possible ways in which local communities and governments can reduce the effects of climate change
- Take part in activities to improve local land and water resources

#### Topic 3.4: Some agricultural areas of North America: Large scale farming in the Prairies; Irrigated farming in California (8 periods)
**Competency:** Know and understand the main differences between agriculture in North America and agriculture in East Africa and whether methods used in North America are appropriate for Uganda.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know some important types of agriculture in North America; important crops grown and animals kept
- Understand the main differences between agriculture in North America and East Africa and the reasons for these differences
- Suggest whether some agricultural type of methods from North America are suitable for East Africa
- Use a range of sources including maps, photographs, Internet tables and graphs to research relevant information
- Locate the main types of agriculture on a map of North America
- Appreciate why methods of agriculture used in North America are not always suitable for Uganda
- Appreciate that development of agriculture depends on the level of economic development and technology

## SENIOR 2

### THEME 4: MINING AND MANUFACTURING IN EAST AFRICA AND NORTH AMERICA

#### Topic 4.1: Mining in East Africa (10 periods)
**Competency:** Know what minerals are found in East Africa, and understand the methods used to extract them, the factors favouring mining and its contribution to the East African economy.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand what a mineral is and why some minerals are valuable
- Locate the main mining centres on the map of East Africa
- Know the main minerals occurring in East Africa and their amounts
- Understand the use of mineral resources in the development of any two industries in Uganda
- Understand the methods of mining used for different minerals and their problems
- Draw flow diagrams to show the main stages and methods of mining
- Recognize types and consequences of mining on photographs
- Understand the effects of mining on the environment
- Appreciate the positive and negative contribution of mineral resources to development
- Appreciate that the benefits of mining often go mainly to overseas companies or a local elite only
- Understand the physical and economic problems facing mining
- Appreciate the need for strict laws to control mining physically and economically

#### Topic 4.2: Development of manufacturing industries in East Africa (12 periods)
**Competency:** Understand the advantages of developing manufacturing industries, the types and locations of manufacturing industries and the problems of developing manufacturing industries.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand what manufacturing industries are
- Know some types of manufacturing industries in East Africa and their locations
- Visit a local factory to find out how a factory works and show its effects on the environment
- Understand the factors influencing the distribution and development of any three manufacturing industries
- Appreciate the contribution of manufacturing industries in transforming primary products
- Understand the effects of manufacturing industries on the environment
- Understand the benefits manufacturing industries can bring to African countries
- Understand the problems of developing manufacturing industries in African countries
- Study, through research, examples of areas with manufacturing industries
- Draw a map showing important industrial areas in East Africa
- Appreciate why many African countries are trying to develop manufacturing industries

#### Topic 4.3: Mining and manufacturing industries in North America (The Great Lakes region and Silicon Valley) (14 periods)
**Competency:** Understand the importance of mining and manufacturing industry in North America especially the Great Lakes region and Silicon Valley and what we can learn from that for the development of industries in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the position of the Great Lakes region of North America and the main towns and industries there
- Know the main minerals in the Great Lakes region
- Know the position of Silicon Valley and the main industries there
- Understand the reasons for the development of industries in the Great Lakes region
- Know the importance of mining in this area
- Understand how mining and manufacturing industries are linked
- Understand the consequences of the development of industries in this area
- Understand the reasons for the decline of the traditional industries
- Understand how new technology is changing industrial development
- Understand the reasons for the development of industries in Silicon Valley
- Understand what East Africa can learn from the decline of the Great Lakes area and rise of Silicon Valley
- Appreciate that technology is changing industry
- Appreciate that East Africa has a lot to learn from changing technology in industrial development

### THEME 5: FISHING, WILDLIFE CONSERVATION AND TOURISM IN EAST AFRICA AND NORTH AMERICA

#### Topic 5.1: Sustainable use of fisheries resources in East Africa (10 periods)
**Competency:** Know the main fishing areas in East Africa, factors affecting the development of fishing, types of fishing and the contribution of fishing to the economy, the dangers facing fishing and ways to make it sustainable.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the main types of agriculture in Uganda and the rest of East Africa and the factors influencing them
- Know the major fishing areas in East Africa, inland and on the sea
- Understand the main methods of fishing and types of fish caught
- Understand the factors favouring fishing in East Africa
- Know the differences between traditional and modern fishing methods
- Describe the characteristics, trends, benefits and problems of fishing in a local area
- Understand the factors affecting the choice of methods
- Understand the dangers of over-fishing and how this can be prevented
- Understand the factors causing damage to fishing grounds, including pollution and how this can be prevented
- Know some methods of preserving fish after catching
- Understand the methods of farming fish and their help in conserving fish stocks
- Describe the marketing of fish
- Understand the methods of conserving fishing grounds
- Draw diagrams to illustrate methods of fishing
- Use statistics to show the decline in fish stocks and fish catches
- Appreciate the dangers facing fishing in East Africa, including over-fishing, poor methods and pollution
- Appreciate the need for strict laws and enforcement to preserve fish stocks

#### Topic 5.2: Wildlife conservation and tourism in East Africa (12 periods)
**Competency:** Understand the need for conserving wildlife, why tourists come to East Africa, what facilities they want, the benefits we get from tourism and how we can encourage more tourists to come.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know what is meant by wildlife
- Know the meaning of National Park, game/wild life reserves, sanctuaries, historic and prehistoric sites
- Understand the factors influencing the development of tourism in Uganda and the rest of East Africa
- Understand that tourism is an important form of trade
- Understand what a tourist is, why they come and the facilities tourists need
- Understand the benefits of tourism and its possible benefits to young people
- Know the main tourist attractions of East Africa and locations of the main tourist areas
- Study a tourist attraction or potential tourist attraction in the local area through field work
- Guide visitors and tourists around attractions in the local area
- Know where most tourists to East Africa come from
- Understand the meaning of domestic tourism
- Represent the statistics about tourism using simple graphs
- Understand why we should preserve wildlife and the challenges facing wildlife conservation in East Africa
- Understand why tourists are particularly interested in natural scenery and wild animals
- Appreciate that tourism can bring both benefits and problems
- Understand the challenges facing tourism in East Africa
- Understand the problems which can be brought by tourism
- Appreciate the need for conserving wildlife
- Appreciate the need for ensuring political stability and security if we are to develop tourism
- Understand that, in attracting tourists, we are always competing with other countries

#### Topic 5.3: Wildlife conservation, forests, fishing and tourism in North America: (British Columbia) (14 periods)
**Competency:** Understand the relief, climate and natural vegetation of British Columbia, the traditional way of life, need to conserve the wild life and vegetation and uses for tourism.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the location, relief features and main towns of British Columbia
- Know the main natural resources of British Columbia
- Understand the climate of British Columbia and its effect on vegetation
- Understand the importance of forest vegetation
- Understand the traditional way of life of the Native Americans in this area and the effects of European occupation
- Understand the importance of fishing in British Columbia
- Appreciate the need to conserve the natural environment
- Understand the dangers of deforestation, over-fishing and pollution of water bodies
- Draw a map to locate main features of British Columbia
- Draw a climate graph and use this to describe climate of British Columbia
- Draw diagrams and flow diagrams to show stages in logging
- Draw diagrams to show fishing techniques
- Recognize features of British Columbia on photographs
- Appreciate the need to preserve the area
- Appreciate the dangers of over-use of natural resources: deforestation and over-fishing

### THEME 6: POPULATION, URBANISATION AND TRADE IN EAST AFRICA AND NORTH AMERICA

#### Topic 6.1: Population and urbanisation in East Africa (8 periods)
**Competency:** Understand and appreciate factors influencing population growth and distribution in East Africa and the effects this has, including effects on environment and urbanization.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the concepts related to population
- Know the main areas of low and high density of population in East Africa
- Know the location of the main urban centres of East Africa
- Use maps, statistics, graphs and diagrams to analyse population
- Understand the demographic transition model and how this relates to the historical growth and population structures of East Africa
- Understand the relationship between population, resources and the provision of services
- Understand the factors which have led to rapid population increase in Uganda and the rest of East Africa
- Understand the relationship between a rapidly growing population and urbanisation
- Understand Kampala city as an example of rapid urbanisation
- Understand the concept of urbanisation, the factors influencing urbanisation, and the functions of urban areas
- Understand the advantages, disadvantages and problems resulting from urbanisation
- Form opinions on the implications of urbanisation for development
- Use statistics and photographs to illustrate urbanisation and its problems
- Understand the methods which can be used to control the rate of population growth
- Form opinions on the need for, and use of population control methods
- Appreciate the social and cultural problems of slowing down population growth

#### Topic 6.2: Urbanisation in North America: New York (12 periods)
**Competency:** Know the situation and site of New York, and understand why New York became a big city and the characteristics and problems of New York as an urban area.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know where New York is situated
- Know the names of some areas in and around New York
- Understand the characteristics of New York as a city
- Understand New York City as an example of rapid urbanisation
- Understand the advantages of the site and situation of New York in relation to early European settlement of North America
- Understand the population structure of New York and use statistics and graphs to analyse it
- Understand the concept of urbanization, the factors influencing urbanization, and the functions of urban areas
- Know the layout and divisions of New York
- Understand the importance of New York in USA
- Make a comparison between New York and Kampala
- Appreciate the attraction of New York to young people in America and its influence on young people in East Africa
- Understand the advantages, disadvantages and problems resulting from urbanisation
- Use statistics and graphs to show the rapid urban development in New York
- Use statistics and photographs to illustrate urbanization and its problems
- Understand Kampala city as an example of rapid urbanisation
- Form opinions on the implications of urbanisation for development

#### Topic 6.3: Transport and communication in East Africa (8 periods)
**Competency:** Understand the major types of transport and communication in East Africa and factors influencing their development and their role in development.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the difference between transport and communication
- Use photographs to identify the different types/modes of transport
- Use maps to show the major transport routes
- Understand the factors which influence the distribution of the major transport routes in Uganda and the rest of East Africa
- Understand the role of the different types of transport in development
- Understand the advantages and disadvantages of each form of communication
- Understand the consequences of the revolution in communication caused by digital communication: mobile phones, computers and Internet, social media
- Form opinions on the importance of transport in national and regional development

#### Topic 6.4: Trade within and outside East Africa (8 periods)
**Competency:** Understand the types of trade carried out within East Africa and between East Africa and Africa and the rest of the world and the importance and difficulties of each type of trade.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Study a local market/shopping centre to identify the patterns of trade
- Know the main exports of Uganda and their contributions to intraregional and international trade
- Know the main exports and imports of East African countries
- Know the destination and origins of the main exports and imports
- Understand the importance of trade in the development of a country
- Know the types of trade including barter, visible trade, invisible trade
- Understand the meaning and importance of a favourable balance of trade and difficulties of an unfavourable balance of trade
- Understand historical problems with trade in East Africa: reliance on exporting raw materials and importing manufactured goods
- Form opinions about ways of overcoming this problem
- Understand why there is limited trade between East African countries
- Understand ways of encouraging trade between East African countries and with other African countries
- Use flow charts, statistics and maps to show trade patterns
- Appreciate the advantages of processing to add value to exports
- Appreciate the advantages of developing import substitution industries
- Appreciate the need for an economic system which helps to distribute the resources in an equitable way

## ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

### Formative Assessment
- **Observation:** Watching learners working (assessing skills)
- **Conversation:** Asking questions and talking to learners (assessing knowledge and understanding)
- **Product:** Appraising learner's work (writing, report, presentation, map, diagram, model, drawing, painting)

### Summative Assessment
- End of year examinations focusing on application of learning
- Teacher assessment contributing 20% to final School Leaving Certificate grade
- Four-point achievement scale for each topic:
  - 0: No Learning Outcome achieved
  - 1: Some LOs achieved, but not sufficient for overall achievement
  - 2: Most LOs achieved, enough for overall achievement
  - 3: All LOs achieved – achievement with ease

### Key Assessment Areas
- **Geographical Knowledge:** Understanding of physical features, climate, population, economic activities
- **Map Skills:** Reading, interpreting, and creating maps and diagrams
- **Field Investigation:** Conducting fieldwork and collecting geographical data
- **Data Analysis:** Using statistics, graphs, and charts to analyze geographical information
- **Critical Analysis:** Evaluating geographical issues and problems
- **Research Skills:** Using various sources to gather geographical information
- **Communication:** Presenting geographical information through various media
- **Problem Solving:** Addressing geographical challenges and proposing solutions
- **Values and Attitudes:** Appreciation for environmental conservation, sustainable development, and cultural diversity
