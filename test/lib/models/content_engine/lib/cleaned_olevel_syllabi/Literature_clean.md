# LITERATURE IN ENGLISH SYLLABUS

**Subject:** Literature in English  
**Level:** O-Level (Lower Secondary)  
**Years:** Senior 1-4  
**Source:** NCDC Uganda, 2019  

---

## SENIOR 1 & 2

### TIME ALLOCATION
- **Literature in English:** 2 periods per week
- **Total periods per year:** 72

---

## SENIOR 1 & 2: TERM 1-3

### Topic 1: Oral Literature (10 periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies the techniques of oral literature and persuasive speaking creatively.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Use subject specific language
- Give instructions orally and in writing
- Make reservations in different media and for different purposes
- Analyse and evaluate how speakers present points effectively through use of language and gesture
- Sustain a conversation that involves explaining and giving reasons for their views or choices
- Participate in debate, using the conventions and language of debates
- Present spoken arguments, sequencing points logically, defending views with evidence and making use of persuasive language
- Know how to interpret real life experiences so as to be able to express likes and dislikes
- Know how to ask a range of questions to extend their thinking
- Know how to write own compositions based on questions asked
- Understand persuasive techniques related to selling a product or service

**Forms of Oral Literature:**
- Lullabies
- Work songs
- Lyrics
- War songs
- Rhymes
- Chants
- Puzzles
- Proverbs
- Dirges
- Folk tales
- Sayings
- Myths
- Legends
- Parables
- Allegories
- Fairy tales
- Puns
- Tongue twisters

---

### Topic 2: Poetry (20 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands form and structure in poetry, responds to poetic techniques and thinks critically about a poem's meaning.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand basic poetic features such as stanzas, themes, rhythm and rhyme schemes
- Compare poems with a similar theme
- Think critically about what is unique about African poetry and the different ways in which poets approach the same theme
- Analyse their response to poetry with reference to a particular poem

**Forms and Aspects of Poetry:**
- Ballads
- Free verse
- Sonnets
- Blank verse
- Haiku
- Descriptive poetry
- Meaning of a poem
- Structure of a poem
- Sound features
- Message/Theme in a poem
- Speaker
- Rhymes/Rhyme scheme
- Characters in a poem
- Lyrics
- Songs

---

### Topic 3: Drama (21 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands basic dramatic terms and techniques, and can assess their application in a performance.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the themes and techniques of a range of selected plays using basic drama terminology
- Understand how drama skills and techniques are used to create characterisation, mood and atmosphere
- Analyse events in plays to deepen understanding of themes and characters, and to enrich a performance
- Write short plays (one scene) of their own, and know how to set out dialogue

**Forms and Aspects of Drama:**
- One-man plays/monologue
- Improvised drama pieces such as skits and simulations
- Soap opera/Melodrama
- Mimes
- Comedies
- Tragedies

---

### Topic 4: Prose (21 periods)
**Competency:** The learner responds to a variety of prose, demonstrating understanding and creativity with regard to literary techniques.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Use literary terms when discussing a variety of prose texts
- Form opinions about a book in a review and other formats based on specific character and plot points
- Analyse prose in order to identify themes and structures
- Understand and employ literary language effects in order to describe setting and character
- Demonstrate understanding of language effects and characterisation in prose by being able to read/act scenes dramatically
- Write a short story that reflects their best understanding of literary techniques

**Forms and Aspects of Prose:**
- Descriptive and narrative stories
- Genres: crime, thriller, drama, adventure, horror, romance, etc.
- Short prose such as short stories, micro-stories and novelettes or abridged series
- Structure
- Language effects: adjectives, imagery, metaphor, simile
- Storyline/plot
- Characterisation/Character arc
- Climax
- Resolution
- Message/themes

## SENIOR 3 & 4

### TIME ALLOCATION
- **Literature in English:** 3 periods per week
- **Total periods per year:** 216

---

## SENIOR 3 & 4: TERM 1-3

### Topic 5: Poetry (72 periods)
**Competency:** The learner gains a deeper understanding of poetic techniques and employs them in performance and their own poetic writing.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand in more depth how poetic forms and techniques are employed to create an effect on a reader, particularly rhythm and rhyme
- Analyse poems to determine how they create an impact on the reader, including characterisation and various types of language effects
- Write in the voice and style of a poet, using their techniques
- Write poems on a given theme that create a strong reader response
- Convert a poem into prose or a play, demonstrating an understanding of the poem's underlying story
- Use their understanding of how a poem works to teach the class about the effectiveness of a poem
- Co-operate in the choral performance of a poem to highlight and dramatise its impact

**Forms and Aspects of Poetry:**
- Forms: Lullabies, Free verse, Sonnets, Ballads, Narrative and descriptive poems, Epics, Songs/lyrics, Sagas
- Aspects: Title, Themes and ideas, Language, Meaning of the poem, Message, Speaker/persona and other characters in the poem, Sound patterns such as rhyme, rhythm, assonance, consonance, Feelings such as those of the persona, subject, the reader and the poet

---

### Topic 6: Drama (72 periods)
**Competency:** The learner deepens their understanding and analysis of how plays create an impact on an audience, and they can apply these techniques in writing and performance.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the decisions required in order to turn a play script into a stage performance
- Understand how a play is different from prose and poetry, and use specialist drama terminology
- Explore their own responses to a play, citing words and actions from the script
- Summarise the plot of a play succinctly and accurately
- Be able to analyse a play in order to identify its themes
- Understand the importance of non-verbal communication in the staging of a play
- Understand how character is portrayed in drama, and practice creating characters
- Form written opinions of a play based on all the techniques used
- Bring all their knowledge and understanding of dramatic themes and techniques to bear in writing and performing a three-act play

**Forms and Aspects of Drama:**
- Forms: Tragedy, Comedy, Drama
- Acts: Title, Storyline, Conflict in the play, Characters and characterisation, Themes and ideas, Moral lessons in the play, Key dramatic feature such as action, dialogue, humour, suspense, tension, resolution, Dramatic techniques such as aside and soliloquy

---

### Topic 7: Prose (72 periods)
**Competency:** The learner identifies and analyses the literary techniques used to convey setting, character, plot and theme, and apply them in their own creative writing.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Read and enjoy prose texts with comprehension and understanding, and communicate their opinions about them
- Understand the value of the oral tradition of storytelling, and hearing stories aloud
- Communicate what they have learned and enjoyed about books
- Analyse the differences between written/fictional accounts and real life
- Know how to identify the theme of a text, and how to analyse its intended effect on the reader
- Understand why a novel uses a particular structure
- Analyse how setting is used to convey atmosphere and emotion, and to apply this knowledge to their own description of a setting
- Explore the way characters are portrayed through actions, dialogue and relationships in order to understand characterization techniques
- Explore plot development to understand how conflict and tension builds to a climax
- Identify and analyse the effects of different language and dialogue techniques
- Employ all the literary techniques studied in the composition of an original short story

**Forms, Aspects and Techniques of Prose:**
- Forms: Fictional Prose (includes novels, novellas, short stories), Non-fictional Prose (includes biographies, essays, journals), Heroic Prose (includes legends, tales), Poetry Prose (poetry written in prose instead of using verse, but maintaining poetic quality), Epistolary
- Aspects/Techniques: Setting, Dialogue, Language effects: simile, metaphor, hyperbole, personification, pathetic fallacy, onomatopoeia, oxymoron, emotive language, imagery, Unreliable narrator, Stream of consciousness, Themes, Plot (twist, resolution, open-ended), Character, Symbolism

## ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

### Formative Assessment
**Assessment Methods:**
- **Observation:** Watching learners working (assessing skills and values)
- **Conversation:** Asking questions and talking to learners (assessing knowledge and understanding)
- **Product:** Appraising learner's work (writing, report, presentation, map, diagram, model, drawing, painting)

**Assessment Cycle:**
1. Establish learning outcomes
2. Collect and analyze assessment data
3. Compare and evaluate learning against expected learning outcomes
4. Use data to improve student learning
5. Make changes to plans for future teaching and learning

### Summative Assessment
- End of year examinations set by the school
- Summing up of ongoing teacher assessments made in the context of learning
- Final examination at Senior 4 focuses on learners' ability to apply learning in new situations
- Teacher assessment contributes 20% to final School Leaving Certificate grade

### Achievement Scale
- **3:** All Learning Outcomes achieved – achievement with ease
- **2:** Most Learning Outcomes achieved, enough for overall achievement
- **1:** Some Learning Outcomes achieved, but not sufficient for overall achievement
- **0:** No Learning Outcome achieved

### Key Assessment Areas
- **Knowledge:** Retention of information, understanding literary terms and concepts
- **Understanding:** Putting knowledge into framework of meaning, analyzing literary techniques
- **Skills:** Ability to perform mental operations (reading, writing, analysis, creative writing, performance)
- **Values:** Appreciation for literature, cultural heritage, critical thinking, creativity

## GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS

- **Competency Curriculum:** One in which learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations
- **Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit an individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities
- **Formative Assessment:** The process of judging a learner's performance, by interpreting responses to tasks, in order to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps
- **Generic Skill:** Skills which are deployed in all subjects, and which enhance the learning of those subjects. These skills also equip young people for work and for life
- **Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences which allows each student to feel confident, respected and safe and equipped to learn at his or her full potential
- **Learning Outcome:** A statement which specifies what the learner should know, understand, or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject
- **Sample Assessment Strategy:** A strategy which gives a learner the opportunity to show the extent to which they have achieved the Learning Outcomes. This is usually part of the normal teaching and learning process, and not something extra at the end of a topic
- **Suggested Learning Activity:** An aspect of the normal teaching and learning process that will enable a formative assessment to be made
- **K,s,v,a:** Knowledge, Skills, Values and Attitudes. These are the experiences which the learner will acquire in the course of learning this subject
