# FOREIGN LANGUAGES SYLLABUS

**Subject:** Foreign Languages (German, French, etc.)  
**Level:** O-Level (Lower Secondary)  
**Years:** Senior 1-4  
**Source:** NCDC Uganda, 2018  

---

## OVERVIEW

Foreign Language is an elective subject from Senior 1 to Senior 4. The syllabus focuses on developing communication skills, cultural understanding, and language proficiency through experiential learning and deeper understanding.

### TIME ALLOCATION
- **Senior 1 & 2:** 2 periods per week (72 periods per year)
- **Senior 3 & 4:** 3 periods per week (108 periods per year)

### RATIONALE
Learning a foreign language enables learners to:
- Communicate with people in neighboring countries and around the world
- Understand the culture and traditions of other countries
- Appreciate the richness of cultural diversity
- Develop global citizenship and expand job opportunities
- Build critical thinking through language comparison and analysis

## SENIOR 1: TERM 1

### Theme 1: Introduction and Acquaintances

#### Topic 1.1: Welcome and Introductions (18 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands cultural differences and exchanges information orally and in writing in relation to own country and community and those where the target language is spoken.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know where in the world the target language is spoken
- Locate principal cities and countries
- Know and use simple phrases for conducting classroom business
- Know words and phrases for greetings, introductions, farewells, and polite requests
- Greet people using correct conventions
- Introduce self, stating name, town, and country
- Understand simple prepositions of place
- Recognize letters and sounds of the alphabet
- Understand when to use capital letters
- Recognize numbers 1-31, days of the week, and months
- Know key question words and phrases
- Ask questions using correct word order and pronunciation
- Respond to others' questions
- Understand the concept of gender and number
- Know words for family members and friends
- Use possessive adjectives
- Use the verb "to have" and "to be" for descriptions

## ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

### Formative Assessment
**Three-Way Assessment Approach:**
- **Observation:** Watching learners working (assessing skills)
- **Conversation:** Asking questions and talking to learners (assessing knowledge and understanding)
- **Product:** Appraising learner's work (writing, reports, presentations, translations, diagrams, models, drawings)

**Continuous Assessment:**
- Daily classroom-based assessment
- Focus on learning process and progress
- Integral part of teaching-learning process
- Teacher uses evidence to guide next learning steps

### Summative Assessment
- **End of year examinations** (80% of final grade)
- **Teacher assessment** (20% of final grade)
- Focus on application of learning in new situations rather than recall

### Achievement Scale
- **3:** All Learning Outcomes achieved – achievement with ease
- **2:** Most Learning Outcomes achieved, enough for overall achievement
- **1:** Some Learning Outcomes achieved, but not sufficient for overall achievement
- **0:** No Learning Outcome achieved

### Key Assessment Areas
- **Knowledge:** Vocabulary retention, grammatical concepts, cultural understanding
- **Understanding:** Application of language rules, comprehension of texts, cultural appreciation
- **Skills:** Speaking, listening, reading, writing, communication, critical thinking
- **Values:** Cultural appreciation, respect, global citizenship, social responsibility

## LANGUAGE SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS

### German Grammar and Vocabulary
**Grammar Areas:**
- Case system (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive)
- Tenses (present, perfect, imperfect, future, pluperfect)
- Verb conjugations (regular, irregular, modal, reflexive)
- Adjective endings and agreements
- Prepositions (fixed case, dual case)
- Clause structures (main, subordinate, relative)

**Vocabulary Categories:**
- Common verbs, adjectives, adverbs
- Numbers, colors, time expressions
- Countries, nationalities, geographical features
- Social conventions and dialogue language

### French Grammar and Vocabulary
**Grammar Areas:**
- Noun gender and plural forms
- Articles (definite, indefinite, partitive)
- Tenses (present, perfect, imperfect, future, conditional, subjunctive)
- Adjective agreement and position
- Prepositions and compound prepositions
- Pronouns (personal, reflexive, relative, object)
- Conjunctions (coordinating, subordinating)

**Vocabulary Categories:**
- High-frequency language
- Common verbs, adjectives, adverbs
- Numbers, colors, time expressions
- Countries, nationalities, French regions
- Social conventions and dialogue language
