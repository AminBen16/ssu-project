# AGRICULTURE SYLLABUS

**Subject:** Agriculture  
**Level:** O-Level (Lower Secondary)  
**Duration:** Senior 1-4 (4 years)  
**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre, Uganda, 2019  

---

## SUBJECT OVERVIEW

### Rationale
Agriculture is an elective subject that contributes to the development of critical thinking, creativity, invention, innovation, and hands-on performance. It offers high potential to transform traditional agricultural practices and supports fast-track entrepreneurial development.

### Time Allocation
- **Senior 1 & 2:** 2 periods per week
- **Senior 3 & 4:** 4 periods per week

### Key Learning Outcomes
The Agriculture syllabus aims to develop:
- Land management and food production skills
- Ability to produce competitive products for profit
- Understanding of modern production methods
- Knowledge of sustainable development
- Adaptability and flexibility for changing environments

## PROGRAMME STRUCTURE

### SENIOR 1
#### Term 1: Introduction to Agriculture (24 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the sector and opportunities in agriculture for making a living in Uganda.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand the historical background of agriculture in terms of animal herding, nomadic way of life, food gathering, hunting, and nomadism
- Understand the value of agriculture to human beings and society, and the importance of the farm as a production unit
- Understand the value of various farming systems and their socioeconomic impact in Uganda
- Understand the importance of keeping records in agriculture
- Understand the requirements of a career in agriculture and key principles of the Labour Act on living conditions of farmworkers

#### Term 2: Farm Tools, Equipment and Implements (24 periods)
**Competency:** The learner uses measurement tools, farm tools, equipment, and implements properly and safely in agricultural activities.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Identify tools used on the farm, including garden tools, wood working tools, metal tools, and basic tools used for fencing, mechanics and other farming activities
- Demonstrate skills of using farm tools and implements correctly for better production
- Show skill in using common measurement tools for length, volume, time and mass/weight
- Show skills in handling conversion of agricultural measurements into SI units
- Demonstrate basic occupational safety and health standards in agriculture
- Show skills in applying the steps in giving first aid on the farm and during agricultural activities

#### Term 3: Soil Science (12 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands how soil is formed from rocks through the process of weathering.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Show skills in analyzing soil and identifying its different components
- Understand various types of weathering processes and factors influencing soil formation
- Show skills in distinguishing between different soil particles, soil textures, and soil structures, as well as soil profile horizons and types of soils
- Show skills in soil sampling
- Understand the importance of plant nutrients and soil pH
- Demonstrate soil improvement practices and understand their effects on plant growth

#### Term 3: Vegetable Growing (12 periods)
**Competency:** The learner selects a suitable vegetable for a locality and carries out all the processes required to grow and market the vegetable.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know a range of vegetables grown locally and understand their value in both nutritional and financial terms
- Understand how to select an appropriate vegetable for growing locally
- Establish a nursery and apply good practice for vegetable growing
- Show skills in preparing bio pesticides and plant derivatives
- Handle vegetables during and after harvest to maintain quality
- Market vegetables effectively
- Follow food safety guidelines for harvested fresh vegetables

### SENIOR 2
#### Term 1: Cereal Growing (12 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the basic parts of a plant and their functions; appreciates the growth requirements of crop plants, and grows cereals successfully.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Explain the ways in which crops are important to humans
- Understand the structures and functions of the basic parts of a plant
- Understand the structures and functions of the parts of the reproductive system of a plant
- Understand the factors that influence plant growth and crop production
- Show skills in classifying crops as annual, biennial and perennial
- Understand the value of cereals both in nutritional terms and as a commodity
- Understand how to select appropriate planting material for the cereal chosen for growing
- Show skills in growing cereals
- Show skill in maintaining soil fertility in cereals
- Show skills in handling cereals during and after harvest
- Understand the various methods for storing cereals

#### Term 1: Root and Stem Tuber Growing OR Legume and Oil Seed Growing (12 periods)
**Root and Stem Tuber Growing Competency:** The learner grows and processes root and stem tubers for the market.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand the meanings and the value of roots and stem tubers, both in terms of nutrition and as a commodity
- Understand how to select appropriate planting material for the crop chosen for growing
- Show skills in growing roots/stem tubers
- Show skill in handling roots/stem tubers during and after harvest
- Show skill in processing and solar drying of roots/stem tubers

**Legume and Oil Seed Growing Competency:** The learner grows and stores legumes and oil seeds for the market.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know the structure of a seed and understand the functions of each part
- Show skills in breaking seed dormancy in crops and optimizing germination
- Understand the value of legumes and oil seeds in both nutritional and financial terms
- Understand how to select appropriate seeds for growing a chosen crop
- Show skills in growing legumes and oil seeds
- Show skill in handling legumes and oil seeds during and after harvest
- Know, understand, and apply the skills involved in the various methods of storing legumes and oil seeds

#### Term 2: Domestic Animal Rearing (24 periods)
**Choose one of the following:** Poultry rearing, Fish and aquaponics farming, Rabbit rearing, Goat rearing

**Competency:** The learner rears and markets a domestic animal profitably.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Identify different types of farm/farmed animals and their purpose
- Understand the value of rearing of domestic animals both in terms of their contribution to the human diet and as a commodity
- Understand how to select an animal to rear
- Understand the basics of caring for domestic animals, including the nutrient and feed requirements and provision of water
- Show skills needed to keep farm animals in a healthy state
- Understand the types of housing for different animals and the reasons for the designs, and show skills in constructing an animal house
- Understand the digestive and reproductive system of ruminants and non-ruminant animals
- Understand the significance of animal feeding programs
- Know how to conserve and use feeding resources
- Understand how to prepare fodder grass and crops for feeding animals
- Understand the importance of planned animal breeding and selection

#### Term 3: Perennial Crop Production (24 periods)
**Choose one of the following:** Beverage Production (coffee/tea/cocoa), Fruits crop Production, Agroforestry/water shade crops/permaculture Production

**Competency:** The learner produces and markets perennial crops profitably.

**Beverage Production Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know the value of coffee/tea/cocoa to the Ugandan economy and the key markets for these products
- Understand the general geographical areas in which coffee/tea/cocoa is grown in Uganda and the soil and climatic requirements for propagating and growing
- Understand how to establish and manage a coffee/tea/cocoa nursery and coffee garden
- Show skills in applying financial management principles to coffee/tea/cocoa production
- Show skills in the preparation of the soil for growing coffee/tea/cocoa seedlings
- Show skills in transplanting coffee/tea/cocoa seedlings into the garden
- Show skills in managing nursery and coffee/tea/cocoa plants until harvesting
- Harvest and process coffee/tea/cocoa for the market
- Roast/cure, grind and serve coffee/tea/cocoa
- Understand the importance of taste, recognize, and describe the differences in taste and smell as functions in cupping specialty coffees/teas and cocoa
- Show entrepreneurial skills in coffee/tea/cocoa production

**Fruit Crop Production Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know the varieties and value of fruit crops grown in Uganda
- Understand how to select an appropriate fruit crop for growing
- Show skills in the methods of planting fruit trees
- Understand the soil and climatic requirements for propagating a selected fruit tree
- Propagate fruit tree crops
- Show skills in the preparation of the soil for growing selected fruit trees
- Show skills needed in the planting of seeds and seedlings/cuttings
- Understand and apply the principles of good management of fruit trees
- Carry out basic fruit tree nursery tending operations
- Show skills in applying financial management principles to fruit production
- Establish and harvest fruit tree crops in the field
- Perform vegetative propagation in fruit trees (grafting, budding, pollarding and pruning)
- Exercise disease and pest control
- Know how to market fruit products
- Show entrepreneurial skills in fruit growing

**Agroforestry/Watershed/Permaculture Production Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know the meaning of the terms: agroforestry, watershed, and permaculture. Understand their importance to the environment and value to the Ugandan economy
- Understand the rationale for eco-friendly farming techniques in managing and caring for the environment and natural resources
- Show appropriate skills in agroforestry, watershed, and permaculture development
- Design an agroforestry, a watershed and a permaculture model plan for the area
- Understand how to select appropriate tree species and crops for an agroforestry garden, a watershed development area, and a permaculture garden
- Understand how to establish and manage an agroforestry garden, a watershed development area, and a permaculture garden
- Show skills in applying financial management principles to planning an agroforestry garden, a watershed development area, and a permaculture garden
- Know how to harvest and process the products of agroforestry, watershed, and permaculture
- Know how to market agroforestry, watershed, and permaculture
- Show entrepreneurial skills required to make a success of agroforestry, watershed, and permaculture

### SENIOR 3
#### Term 1: Cattle Production (48 periods)
**Competency:** The learner selects desirable cattle for production and breeding and carries out all processes involved in their rearing.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Distinguish different breeds of cattle and describe their characteristics
- Understand the physical characteristics of cattle with potential for rearing profitably, and show skills in selecting the best breed for rearing
- Understand the internal organs and outline their functions, particularly the digestive system and related physiological processes
- Understand female and male reproductive systems
- Understand and outline the different breeding and selection systems used in cattle
- Apply artificial insemination in rearing of cattle
- Understand the significance of good nutrition in rearing of cattle, and be able to prepare a balanced ration and various feeds required by dairy cattle at different stages from locally available foodstuff
- Make efficient use of high-quality roughage in rearing of cattle
- Distinguish between and evaluate different cattle management systems
- Show skills in prevention and control of diseases and parasites affecting cattle
- Detect milk abnormalities using a strip cup
- Keep records of breeding, management, and production
- Carry out identification, dehorning, and castration in cattle
- Understand the importance of milking techniques and clean milk/meat production
- Show skills in handling cattle products and bi-products

#### Term 2: Livestock Feed Making (24 periods)
**Competency:** The learner makes animal feeds based on nutritional requirements, using feed ingredients available for maximum nutrition and optimal economic production.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know the range and types of livestock feeds
- Know the ingredients and understand the technique for making livestock feeds according to specific animal requirements
- Know how to prepare and process a single material feed for livestock
- Prepare air-dried livestock compound feeds
- Make dry season livestock feeds
- Blend prepared livestock feeds
- Know how to make mineral supplements

#### Term 2: Pasture Management and Conservation OR Hydroponic Farming (24 periods)
**Pasture Management and Conservation Competency:** The learner identifies, establishes, manages, and conserves nutritive pasture.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Identify and describe different types of pasture
- Evaluate the quality of natural pasture land and improve it
- Select appropriate plant species to improve the botanical composition of a pasture land
- Select appropriate planting material for growing pastures
- Show skills in managing and conserving pastures
- Show creativity in using available waste resources to grow irrigated crops
- Understand the causes of soil erosion, how soil can be conserved, and apply learning in practice
- Understand and apply learning in relation to the importance of the conservation of soil moisture

**Hydroponic Farming Competency:** The learner identifies, establishes, manages, and produces crops using hydroponic systems.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know the meaning and understand the principles of hydroponics
- Distinguish between and explain open and closed hydroponic farming systems
- Understand how to select an appropriate hydroponic system and media to suit the requirements of different plants
- Understand and apply the practical steps involved in growing crops in the hydroponic farming system
- Budget and estimate the construction costs of a selected hydroponic farming system
- Know how to apply agrochemicals in the hydroponic garden for increased production
- Understand how to market crops produced in hydroponic farming system

#### Term 3: Financial Services and Money in Agriculture (24 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the importance of prudent financial management and uses knowledge and skills to manage financial resources effectively.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand the difference between needs and wants
- Explain the five core pillars of financial literacy
- Know the difference between investment and saving
- Understand the importance of the saving culture
- Know the different types of banks and bank accounts
- Demonstrate the ability to open a bank account
- Show skills in budgeting using spread-sheets/Excel, or books and calculators

#### Term 3: Farm Buildings and Farm Structures (24 periods)
**Competency:** The learner establishes a cost effective and functional farm building and farm structure.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Show skills in handling building tools correctly and safely
- Understand and perform the processes of concrete mixing, mortar mixing, brick making, plastering and painting of farm structures and farm building
- Know appropriate types of housing for different animals
- Budget and procure materials for establishing farm buildings and farm structures
- Show skills in constructing farm building and structures
- Know how to use locally available materials for roofing

### SENIOR 4
#### Term 1: Value Addition to Domestic Milk Products (12 periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates hygienic milk handling, processing and milk safety.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand and be able to carry out pasteurization of milk
- Understand and be able to carry out homogenization of milk
- Demonstrate skills in processing fermented and other milk products
- Process flavored milk and sweetened condensed milk
- Handle, collect, test, and quality control milk

#### Term 1: Value Addition to Domestic Meat Products (12 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands and follows safe and hygienic procedures when handling and processing meat.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know the different sources of meat for processing and the meat products made
- Select, handle, and process meat safely and hygienically
- Demonstrate basic practical skills such as the use of relevant tools in meat processing
- Show skill in meat processing of different animals
- Know how to smoke meat
- Market processed meat

#### Term 2: Biotechnology and Bio safety in Agriculture (24 periods)
**Competency:** The learner should be able to appreciate the emerging trends in agricultural production and their implications.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know the meaning of biotechnology, bioengineering and bio safety
- Understand how genetic engineering is carried out in crops and animals
- Understand and put the case for the use and development of biotechnology in agricultural production
- Research and understand emerging trends in biotechnology, bioengineering and biosafety

#### Term 2: Land Tenure System (24 periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates land reforms in Uganda.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know and understand the land tenure system, tenure types and land legislation of Uganda
- Obtain information from land tenure documents and maps
- Distinguish between land reform and land registration
- Promote land reforms

#### Term 2: Cooperatives and Self-help Groups in Agriculture (24 periods)
**Competency:** The learner organizes and participates in farmers' organisations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know the different types of farmers' organisations in Uganda and understand their purposes
- Explain the principles that govern cooperatives and self-help groups
- Understand how to participate in a meeting following parliamentary procedures

#### Term 3: Value Addition to Agro Wastes and By-products (24 periods)
**Competency:** The learner manufactures biofuels and handmade paper to preserve the environment.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know materials used in making paper, biogas and briquettes
- Show creativity and skills in utilising domestic animal wastes
- Make uncarbonized and carbonized briquettes from crop and animal domestic waste
- Understand how handmade paper is manufactured

#### Term 3: Value Addition to Vegetables OR Value Addition to Fruits (24 periods)
**Value Addition to Vegetables Competency:** The learner processes vegetables for sale.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know how to find information on vegetable processing
- Understand the principles of and show skills in using different methods of drying vegetables
- Understand the principles and demonstrate methods of preserving vegetables with acids, salt, and sugar
- Market processed vegetable products

**Value Addition to Fruits Competency:** The learner processes and markets quality fruit products.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Show skills in planning and budgeting (using Excel/spreadsheets or books and calculators) for a recipe, and purchasing
- Know and understand the requirements regarding quality of water to use in processing fruit, and apply basic methods of water treatment
- Plan and prepare processing premises, taking account of the importance of premises hygiene
- Know the different types of fruit beverages and their waste recycling specifications
- Show skills in juice and pulp extraction, processing and preservation, taking account of good manufacturing practices
- Understand the principles and demonstrate methods of preserving fruit with acids, salt, and sugar
- Know how to advertise and market processed fruits

## ASSESSMENT GUIDELINES

### Assessment Approach
The new curriculum emphasizes formative assessment through:
- **Observation** – watching learners working (good for assessing skills and values)
- **Conversation** – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and understanding)
- **Product** – appraising the learner's work (writing, report, presentation, map, diagram, model, drawing, painting etc.)

### Learning Outcome Categories
Each Learning Outcome is categorized as:
- **Knowledge (k)** – The retention of information
- **Understanding (u)** – Putting knowledge into a framework of meaning
- **Skills (s)** – The ability to perform a physical or mental act or operation
- **Values/Attitudes (v/a)** – Behaviours or actions that form character; emotions, beliefs or behaviors toward particular objects

### Assessment Descriptors
For overall unit assessment:
- **0** – No Learning Outcome achieved
- **1** – Some LOs achieved, but not sufficient for overall achievement
- **2** – Most LOs achieved, enough for overall achievement
- **3** – All LOs achieved – achievement with ease

## GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS

- **Competency Curriculum** – One in which learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations
- **Differentiation** – The design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit an individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities
- **Formative Assessment** – The process of judging a learner's performance, by interpreting the responses to tasks, in order to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps
- **Generic skill** – Skills which are deployed in all subjects, and which enhance the learning of those subjects. These skills also equip young people for work and for life
- **Inclusion** – An approach to planning learning experiences which allows each student to feel confident, respected, safe, and equipped to learn at his or her full potential
- **Learning Outcome** – A statement which specifies what the learner should know, understand, or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject
- **Process Skill** – A capability acquired by following the programme of study in a particular Learning Area; enables a learner to apply the knowledge and understanding of the Learning Area
- **Sample Assessment Strategy** – An activity which gives a learner the opportunity to show the extent to which s/he has achieved the Learning Outcomes
- **Suggested Learning Activity** – An aspect of the normal teaching and learning process that will enable formative assessment to be made
