# CHEMISTRY SYLLABUS

**Subject:** Chemistry  
**Level:** O-Level (Lower Secondary)  
**Duration:** Senior 1-4 (4 years)  
**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre, Uganda, 2019  

---

## SUBJECT OVERVIEW

### Rationale
Chemistry is concerned with the physical and chemical properties of substances and the interaction of energy and matter. The study of Chemistry involves investigation into chemical reactions and processes, seeking to explain and predict events at the atomic and molecular level.

This syllabus aims to:
- Appreciate and understand natural phenomena and how materials behave
- Be aware of the power, impact, and influence of Chemistry in the modern scientific world
- Appreciate, understand, and use methods of science
- See the relevance of Chemistry to everyday life
- Appreciate and understand the role of Chemistry in Uganda and elsewhere
- Understand basic chemical concepts to provide adequate foundation for specialization
- Develop spirit of inquiry and continue searching for new ways materials may be used in service of mankind
- Appreciate inter-relationships among Chemistry, Biology, Physics, Mathematics and other subjects
- Make use of chemical data, concepts, principles, and terminology in communicating chemical information
- Develop ability to work independently and collaboratively
- Appreciate significance and limitations of science in social and economic development
- Integrate ICT tools and skills into teaching and learning of chemical concepts

### Time Allocation
- **Senior 1 & 2:** 3 periods per week
- **Senior 3 & 4:** 4 periods per week

### Key Learning Outcomes
The Chemistry syllabus aims to develop:
- Critical thinking, creativity, invention, and innovation
- Ability to use evidence to evaluate how Science can be applied in everyday life
- Understanding of Chemistry within different topics across four years
- Foundation for knowledgeable, skilled, and responsive workforce crucial for national development
- Skills that will be of long-term value in increasingly technological world

## PROGRAMME STRUCTURE

### SENIOR 1 (108 periods)

#### Term 1 (36 periods)
**Theme: Introduction to Chemistry and Experimental Techniques**

**Chemistry and Society (6 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner assesses the application of chemistry in everyday life and its contribution to the economy.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know appropriate activities to explain the discrete nature of chemistry
- Understand why chemistry is studied and how it overlaps with other subjects such as biology, physics, mathematics, and geology
- Understand the importance of chemistry and relate knowledge of chemistry to relevant careers
- Know the contribution of chemistry to the Ugandan economy

**Experimental Chemistry (12 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner understands that chemistry is a process of evidence-based enquiry involving collection of evidence about the natural world, identification of trends and patterns, and development of theories.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know laboratory rules and regulations and understand importance of risk assessment for safe work
- Know and use laboratory equipment appropriately for measuring time, temperature, mass, and volume
- Understand the scientific method to carry out investigations and importance of risk assessment
- Know how to purify mixtures, including chromatography
- Know how to identify substances and their purity using melting and boiling points

**States and Changes of States of Matter (9 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner uses knowledge of arrangement and motion of particles to explain properties of solids, liquids, and gases.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand that matter occupies space and has mass and can exist in solid, liquid, gas, and plasma form
- Understand that solids, liquids, and gases have different properties including shape, pouring, and compressing
- Know the kinetic theory of matter and use it to explain particle arrangement, inter-particle forces, movement and properties
- Understand that change from one state to another involves heat gain or heat loss
- Appreciate cooling effect of evaporation and how it contributes to maintaining constant body temperature

**Using Materials (9 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner explores how materials are used and relates these uses to their molecular structures.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know that materials used in everyday life can be classified into natural and synthetic groups
- Understand how physical properties of polymers determine uses in everyday life
- Know about molecular structures of materials and relate this to their use
- Know that polymers are useful long chain molecules made by both natural and synthetic processes
- Understand how physical properties of polymers determine uses in everyday life
- Know how common materials can pollute environment and which materials can be recycled

#### Term 2 (36 periods)
**Theme: Temporary and Permanent Changes to Materials**

**Temporary and Permanent Changes (16 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner recognises occurrence of temporary and permanent changes, and their importance in everyday life.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand that many substances undergo permanent changes when heated or burnt, forming new materials
- Recognise temporary (reversible) and permanent (irreversible) changes to matter under different conditions

**Mixtures, Elements and Compounds (20 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner recognises the characteristics of mixtures and compounds.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know criteria for determining whether a substance is pure or not
- Understand that substances are either elements, mixtures or compounds
- Identify different mixtures and devise ways of separating pure substances from them
- Know that when added together, some liquids mix while others form two layers

#### Term 3 (36 periods)
**Theme: Air and Environment**

**Air (10 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner appreciates that air is a mixture of gases in which oxygen is the active constituent, and can describe processes that may affect air quality.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand that air is a mixture of different gases that can be separated and used
- Understand how air pollution can affect the atmosphere
- Understand and appreciate that processes such as burning and rusting use oxygen from air to form oxides

**Water (14 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner investigates evaporation and condensation as natural processes essential for replenishing supplies of fresh water in the water cycle.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand occurrence of water as natural resource, its physical and chemical properties and importance in everyday life
- Appreciate how water is recycled by natural processes of water cycle
- Understand process of water and sewage treatment

**Earth and Space**

**Rocks and Minerals (12 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner appreciates that rocks are composed of different minerals and these determine the properties of rocks.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand how igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks are formed, that they are made up of minerals, and that some minerals are useful
- Identify physical properties of rocks and distinguish between different types
- Understand process of weathering and how weathered rock particles form basis of soil

### SENIOR 2 (108 periods)

#### Term 1 (36 periods)
**Theme: Acids and Alkalis**

**Acids and Alkalis (12 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the properties and importance of acids, alkalis/bases and salts in everyday life.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Recognise that locally available materials and substances are either acidic or alkaline
- Understand concept of pH as measure of strength of acids and alkalis
- Understand reaction between acids and alkalis

**Salts (12 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner appreciates that acids and alkalis form salts.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Be familiar with and carry out neutralisation reactions to prepare salts
- Know and appreciate uses of common salts in everyday life

**The Periodic Table (12 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner investigates the diversity of elements in the Periodic Table.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand that elements can be grouped into metals and non-metals and relate physical properties to their uses
- Know that Periodic Table is classification of elements according to atomic or proton number
- Relate arrangement of electrons in first 20 elements to their positions in Periodic Table
- Understand relationship between position of elements in groups and charge on ions they form

#### Term 2 (36 periods)
**Theme: The Periodic Table**

**Carbon in the Environment (36 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand carbon cycle and its importance
- Know different forms of carbon and their properties
- Understand role of carbon compounds in environment
- Appreciate importance of carbon in living organisms

#### Term 3 (36 periods)
**Theme: Order of Reactivity of Metals**

**The Reactivity Series (36 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand reactivity series of metals
- Know reactions of metals with water, acids, and oxygen
- Understand extraction of metals from their ores
- Relate reactivity to uses of metals

### SENIOR 3 (144 periods)

#### Term 1 (48 periods)
**Theme: Carbon in Life**

**Carbon in Life (30 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand importance of carbon compounds in living organisms
- Know structure and properties of organic compounds
- Understand role of carbon in biological processes
- Appreciate applications of organic chemistry

**Structures and Bonds (18 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand types of chemical bonding
- Know structure of atoms and electron configuration
- Understand ionic and covalent bonding
- Relate bonding to properties of substances

#### Term 2 (48 periods)
**Theme: Using Equations in Chemistry**

**Formulae, Stoichiometry and Mole Concept (30 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand chemical formulae and equations
- Know mole concept and its applications
- Perform stoichiometric calculations
- Understand quantitative relationships in chemical reactions

**Structures of Substances (18 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand properties and structures of different substances
- Relate structure to physical and chemical properties
- Know types of crystalline structures
- Understand bonding in different substances

#### Term 3 (48 periods)
**Theme: Fuels and Energy**

**Fossil Fuels (20 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know types of fossil fuels and their formation
- Understand combustion of fossil fuels
- Know environmental impact of fossil fuels
- Appreciate renewable energy sources

**Chemical Reactions (28 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand types of chemical reactions
- Know factors affecting rate of reactions
- Understand energy changes in reactions
- Apply knowledge of chemical equations

### SENIOR 4 (128 periods)

#### Term 1 (48 periods)
**Theme: REDOX Reactions**

**Oxidation and Reduction Reactions (18 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand oxidation and reduction processes
- Identify oxidising and reducing agents
- Write and balance redox equations
- Apply redox concepts to real-life situations

**Industrial Processes (30 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand important industrial chemical processes
- Know applications of chemistry in industry
- Understand economic importance of chemical industries
- Appreciate environmental considerations in industrial processes

#### Term 2 (48 periods)
**Theme: Periodicity**

**Trends in the Periodic Table (20 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand periodic trends in properties of elements
- Know variations in atomic radius, ionization energy, electronegativity
- Relate trends to position in periodic table
- Predict properties of elements based on trends

**Energy Changes during Chemical Reactions (28 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand endothermic and exothermic reactions
- Know enthalpy changes and their measurement
- Understand energy profile diagrams
- Apply knowledge of energy changes to chemical processes

#### Term 3 (32 periods)
**Theme: Consumable Chemicals**

**Chemical for Consumers (20 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand chemicals used in everyday life
- Know properties and uses of consumer chemicals
- Understand safety considerations in using chemicals
- Appreciate role of chemistry in consumer products

**Nuclear Processes (12 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand basic concepts of nuclear chemistry
- Know types of nuclear reactions
- Understand applications and risks of nuclear processes
- Appreciate nuclear energy and its implications

## ASSESSMENT GUIDELINES

### Assessment Approach
The new curriculum emphasizes formative assessment through:
- **Observation** – watching learners working (good for assessing skills and values)
- **Conversation** – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and understanding)
- **Product** – appraising learner's work (writing, report, presentation, map, diagram, model, drawing, painting etc.)

### Learning Outcome Categories
Each Learning Outcome is categorized as:
- **Knowledge (k)** – The retention of information
- **Understanding (u)** – Putting knowledge into a framework of meaning
- **Skills (s)** – The ability to perform a physical or mental act or operation
- **Values/Attitudes (v/a)** – Behaviours or actions that form character; emotions, beliefs or behaviors toward particular objects

### Assessment Descriptors
For overall unit assessment:
- **0** – No Learning Outcome achieved
- **1** – Some LOs achieved, but not sufficient for overall achievement
- **2** – Most LOs achieved, enough for overall achievement
- **3** – All LOs achieved – achievement with ease

### Formative Assessment Cycle
1. **Establish learning outcomes** (from syllabus)
2. **Develop assessment opportunities** (from suggested learning activities)
3. **Collect and analyze assessment data** (through observation, conversation, product)
4. **Evaluate learning against expected outcomes**
5. **Use data to improve student learning** and plan future teaching

## GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS

- **Competency Curriculum** – Curriculum where learners develop ability to apply learning with confidence in various situations
- **Differentiation** – Design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities
- **Formative Assessment** – Process of judging learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps
- **Generic skill** – Skills deployed in all subjects that enhance learning and equip young people for work and life
- **Inclusion** – Approach to planning learning experiences that allows each student to feel confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn at full potential
- **Learning Outcome** – Statement specifying what learner should know, understand, or be able to do within particular aspect of a subject
- **Process Skill** – Capability acquired by following programme of study that enables learner to apply knowledge and understanding of subject
- **Sample Assessment Strategy** – Activity giving learner opportunity to show extent of achieved Learning Outcomes, usually part of normal teaching and learning process
- **Suggested Learning Activity** – Aspect of normal teaching and learning process that enables formative assessment to be made
