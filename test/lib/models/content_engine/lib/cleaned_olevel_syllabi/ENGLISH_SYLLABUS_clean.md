# ENGLISH SYLLABUS

**Subject:** English Language  
**Level:** O-Level (Lower Secondary)  
**Duration:** Senior 1-4 (4 years)  
**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre, Uganda, 2019  

---

## SUBJECT OVERVIEW

### Rationale
Language and literacy are of personal, social, and economic importance. Our ability to use language lies at the centre of the development and expression of our emotions, thoughts, learning, and sense of personal identity. Language is a key aspect of culture.

Literacy is fundamental to all areas of learning, as it unlocks access to the wider curriculum and increases opportunities for individuals in all aspects of life, including laying the foundation for lifelong learning and work, and contributing strongly to the development of all human capacities.

The ability to communicate is fundamental to life and to learning. Facility in speaking, listening, reading, and writing enables learners to express themselves creatively and imaginatively and to communicate with others effectively. It enables learners to become enthusiastic and critical readers of stories, poetry, and drama as well as non-fiction.

### Time Allocation
- **Senior 1 & 2:** 6 periods per week
- **Senior 3 & 4:** 5 periods per week

### Key Learning Outcomes
The English syllabus aims to develop:
- Critical thinking, interaction, and production of language
- Intelligibility, self-expression, and creativity
- Personal, interpersonal, and teamwork skills applicable in everyday life and the world of work
- Competence in listening, speaking, reading, and writing
- Ability to communicate effectively in diverse contexts
- Enthusiastic and critical reading of various text types
- Creative and imaginative writing skills

## PROGRAMME STRUCTURE

### ENGLISH PROCESS SKILLS

#### Listening Skills
**Senior 1:**
- Listening for gist: Identify general ideas of short informative/descriptive texts
- Listening for specific information: Respond to basic information about self, family, and local environment
- Listening for pleasure: Repeat tongue twisters, jokes, proverbs, and riddles

**Senior 2:**
- Listening for gist: State main points of face-to-face/broadcast conversations
- Listening for specific information: Answer questions accurately, identify fact and point of view
- Listening for pleasure: Appreciate reading performances and explain enjoyment

**Senior 3:**
- Listening for gist: Identify main ideas of short explanations using familiar vocabulary
- Listening for specific information: Summarize spoken text, take guided notes
- Listening for pleasure: Keep listening log of programs listened to for pleasure

**Senior 4:**
- Listening for gist: Identify main ideas of argumentative/discursive/descriptive texts
- Listening for specific information: Take notes of specific details in lectures, complete graphs and tables
- Listening for pleasure: Maintain listening log with critical comments

#### Speaking Skills
**Senior 1:**
- Articulate speech sounds accurately to speak clearly
- Manipulate speech sounds appropriately to speak audibly
- Make personal efforts to improve speaking skills
- Talk briefly about self, family, or familiar subjects in social settings

**Senior 2:**
- Use appropriate body language to enrich reading aloud
- Pronounce high-frequency words correctly
- Read aloud single words based on familiar themes
- Follow social conventions of courtesy in speech

**Senior 3:**
- Use non-verbal means to clarify expression
- Read aloud text on familiar themes demonstrating mastery of stress, rhythm, and intonation
- Correct own mistakes and speak with minimal errors
- Express self fluently and comprehensively in everyday situations

**Senior 4:**
- Interpret non-verbal language of audience and respond appropriately
- Express self fluently and comprehensively in everyday communication situations
- Speak with good control of grammatical structures with only occasional errors
- Speak confidently, correctly, and politely as required by the speech situation

#### Reading Skills
**Senior 1:**
- Read short simple texts for enjoyment and information
- Identify main ideas in familiar texts
- Use context clues to understand unfamiliar words
- Develop basic reading strategies

**Senior 2:**
- Read longer texts for information and enjoyment
- Identify specific details and supporting ideas
- Make simple inferences from texts
- Develop vocabulary through reading

**Senior 3:**
- Read complex texts for critical analysis
- Identify themes, main ideas, and supporting details
- Analyze author's purpose and point of view
- Develop advanced reading comprehension skills

**Senior 4:**
- Read sophisticated texts for literary analysis
- Evaluate arguments and evidence in texts
- Analyze literary devices and techniques
- Develop critical literacy and analytical skills

#### Writing Skills
**Senior 1:**
- Write simple sentences and short paragraphs
- Write about familiar topics and experiences
- Use basic punctuation and capitalization
- Develop basic writing organization

**Senior 2:**
- Write longer paragraphs and simple compositions
- Write for different purposes (inform, persuade, entertain)
- Use more complex sentence structures
- Develop paragraph coherence and unity

**Senior 3:**
- Write essays and compositions on various topics
- Use appropriate organizational structures
- Write for different audiences and purposes
- Develop sophisticated writing skills

**Senior 4:**
- Write complex essays and research papers
- Use advanced writing techniques and devices
- Write for academic and professional purposes
- Develop mastery of writing conventions

## ASSESSMENT GUIDELINES

### Assessment Approach
The new curriculum emphasizes formative assessment through:
- **Observation** – watching learners working (good for assessing skills and values)
- **Conversation** – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and understanding)
- **Product** – appraising learner's work (writing, report, presentation, map, diagram, model, drawing, painting etc.)

### Learning Outcome Categories
Each Learning Outcome is categorized as:
- **Knowledge (k)** – The retention of information
- **Understanding (u)** – Putting knowledge into a framework of meaning
- **Skills (s)** – The ability to perform a physical or mental act or operation
- **Values/Attitudes (v/a)** – Behaviours or actions that form character; emotions, beliefs or behaviors toward particular objects

### Assessment Descriptors
For overall unit assessment:
- **0** – No Learning Outcome achieved
- **1** – Some LOs achieved, but not sufficient for overall achievement
- **2** – Most LOs achieved, enough for overall achievement
- **3** – All LOs achieved – achievement with ease

### Formative Assessment Cycle
1. **Establish learning outcomes** (from syllabus)
2. **Develop assessment opportunities** (from suggested learning activities)
3. **Collect and analyze assessment data** (through observation, conversation, product)
4. **Evaluate learning against expected outcomes**
5. **Use data to improve student learning** and plan future teaching

## GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS

- **Competency Curriculum** – Curriculum where learners develop ability to apply learning with confidence in various situations
- **Differentiation** – Design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities
- **Formative Assessment** – Process of judging learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps
- **Generic skill** – Skills deployed in all subjects that enhance learning and equip young people for work and life
- **Inclusion** – Approach to planning learning experiences that allows each student to feel confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn at full potential
- **Learning Outcome** – Statement specifying what learner should know, understand, or be able to do within particular aspect of a subject
- **Process Skill** – Capability acquired by following programme of study that enables learner to apply knowledge and understanding of subject
- **Sample Assessment Strategy** – Activity giving learner opportunity to show extent of achieved Learning Outcomes, usually part of normal teaching and learning process
- **Suggested Learning Activity** – Aspect of normal teaching and learning process that enables formative assessment to be made
