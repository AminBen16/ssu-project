# BIOLOGY SYLLABUS

**Subject:** Biology  
**Level:** O-Level (Lower Secondary)  
**Duration:** Senior 1-4 (4 years)  
**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre, Uganda, 2019  

---

## SUBJECT OVERVIEW

### Rationale
Biology is a four-year programme of study that builds upon concepts, skills, attitudes, and values developed at primary school level. The syllabus provides a firm foundation for further learning of the subject at the secondary level and meets specific needs of higher-achieving learners who will go on to study Biology at a higher level.

Learning Biology enables learners to:
- Apply and use principles of the scientific method/process and application of experimental techniques to solve specific problems
- Write, communicate and report on biological concepts
- Apply biological concepts to better understand other fields of science to accomplish day-to-day tasks
- Understand, address and successfully manage health, environmental, and sustainability challenges facing society

### Time Allocation
- **Senior 1 & 2:** 3 periods per week
- **Senior 3 & 4:** 4 periods per week

### Key Learning Outcomes
The Biology syllabus aims to develop:
- Critical thinking, creativity, invention, and innovation
- Ability to use evidence to evaluate how Science can be applied in everyday life
- Independent thinking, science process skills, and scientific attitude
- Understanding of Biology within different topics across the four-year study
- Foundation for knowledgeable, skilled, and responsive workforce crucial for national development

## PROGRAMME STRUCTURE

### SENIOR 1 (108 periods)

#### Term 1 (36 periods)
**Theme: Diversity of Living Things**

**Introduction to Biology (8 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner understands biology as a study of life and that all living organisms experience common life processes.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Appreciate that biology is the study of life
- Appreciate that biology is applied in everyday life
- Understand that life processes are common to all living things, but they are manifested differently in different organisms

**Cells (20 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the cell as the basic unit of living organisms including how structures of different specialized cells are related to their functions.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Know that the basic unit of living organisms is a cell
- Know and understand the structure and functions of a typical animal cell and plant cell
- Understand the structure of specialized cells in terms of their functions in an organism
- Understand levels of organization in organisms (cell, tissue, organ, system, organism)

**Classification (8 periods)**
**Competency:** The learner understands that classification is the sorting out of living things based on their similarities.

**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand the concept of classification of organisms at different levels
- Understand that each organism has a Latin name consisting of the genus and species

#### Term 2 (56 periods)
**Theme: Diversity of Living Things**

**Classification (36 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand the major groups of living organisms and their characteristics
- Understand the principles of classification
- Use classification keys to identify organisms

**Insects (20 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand the characteristics of insects
- Understand the life cycle of insects
- Understand the economic importance of insects

#### Term 3 (16 periods)
**Theme: Diversity of Living Things**

**Flowering Plants (16 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand the structure and functions of flowering plants
- Understand the process of photosynthesis
- Understand the reproduction in flowering plants

### SENIOR 2 (108 periods)

#### Term 1 (36 periods)
**Theme: Soil**

**Physical and Chemical Properties of Soil (20 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand the physical properties of soil
- Understand the chemical properties of soil

**Soil Erosion and Conservation (16 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**

- Understand the causes of soil erosion

**Transport in Animals (18 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:

**Chemical Coordination in Humans (18 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand endocrine glands and their functions
- Understand hormones and their effects

**Nervous Coordination in Humans (22 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**

- Understand the structure and function of the nervous system

**Receptor Organs in Man (8 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**

**Theme: Locomotion and Growth**

**Locomotion in Mammals (20 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:

**Growth and Development in Plants and Animals (28 periods)**

- Understand the process of growth

- Understand the structure of flowers
- Understand the process of pollination
- Understand fertilization and fruit formation

**Sexual Reproduction in Humans (22 periods)**

The learner should be able to:
- Understand the male and female reproductive systems
- Understand gametogenesis
- Understand fertilization and development

#### Term 2 (53 periods)
**Theme: Genetics and Inter-Relationships**

**Inheritance (20 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand chromosomes and genes

**Variation and Selection (13 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand continuous and discontinuous variation

**Concept of Ecology (3 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand the meaning of ecology
- Understand ecological terms

**Food Chains and Food Webs (12 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand food chains and food webs
- Understand trophic levels
- Understand energy flow in ecosystems

**Associations in Biological Communities (18 periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
The learner should be able to:
- Understand symbiosis, parasitism, and commensalism

#### Term 3 (33 periods)

**Humans and the Natural Environment (20 periods)**

## ASSESSMENT GUIDELINES

### Assessment Approach
The new curriculum emphasizes formative assessment through:
- **Observation** – watching learners working (good for assessing skills and values)
- **Conversation** – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and understanding)
- **Product** – appraising learner's work (writing, report, presentation, map, diagram, model, drawing, painting etc.)

### Learning Outcome Categories
Each Learning Outcome is categorized as:
- **Knowledge (k)** – The retention of information
- **Understanding (u)** – Putting knowledge into a framework of meaning
- **Skills (s)** – The ability to perform a physical or mental act or operation
- **Values/Attitudes (v/a)** – Behaviours or actions that form character; emotions, beliefs or behaviors toward particular objects

### Assessment Descriptors
For overall unit assessment:
- **0** – No Learning Outcome achieved
- **1** – Some LOs achieved, but not sufficient for overall achievement
- **2** – Most LOs achieved, enough for overall achievement
- **3** – All LOs achieved – achievement with ease

### Formative Assessment Cycle
1. **Establish learning outcomes** (from syllabus)
2. **Develop assessment opportunities** (from suggested learning activities)
3. **Collect and analyze assessment data** (through observation, conversation, product)
4. **Evaluate learning against expected outcomes**
5. **Use data to improve student learning** and plan future teaching

## GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS

- **Competency Curriculum** – Curriculum where learners develop ability to apply learning with confidence in various situations
- **Differentiation** – Design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities
- **Formative Assessment** – Process of judging learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps
- **Generic skill** – Skills deployed in all subjects that enhance learning and equip young people for work and life
- **Inclusion** – Approach to planning learning experiences that allows each student to feel confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn at full potential
- **Learning Outcome** – Statement specifying what learner should know, understand, or be able to do within particular aspect of a subject
- **Process Skill** – Capability acquired by following programme of study that enables learner to apply knowledge and understanding of subject
- **Sample Assessment Strategy** – Activity giving learner opportunity to show extent of achieved Learning Outcomes, usually part of normal teaching and learning process
- **Suggested Learning Activity** – Aspect of normal teaching and learning process that enables formative assessment to be made
