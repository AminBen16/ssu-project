# Agriculture - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Agriculture  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Agriculture syllabus aims to equip learners with the necessary knowledge and skills in sustainable agriculture, including crop management, animal husbandry, and value addition to agricultural products. It focuses on developing learners' ability to contribute to the transformation of the agricultural sector and improvement of agricultural productivity. The course emphasizes:

- **Sustainable Agriculture:** Understanding and applying modern agricultural practices
- **Animal Production:** Comprehensive study of livestock management and production
- **Crop Production:** Advanced crop management and production techniques
- **Value Addition:** Processing and marketing agricultural products
- **Entrepreneurial Skills:** Business management in agricultural contexts
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Environmental stewardship, food security, and sustainable development

1. **Agricultural Development**

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 9 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across animal production, crop production, and value addition

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate through research directed by their interest
   - Solve problems through series of questions and scenarios
   - Enhances critical thinking, communication and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners actively participate in hands-on experiences during project activities
   - Learn by reflecting upon what they are doing
   - Leads to development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners find solutions to problems through their experience in research and projects
   - Develops critical thinking, social and research skills

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Learners refer to real-world scenarios to discuss and analyse them
   - Enables them to develop critical thinking, analytical and research skills

5. **Discovery Learning**
   - Learners construct their own knowledge through active participation, exploration and inquiry
   - Encourages them to critically think, ask questions and hypothesise through research

### Value Chain Focus

The syllabus focuses on two main value chains:

1. **Crop Value Chain**
   - Establishment of a crop enterprise
   - Managing the enterprise
   - Product value addition for the market

2. **Animal Value Chain**
   - Establishment of an animal enterprise
   - Managing the enterprise
   - Product value addition for the market

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: Agricultural Biology in Crops and Animals (63 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates understanding of cellular Biology principles to illustrate their role in supporting agricultural production.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explore the role of plant cells and animal cells in agriculture production
- Apply the knowledge of genetics and genes to address challenges in crop and animal production
- Demonstrate diffusion, osmosis and active transport in cells
- Predict outcomes of genetic crosses according to Mendelian theory
- Participate in project-based learning on improving yields and health of crops and animals using genetics

**Sub-topics:**
- Cells and Cell Physiology
- Biochemical compounds in Cells
- Transmission Genetics and Genetic Engineering

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 2: Anatomy, Morphology and Physiology (40 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates the relationship between anatomy and physiology of animals for increased production.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the shape, form and appearance of an animal to determine the quality of the animal
- Analyse the relationship between different animal body systems for increased production
- Apply the knowledge of morphology to select an animal to rear
- Examine digestive and reproductive systems to explain how different systems interact

**Sub-topics:**
- Animal Anatomy, Morphology and Physiology

#### Topic 3: Livestock Management and Rearing Practices (50 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies evidence-based livestock management and rearing practices to ensure optimal quality production.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Establish a farm animal enterprise by preparing a business model canvas for rearing a selected animal
- Perform relevant animal husbandry practices for a selected animal for optimal production
- Apply basic occupational health, safety and environmental management and protection practices for a selected animal
- Handle and manage health in a selected animal while observing basic occupational environmental protection practices

**Sub-topics:**
- Establishing a Farm Animal Enterprise
- Animal Husbandry and Management Practices

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

#### Topic 4: Harvesting Farm Animals and Animal Products (27 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner harvests optimal quality farm animals and animal products for the market.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate understanding of various harvesting techniques for the selected animal and animal product for the market
- Harvest farm animals and animal products in compliance with local and international regulations regarding farm animal harvesting and animal product safety
- Analyse a case study on animal welfare and product safety in farm animal harvesting and recommend improvements
- Exhibit affective and psychomotor skills including harvesting process and procedures, animal handling techniques, attention to details

**Sub-topics:**
- Preparation for Harvesting
- Harvesting of Animals and Animal Products

#### Topic 5: Value Addition to Farm Animal and Animal Products (23 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner adds value to the farm animal and to animal products for the market.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Add value to selected farm animal products using appropriate techniques
- Apply preservation methods to extend shelf life of animal products
- Develop packaging solutions for animal products
- Create marketing strategies for value-added animal products

#### Topic 6: Animal Product Marketing (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner markets animal products effectively using appropriate marketing strategies.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Develop marketing strategies for animal products
- Apply financial management principles in animal product marketing
- Analyse market trends and consumer preferences
- Create business plans for animal product marketing

**Sub-topics:**
- Marketing Animal Products
- Financial Management

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: Land Policy (18 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses land policy and its impact on agricultural production.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine different land tenure systems and their implications for agriculture
- Analyse land reforms in Uganda and their impact on agricultural development
- Evaluate land use policies and their effect on agricultural productivity
- Develop recommendations for sustainable land management

**Sub-topics:**
- Land Tenure System
- Land Reforms in Uganda

#### Topic 2: Soil Systems (32 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses soil properties and applies appropriate soil management practices.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse soil physical, chemical, and biological properties
- Evaluate different types of fertilizers and their application
- Develop soil fertility management plans
- Apply soil conservation techniques

**Sub-topics:**
- Soil Properties
- Fertilizers

#### Topic 3: Crop Production and Husbandry (90 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner establishes and manages crop enterprises effectively.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Establish a crop enterprise using appropriate planning and management techniques
- Manage a crop enterprise for optimal production
- Apply integrated pest management practices
- Develop harvesting and post-harvest handling strategies

**Sub-topics:**
- Establishing a Crop Enterprise
- Managing a Crop Enterprise

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 2

#### Topic 4: Crop Harvesting and Handling (81 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner harvests and handles crops effectively to maintain quality and market value.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply appropriate pre-harvest practices to ensure crop quality
- Harvest crops using appropriate techniques and timing
- Handle harvested crops to maintain quality and reduce losses
- Implement post-harvest management strategies

**Sub-topics:**
- Pre-harvest Practices
- Harvesting of the Crop

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 3

#### Topic 5: Crop and Crop Product Marketing (54 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner markets crops and crop products effectively using appropriate marketing strategies.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply cobweb theorem principles in crop marketing
- Develop marketing organization strategies for crops and crop products
- Analyse market trends and consumer preferences for crops
- Create business plans for crop marketing

**Sub-topics:**
- Cobweb Theorem Application in Crop Marketing
- Marketing Organisation of Crop and Crop Products

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., writing, presentations, performances, projects), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Projects and project-based learning are part and parcel of learning in the 21st century. A number of tasks have been integrated in the syllabus for different topics. Teachers are encouraged to develop more project-based learning tasks with learners that can easily be linked to what is happening in their local environment.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.

**Value Chain:** The full range of activities required to bring a product or service from conception, through the different phases of production, delivery to final consumers

**Sustainable Agriculture:** Farming in sustainable ways meeting society's present food and textile needs, without compromising the ability for current or future generations to meet their needs
