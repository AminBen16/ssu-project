# Physics - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Physics  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Physics syllabus is designed to enable learners to develop a deep understanding of physical principles, experimental skills, and real-world applications. The course emphasizes:

- **Theoretical Understanding:** Mechanics, thermodynamics, waves, electricity, magnetism, and modern physics
- **Practical Skills:** Laboratory techniques, experimental design, and data analysis
- **Real-World Applications:** Industrial processes, energy systems, communication technologies, and healthcare applications
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Scientific integrity, environmental responsibility, and safety consciousness

---

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Physics at Advanced Level

The Advanced Level Physics syllabus aims to enable learners to:

1. **Knowledge and Understanding**
   - Understand basic principles and concepts of Physics
   - Know how theories and models are used to explain concepts in Physics
   - Know the resources available and how they are utilised using principles and concepts in Physics
   - Know the use of knowledge, principles and concepts of Physics in everyday life situations

2. **Experimental Skills**
   - Develop an experimental attitude by performing experiments in school
   - Familiarise themselves with and practice scientific methods
   - Develop the necessary skills to design and carry out practical investigations based on the knowledge of Physics

3. **Higher Order Thinking**
   - Develop an initiative for inventiveness
   - Develop capacity to design models and/or devices for use in problem-solving situations, basing on Physics principles

4. **Career Preparation**
   - Prepare for further studies in Physics and the related fields
   - Appreciate the applicability of Physics in other disciplines

### Generic Skills Development

The syllabus integrates the following generic skills:

- **Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving**
  - Planning and carrying out investigations
  - Sorting and analysing information
  - Identifying problems and proposing solutions
  - Predicting outcomes and making reasoned decisions

- **Co-operation and Self-Directed Learning**
  - Working with others effectively
  - Taking responsibility for own learning
  - Working independently with persistence
  - Managing goals and time

- **Creativity and Innovation**
  - Using imaginations to explore possibilities
  - Working with others to generate ideas
  - Suggesting and developing new solutions
  - Experimenting with innovative alternatives
  - Looking for patterns and making generalisations

- **Communication**
  - Listening attentively and with comprehension
  - Talking
  - Reading accurately and fluently
  - Writing and presenting information coherently
  - Using a range of media to communicate ideas

- **Mathematical Computation**
  - Using numbers and measurements accurately
  - Interpreting and interrogating mathematical data
  - Using mathematics to justify and support decisions

- **Digital Literacy**
  - Using technology to create, manipulate and process information
  - Using technology to access, manage and share information

- **Diversity and Multicultural Skills**
  - Appreciate cultural diversity
  - Respectfully responding to people of all cultures

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 9 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical and practical components

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate and research using various resources, including ICTs
   - Problem-solving through questions and scenarios
   - Enhancement of critical thinking, communication and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Hands-on experiences during demonstrations and experiments
   - Construction of knowledge and innovation
   - Development of practical skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Finding solutions through research and projects
   - Development of critical thinking, social and research skills
   - Real-world application of concepts

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Reference to real-world scenarios
   - Discussion and analysis of cases
   - Development of critical thinking, analytical and research skills

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: Measurement and Dimensions of Physical Quantities (10 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner uses a variety of instruments to accurately measure physical quantities and applies the concept of dimensions to establish the relation between these quantities.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Measure a variety of physical quantities with minimal errors
- Analyse the dimensions of a variety of fundamental and derived physical quantities and use them to check the consistency of equations

#### Topic 2: Statics (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the effect of a number of forces on a system and how the centre of gravity relates to the stability of mechanical structures.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Determine the resultant of vectors in different situations
- Appreciate the concepts of moments and torque in the construction of different structures

#### Topic 3: Linear Motion (38 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the effect of force on the motion of bodies on land, water and air, and devises safety precautions for users of automobiles.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Derive the equations describing the linear motion of uniformly accelerated objects and apply them in a variety of situations
- Determine the relative velocity and the distance of closest approach for moving bodies in real life
- Verify the principle of conservation of linear momentum and apply it in a variety of situations
- Apply Newton's laws of motion in different situations

#### Topic 4: Motion Under Gravity (14 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the effect of force on bodies moving under gravity and the applications of this phenomenon in different situations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the behaviour of bodies moving under gravity
- Derive the equations of projectile motion and apply them in different real-life situations

#### Topic 5: Work, Energy and Power (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the resources and transformations of energy in the environment in order to ensure sustainable and improved efficiency of energy utilisation.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Derive expressions for work, energy and power and apply them to solve real-life problems
- Explain the work-energy theorem and apply it to solve problems
- Describe the structure and the energy production by the sun and its implication to life on Earth
- Apply the concepts of renewable and non-renewable energies in the conservation of energy

#### Topic 6: Solid Friction (14 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates factors that determine solid friction and its effect on static and dynamic systems, and uses this concept to model efficient systems with minimised friction.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate the nature of friction between static bodies and its implications
- Investigate the effects of friction on dynamic bodies in order to improve the operation of systems

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 7: Fluid Mechanics (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the effect of molecular forces in fluids, fluid pressure and their applications in fluid systems.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine molecular forces in liquids and their implications
- Assess the application of pressure in fluids in different situations
- Explain pressure variations in flowing fluids and their applications in a variety of situations

#### Topic 8: Mechanical Properties of Matter (12 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner explores the effects of force on the strength of different materials in order to guide on the selection of appropriate materials for construction work.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate the elastic and plastic behaviour of stretched materials and their implications
- Investigate Young's Modulus and measure Young's Modulus for different materials

#### Topic 9: Thermometry (14 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the mode of operation of different types of thermometers and their applications in the real world.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Relate the expressions of temperature scales in order to solve real-life problems
- Compare different types of thermometers and how they are used in real life

#### Topic 10: Heat Quantities (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner explores the behaviour of substances when heat is applied to them in order to sustainably apply them in heat systems.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Deduce that different objects require different amounts of heat to change their temperatures
- Evaluate the concept of change of state and its implication in real life

#### Topic 11: Transfer of Heat (18 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates modes of heat transfer in nature and their application in industry and society.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explain the mechanism of heat conduction in solids and how it applies to materials in heat systems
- Examine the significance of convection in explaining weather and climate
- Deduce that heat can be transferred in form of electromagnetic radiations and that this has many real-life applications

#### Topic 12: Behaviour of Gases (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the effect of heat on the properties of gases and their applications in the operation of different systems in the oil and gas industry.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the behaviour of ideal gases, deduce and verify the ideal gas laws and apply them to solve a variety of numerical problems
- Evaluate the kinetic theory of gases and use it to derive the expression for the pressure of an enclosed system
- Modify the ideal gas equation to suit the behaviour of real gases
- Infer that vapours exert saturated vapour pressure and that this has many real-life applications

#### Topic 13: Thermodynamics (12 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the behaviour of compressed gas systems in relation to their domestic and industrial applications.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Deduce that every system has internal energy which is a function of its temperature
- Analyse the first law of thermodynamics and its implications in real-life situations

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

#### Topic 14: Reflection of Light (14 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the light reflecting properties of different surfaces and their applications in real life.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Employ the laws of reflection of light at plane surfaces to resolve different life scenarios
- Apply curved reflecting surfaces in real-life situations

#### Topic 15: Refraction of Light (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner explores the refractive properties of different materials for application in industries and medicine.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply the concept of refraction on plane surfaces to various situations in real life
- Analyse the concepts of critical angle and total internal reflection and their applications
- Apply lenses in solving a variety of problems in community

#### Topic 16: Optical Instruments (10 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner explores the optical operation of different optical instruments that can be applied in a variety of fields such as medicine, military and navigation.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explain the principle of operation of a compound microscope and their real-life applications
- Describe the principle of operation of refracting telescopes and their applications

#### Topic 17: Electrostatics (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the behaviour of charges at rest and their applications in printing and energy storage in batteries.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply the concept of Electrostatics in production of charge in real-life situations
- Appreciate the existence of a force between charges and its implications
- Examine electric fields and their implications in various situations

#### Topic 18: Capacitors (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner models capacitors for use in electronic devices.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Appreciate that capacitors store charge and have many applications
- Apply the charging and discharging processes of a capacitor in electrical and electronic devices
- Deduce that capacitors store energy which can be used in a variety of real-life situations

#### Topic 19: Digital Electronics (14 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner models electronic circuits using semiconductor materials and electronic devices for a variety of purposes.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the performance of semiconductor diodes in different devices
- Analyse the operation and application of transistors in various situations

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 1

#### Topic 20: Circular Motion (12 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner examines the motion of bodies on curved paths and ensures safety precautions for people using automobiles on such paths.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate the forces acting on bodies moving in circular paths and how they relate to safety
- Derive the expressions for motion under conical pendulum and apply them in real life
- Investigate the conditions for non-skidding motion on banked roads and ensure safety

#### Topic 21: Simple Harmonic Motion (18 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the behaviour of oscillating systems in order to optimise the performance of devices that employ simple harmonic motion.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explain terminologies used in simple harmonic motion (SHM) and apply them to oscillating systems
- Explore energy conservation in oscillating systems and its implications in various daily experiences

#### Topic 22: Gravitation (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner evaluates the effects of gravitation and its applications on objects both on earth and in space.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply the laws and principles of gravitation to real life
- Investigate the effect of gravity on bodies orbiting in space such as communication satellites and in navigation, disaster management and security among others

#### Topic 23: Progressive Waves (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the propagation of progressive waves in matter and its application in different situations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Deduce that waves are a means of transferring energy and that this has many applications
- Deduce that when waves interfere, they generate and properties that have various applications in real life
- Explain concepts related to diffraction of waves and apply them on wavelength; and
- Examine the phenomenon of wave polarisation and how it applies to real life

#### Topic 24: Stationary Waves (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the behaviour of stationary waves and their applications in different situations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the behaviour of stationary waves, progressive waves and their characteristics
- Deduce that stationary waves can be produced in pipes and strings for a variety of wavelengths, velocity and frequency and use it to solve numerical problems
- Illustrate the concept of resonance of waves and its applications

#### Topic 25: Sound Waves (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the behaviour of sound waves and their applications in different situations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse beats, notes and octaves as applied in a variety of situations
- Investigate doppler effect and how it applies to various fields

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 2

#### Topic 26: Current Electricity (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates electric current, its properties, transmission and uses in order to reduce power losses and improve personal and national safety.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Deduce the relationship between current, resistance and voltage and how they relate to transmission of charges in materials
- Investigate the operation of slide wire meters and apply them in a variety of situations

#### Topic 27: Magnetism in Matter (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the magnetic properties of matter and uses them to model different useful magneto-electric devices.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Deduce that the Earth has magnetic properties and this has many implications
- Explain how magnetisation and demagnetisation occur in matter

#### Topic 28: Magnetic Effect of an Electric Current (26 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates that a current carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences a force and uses this concept to design models of different devices to solve societal needs.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate the existence of a force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field
- Use Biot-Savart's law to write expressions for magnetic flux density in a variety of situations
- Explain how the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field is applied in the operation of various electrical devices

#### Topic 29: Electromagnetic Induction (18 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner evaluates how magnetism and current are linked and how this principle is applied in the operation of transformers and generators.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explain the concept of electromagnetic induction and how it is applied in a variety of situations
- Appreciate the concepts of self and mutual induction and their application in a variety of devices

#### Topic 30: A.C Circuits (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner measures a.c and investigates its behaviour in different devices.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explain the operation of a.c measuring devices and their various applications in real life
- Evaluate the inductance, reactance and impedance of a.c circuits and their applications in real life

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 3

#### Topic 31: Atomic Particles (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the structure of the atom and how the components of the atom are applied in different devices.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply Rutherford's scattering experiment to explain atomic structure
- Examine the behaviour of charged particles and their applications in real life

#### Topic 32: Quantum Theory (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner examines how electromagnetic waves carry energy in packets and that this behaviour can be harnessed in a variety of fields.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explain the process of photoelectric effect and its characteristics and applications in real life
- Analyse Bohr's model and how it is used to explain atomic spectra of different elements
- Deduce that when fast moving electrons are stopped by matter, they produce energetic radiations which have many applications

#### Topic 33: Nuclear Processes (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates that nuclear reactions involve high amounts of energy which can be both constructive and destructive.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Appreciate that nuclear reactions generate energy which can be harnessed for different purposes
- Deduce that nuclides disintegrate emitting particles and energy which have a variety of applications
- Appreciate that nuclear reactions generate energy which can be harnessed for different purposes

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – watching learners working (good for assessing skills, values and attitudes)
2. **Conversation** – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and understanding)
3. **Product** – appraising learner's work (writing, report, calculation, presentation, model, drawing, etc.)

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, information from any one form can be checked against the other two forms of assessment for uniformity and reliability.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are assessed using:
- Observation checklists
- Scoring rubrics
- Portfolio assessment
- Project evaluation

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes are assessed over time through:
- Observation of behaviour
- Interactions with learners
- Long-term monitoring of personal development
- Anecdotal records

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

- Assessed using rubrics and observation checklists
- Focus on sustained inquiry and authentic challenges
- Evaluation of driving questions and solutions

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- End-of-topic scenario-based tasks (Activities of Integration)
- End of cycle assessment for certification

### Record-Keeping

Accurate records maintained including:
- Observation checklists
- Rubrics and scoring grids
- Learner portfolios
- Anecdotal notes
- Progress tracking files

## Mathematical Requirements

### Arithmetic Skills

- Recognition and use of expressions in decimal and standard form (scientific)
- Use of appropriate calculating aids (electronic calculator)
- Finding arithmetic means, powers, sines, cosines, tangents, exponentials and logarithms
- Taking account of accuracy in experimental work
- Making approximate evaluations of numerical expressions

### Algebra Skills

- Changing the subject of an equation
- Solving simple algebraic equations
- Substituting physical quantities into physical equations using consistent units
- Formulating simple algebraic equations as mathematical models of physical situations
- Using logarithmic forms of expressions
- Manipulating and solving equations involving logarithmic and exponential functions

### Geometry and Trigonometry

- Calculating areas of triangles, circumference and area of circles, areas and volumes of blocks, cylinders and spheres
- Using Pythagoras' theorem, similarity of triangles, angle sum of a triangle
- Using sines, cosines and tangents for common angles
- Understanding relationship between degrees and radians
- Using trigonometric relationships for triangles

### Vector Skills

- Finding the resultant of two coplanar vectors
- Obtaining expressions for components of a vector in perpendicular directions
- Recognizing situations where vector resolution is appropriate

### Graph Skills

- Translating information between graphical, numerical, algebraic and verbal forms
- Selecting appropriate variables and scales for graph plotting
- Determining slope, intercept and intersection for linear graphs
- Using logarithmic plots to test exponential and power law variations
- Understanding and using the slope of a tangent to a curve
- Understanding and using the area below a curve where the area has physical significance

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** One in which learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit an individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of judging a learner's performance, by interpreting responses to tasks, in order to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills which are deployed in all subjects, and which enhance the learning of those subjects. These skills also equip young people for work and for life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences which allows each student to feel confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn at his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement which specifies what the learner should know, understand, or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired by following the programme of study in a particular learning area; enables a learner to apply the knowledge and understanding of the learning area.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity which gives a learner the opportunity to show the extent to which they have achieved the Learning Outcomes.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An aspect of the normal teaching and learning process that will enable a formative assessment to be made.
