# BIOLOGY SYLLABUS

**Subject:** Biology  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Years:** Senior 5-6  
**Source:** NCDC Uganda, 2025  

---

## OVERVIEW

The Advanced Level Biology curriculum enables learners to apply biological concepts and principles to find practical solutions to day-to-day individual and societal challenges. It promotes the acquisition of deeper understanding of biological concepts, principles, and processes, as well as practical and investigative skills for scientific reasoning and solving real-world problems.

### TIME ALLOCATION
- **Senior 5 & 6:** 9 periods of 40 minutes per week
- **Total periods:** 548 periods across two years

### RATIONALE
The advanced-level Biology curriculum aims to:
- Apply biological concepts and principles to find practical solutions to daily challenges
- Communicate or disseminate accurate biological facts
- Develop the ability to design and carry out scientific investigations, interpret data, and write logical reports
- Innovatively use available resources, including ICT, to research and address societal needs
- Develop interest in exploring ethical, social, and environmental implications of biological advancements

## SENIOR FIVE

### TERM 1: Cell Biology (90 periods)

#### Topic 1.1: Chemicals of Life
**Competency:** The learner evaluates cells and tissues by analysing and relating their structure to function, as a basis for medical research to improve health.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the properties and functions of chemical compounds (water, lipids, proteins including enzymes) in living organisms
- Explain the commercial application of enzyme inhibition
- Operate a light microscope to observe tissues from plants and animals under different magnifications
- Analyse the ultrastructure of animal/plant cells, bacterial cells, and distinguish prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
- Analyse the structures of plant and animal tissues to assess their roles in physiological processes, disease diagnosis, and levels of organisation

---

#### Topic 1.2: Microscopy
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Prepare slides and use a microscope to view and estimate cell size
- Measure the field of view and calculate linear magnification
- Use scientific inquiry skills during microscopic investigations

---

#### Topic 1.3: Diversity of Tissues
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Study and compare images of ultra-structures of animal, plant, and bacterial cells
- Create models to show the parts of plasma membrane and explain their functions
- Explain the fluid-mosaic nature of the membrane and its parts
- Identify structural differences between eubacteria and archaebacteria
- Observe and discuss micrographs, prepared slides of plant and animal tissues
- Dissect and extract sample tissues to assess their location and functions
- Conduct investigations to compare healthy and diseased tissues

---

### TERM 2: Nutrition in Plants (28 periods)

#### Topic 2.1: Photosynthesis in C3 and C4 Plants
**Competency:** The learner evaluates the nutritional strategies of C3 and C4 plants by analysing their photosynthetic pathways and adaptation to environmental conditions to optimise agricultural productivity and food security.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate the relationship between the ultrastructure of a chloroplast and its functions
- Demonstrate the light-dependent and independent stages of photosynthesis in C3 and C4 plants
- Assess the influence of environmental factors on photosynthetic efficiency and crop yields
- Design, build, and operate a mini-greenhouse to optimise photosynthetic rates
- Analyse data on distribution and abundance of C3 and C4 plants at different altitudes, temperatures, and oxygen concentrations

---

### TERM 2: Transport in Humans (80 periods)

#### Topic 3.1: Transport of Gases in Humans
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the mechanisms of transport of substances in the blood and the immune system's role in protecting the human body by analysing processes and their role in maintaining human health.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Assess the role of the human heart in blood circulation and body
- Analyse the role of haemoglobin in oxygen transportation under various physiological conditions
- Examine the transportation of gases in blood under different conditions
- Analyse the responses of athletes versus non-athletes, and lowland versus highland dwellers to exercise
- Understand oxygen dissociation in respiring tissue and the effect of carbon monoxide on oxygen transportation

---

#### Topic 3.2: Immunity in Humans
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the role of antibodies in vaccination and allergic reactions in relation to human body
- Examine types of immunity (innate and adaptive), vaccination concepts, and role of histamine in allergy
- Design antibody models and create visual presentations
- Search for mechanisms used in rapid test kits to detect infections
- Understand new-born haemolytic disease and Rhesus compatibility effects on babies

---

### TERM 3: Respiration (30 periods)

#### Topic 4.1: Mitochondrion Structure and Function
**Competency:** The learner appreciates how living organisms generate cellular energy by analysing respiratory processes and chemical breakdown of food within cells.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the relationship between mitochondrion structure and its functions in cellular respiration
- Analyse the biochemical processes leading to ATP production in living organisms
- Examine how these processes are affected by physical activities and respiratory poisons (cyanide)
- Use simulations to demonstrate glycolysis, citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation
- Analyse the role of acetyl coenzyme A in metabolism of carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins

---

#### Topic 4.2: ATP Production Processes
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the detailed structure of mitochondrion and energy release from ATP
- Analyse key steps in sugar breakdown in cytoplasm and central role of acetyl-CoA in metabolism
- Examine the fate of acetyl-CoA, NAD, and FAD, and cyanide effect on electron transport system
- Analyse the effect of exercise intensity on ATP production

---

### TERM 3: Homeostasis (78 periods)

#### Topic 5.1: Negative Feedback Mechanisms
**Competency:** The learner evaluates the regulation and maintenance of optimal internal environment in living organisms by analysing how organ systems generate and eliminate metabolic wastes.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the homeostatic control system, focusing on maintaining stable internal environment
- Examine components of an efficient homeostatic system and role of negative feedback mechanisms
- Illustrate physiological and behavioural adaptations of endotherms to survive in varying temperature conditions
- Examine the role of thermal receptors and the hypothalamus in regulating temperature in endotherms

---

#### Topic 5.2: Osmoregulation
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine adaptations and management of different plant categories (xerophytes, mesophytes, hydrophytes) to survive in different environments
- Analyse techniques employed by humans to manage water stress
- Investigate excretory plant products (latex, anthocyanins, oils, quinine, saponins) and their uses in everyday life
- Examine how ADH works to maintain water balance in the body
- Analyse animal adaptations to different water availability in their environment

---

## SENIOR SIX

### TERM 1: Coordination (90 periods)

#### Topic 6.1: Plant Hormones and Responses
**Competency:** The learner assesses the physiological and behavioural processes during organisms' responses to internal and external stimuli to promote their welfare and ensure survival and reproductive success.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the role of plant hormones in tropisms and photoperiodism
- Design and carry out scientific investigations on phototropism and geotropism
- Analyse the effect of Gibberellins, cytokinins, Abscisic Acid, and Ethylene on plant growth and commercial applications
- Carry out investigations on the effect of ethylene on raw fruits
- Examine the effect of day length changes on plant flowering and applications in floriculture

---

#### Topic 6.2: Impulse Transmission
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the structure of a neurone, chemical synapse, and factors that influence neural activity
- Analyse impulse transmission under varying stimulations and factors affecting transmission speed
- Distinguish between inhibitory and excitatory synapses
- Explain the role of local anaesthetics and the significance of warmups before physical activity
- Examine impulse behaviour under different temperatures and the influence of local anaesthetics

---

#### Topic 6.3: Sensory Receptors
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the properties and functions of sensory receptors and stimuli
- Analyse the role of the retina in visual perception and the distribution of photoreceptor cells
- Examine the ear's organs of balance in relation to their response to environmental stimuli
- Illustrate the role of the vestibular apparatus in maintaining balance during position and movement

---

#### Topic 6.4: Behaviour
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the significance of innate and learned behaviour through field excursions
- Record findings on different animal behaviour patterns, focusing on survival and reproductive success
- Identify key features and patterns of each type of behaviour
- Explain the evolutionary advancement in behavioural patterns and their contribution to survival across different species

---

### TERM 2: Inheritance and Evolution (90 periods)

#### Topic 7.1: Nucleic Acids Structure and Function (18 periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the transmission of traits from one generation to the next and mechanisms that drive change in a gene pool by analysing concepts of inheritance and evolution.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the structural and functional significance of nucleic acids in meiosis and mitosis
- Create 3D models of DNA, RNA, chromosome and base-pairing rules
- Examine properties of the genetic code and its relationship to nucleotide sequences
- Analyse semi-conservative DNA replication and protein synthesis
- Examine mitosis and meiosis, cancer causes, risk factors, prevention, and management

---

#### Topic 7.2: Gene Technology (72 periods)
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Assess gene technology techniques and their applications in various fields
- Explore Recombinant DNA Technology, Gene Cloning, and Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)
- Examine ethical, social, and environmental implications of gene technology, particularly GMOs
- Hold debates on the implications of gene technology for synthetic insulin production and vaccine development
- Visit facilities specializing in genetic engineering to explore key techniques

---

#### Topic 7.3: Mendelian and Non-Mendelian Inheritance
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply Mendelian genetic crosses to demonstrate inheritance laws and predict patterns
- Use calculations to predict allele frequencies and genotype distributions for populations
- Analyse different forms of allele interactions and their influence on phenotypic expression
- Distinguish Mendelian from non-Mendelian inheritance (autosomal linkage, multiple alleles, codominance, incomplete dominance)
- Examine the Hardy-Weinberg principle and factors that upset genetic equilibrium

---

#### Topic 7.4: Evolutionary Advancements in Life Processes
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse evolutionary advancements in key life processes (circulation, reproduction, gaseous exchange, coordination, movement, excretion)
- Use visual aids to summarise findings and discuss the significance of these advancements for survival of living organisms
- Explain how advancements in life processes improve efficiency and their evolutionary trends across different species

---

#### Topic 7.5: Speciation and Extinction
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Assess speciation and resistance mechanisms and factors contributing to extinction
- Analyse case studies of antimicrobial and pesticide resistance through problem-based learning
- Examine timelines of documented mass extinctions, highlighting key events, causes, and effects
- Explain how isolation mechanisms contribute to evolution and how resistance arises
- Suggest and develop solutions for resistance management

---

### TERM 2: Growth and Development (36 periods)

#### Topic 8.1: Growth in Plants
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse pre- and post-germination stages during growth and development of plants
- Examine the concept of seed dormancy, seed banks, and factors affecting germination and measurement of growth
- Analyse the significance of primary and secondary growth
- Use microscopes to investigate cell size in different regions of young dicotyledonous roots or shoots
- Assess the significance of seed dormancy and banks for crop production

---

#### Topic 8.2: Metamorphosis in Insects
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the role of insect growth stages in ecosystems, focusing on waste management, food security, and water quality assessment
- Sort and analyse information on insect roles in ecosystems
- Use a range of media to present findings on insect growth stages
- Examine the contributions of insect growth stages to waste management, food security, and water quality assessment

---

### TERM 3: Ecology (90 periods)

#### Topic 9.1: Population Ecology
**Competency:** The learner evaluates interactions within ecosystems by analysing data to develop strategies for enhancing food security and promoting sustainable management of natural resources.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse population dynamics and factors affecting them in different ecosystems
- Conduct field studies to estimate population density using quadrats and capture-recapture techniques
- Use numbers and measurements accurately and interpret population data
- Take precautions and assumptions to obtain accurate results in population ecology studies

---

#### Topic 9.2: Succession and Ecological Restoration
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the processes of ecological succession and strategies for effective ecological restoration practices in diverse degraded environments
- Examine the role of primary and secondary succession in preserving biodiversity
- Evaluate different solutions for restoring degraded ecosystems
- Plan, design, and implement small-scale restoration projects on degraded areas

---

#### Topic 9.3: Energy Flow in Ecosystems
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the concept of energy flow through ecosystems and its role in maintaining ecosystem stability
- Use ICT tools to illustrate energy flow in ecosystems
- Analyse the effect of human activities on energy flow and ecosystem stability
- Explain bio-accumulation, bio-magnification, and feed conversion ratios and their relationship to energy transfer

---

#### Topic 9.4: Carbon Footprint
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the concept of carbon footprint in relation to climate change, including its measurement
- Analyse activities that contribute to greenhouse gas emissions and carbon output
- Examine the role of carbon sequestration in forests and how it reduces the impact of climate change
- Use ICT tools to present findings on carbon footprint measurement and mitigation strategies

---

#### Topic 9.5: Invasive Species
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the impact of invasive terrestrial or aquatic species on native biodiversity, ecosystems, and economies
- Analyse characteristics of invasive species, their impacts, and strategies for management and control
- Examine case studies of pest infestation controlled using biological agents
- Assess the feasibility of proposed control measures and benefits of biological control over pesticides

---

#### Topic 9.6: Food Security
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the concept of food security, focusing on its components (availability, access, utilisation, and stability)
- Examine the roles of sustainable agricultural practices in maintaining stable food supply
- Analyse threats and solutions to food security challenges
- Use ICT tools to communicate findings on sustainable agricultural practices that support food security

## ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

### Formative Assessment
**Three-Way Assessment Approach:**
- **Observation:** Watching learners working (assessing skills, values, and attitudes)
- **Conversation:** Asking questions and talking to learners (assessing knowledge and understanding)
- **Product:** Appraising learner's work (reports, presentations, models, scientific investigations)

**Continuous Assessment:**
- Daily classroom-based assessment
- Focus on learning process and progress
- Integral part of teaching-learning process
- Teacher uses evidence to guide next learning steps

### Summative Assessment
- **End of year school-based assessment** (100%)
- **No term examinations** - replaced by ongoing teacher assessments
- **End-of-topic scenario-based tasks** (Activities of Integration)
- **End-of-cycle assessment** for certification

### Achievement Scale
- **3:** All Learning Outcomes achieved – achievement with ease
- **2:** Most Learning Outcomes achieved, enough for overall achievement
- **1:** Some Learning Outcomes achieved, but not sufficient for overall achievement
- **0:** No Learning Outcome achieved

### Key Assessment Areas
- **Knowledge:** Biological concepts, theories, principles
- **Understanding:** Application of biological concepts, data analysis, scientific reasoning
- **Skills:** Laboratory techniques, scientific investigations, data interpretation, report writing
- **Values:** Scientific ethics, environmental responsibility, health awareness

## ICT INTEGRATION

### Applications in Learning
- **Field Work:** Use cameras to take photos and record videos of biological specimens
- **Presentations:** Use presentation applications for scientific reports
- **Research:** Use online databases and search engines for biological information
- **Data Analysis:** Use spreadsheet software for population ecology data
- **Simulations:** Use virtual labs and animations for complex biological processes
- **Modeling:** Use graphics programs for 3D models of biological structures
- **Communication:** Use collaboration tools for group research projects

### Cross-Cutting Issues Integration
- **Environmental Awareness:** Conservation biology, ecosystem management, climate change
- **Health Awareness:** Disease prevention, medical research, public health
- **Life Skills:** Scientific inquiry, critical thinking, problem-solving
- **Socio-economic Challenges:** Food security, sustainable development, biotechnology ethics

## GENERIC SKILLS DEVELOPMENT

### Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving
- Plan and carry out scientific investigations
- Sort and analyse biological data
- Identify problems and propose solutions
- Predict outcomes and make reasoned decisions
- Evaluate different solutions

### Co-operation and Self-Directed Learning
- Work effectively in diverse teams during scientific investigations
- Interact effectively with others during group discussions
- Take responsibility for own learning
- Work independently with persistence
- Manage goals and time

### Creativity and Innovation
- Use imagination to explore possibilities in biological research
- Work with others to generate ideas for experiments
- Suggest and develop new solutions to biological problems
- Experiment with innovative alternatives
- Look for patterns and make generalizations

### Communication
- Listen attentively and with comprehension during discussions
- Talk confidently and explain biological concepts clearly
- Read and understand scientific literature accurately
- Write and present scientific reports coherently
- Use a range of media to communicate biological findings

### Mathematical Computation
- Use numbers and measurements accurately in experiments
- Interpret and interrogate mathematical data in biological contexts
- Use mathematics to justify and support scientific decisions
- Apply statistical analysis to biological data

## PROJECT-BASED LEARNING

### Project Requirements
- **Duration:** Extended period of time for sustained inquiry
- **Driving Question:** Authentic challenge related to real-world biological problems
- **Investigation:** Scientific research methodology and data collection
- **Report:** Scientific report with proper documentation
- **Presentation:** Communication of findings to appropriate audience

### Assessment Methods
- **Rubric:** Detailed scoring criteria for project evaluation
- **Observation Checklist:** Monitoring progress during project development
- **Portfolio:** Collection of evidence and artifacts
- **Peer Assessment:** Collaborative evaluation of project outcomes

## GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS

- **Competency:** Ability to apply learning with confidence in various situations
- **Differentiation:** Adaptation of learning experiences to individual learner needs
- **Formative Assessment:** Process of judging performance to gauge progress
- **Generic Skills:** Skills deployed across subjects that enhance learning
- **Inclusion:** Approach ensuring all students feel confident, respected, and safe
- **Learning Outcome:** Statement specifying what learner should know, understand, or do
- **Process Skill:** Capability to apply knowledge and understanding of the subject
- **Sample Assessment Strategy:** Activity allowing learner to demonstrate achievement
