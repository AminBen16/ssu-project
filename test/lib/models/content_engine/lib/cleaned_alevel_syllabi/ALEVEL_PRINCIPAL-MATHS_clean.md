# Principal Mathematics - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Principal Mathematics  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Principal Mathematics syllabus is designed to develop learners' ability to use mathematical tools to analyse and interpret data in various fields, enabling logical reasoning and informed decision-making. The course emphasizes:

- **Data Analysis:** Statistical interpretation, probability theory, and predictive modeling
- **Mathematical Communication:** Effective presentation of mathematical concepts and solutions
- **Model Development:** Application of mathematical models to real-world problems in industry, business, health, insurance, and finance
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Mathematical integrity, precision, and analytical thinking

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Principal Mathematics at Advanced Level

The Advanced Level Principal Mathematics syllabus aims at:

1. **Analytical Skills Development**
   - Developing the learner's ability to use mathematical tools to analyse and interpret data in various fields
   - Enabling logical reasoning and informed decision-making
   - Fostering critical thinking and problem-solving skills through mathematical applications

2. **Practical Application**
   - Equipping learners with skills to model, analyse, and solve practical problems using mathematical approaches
   - Fostering appreciation for the role of mathematics in personal, academic, and societal development
   - Guiding learners to present mathematical reasoning, computations, and solutions effectively

3. **Higher Order Thinking**
   - Promoting acquisition of Higher-Order Thinking Skills (HOTS) such as inquiry, creativity, innovation, decision-making, critical thinking, and problem-solving
   - Encouraging learner-centered pedagogies with hands-on experience in real-life situations
   - Preparing learners for further education and careers in mathematics and related fields

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 8 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical and practical components

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate through research directed by their interest
   - Problem-solving through questions and scenarios
   - Enhancement of critical thinking, communication and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Hands-on experiences during research
   - Learning through reflecting upon work
   - Development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Finding solutions through research and projects
   - Development of critical thinking, social and research skills
   - Real-world application of mathematical concepts

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Reference to real-world scenarios
   - Discussion and analysis of cases
   - Development of critical thinking, analytical and research skills

5. **Discovery Learning**
   - Constructing knowledge through active participation
   - Exploration and inquiry
   - Critical thinking, questioning and hypothesising

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: Numerical Concepts (10 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses contextual use of indices, logarithms and surds through mathematical computations for precision, developing analytical skills and making informed decisions to communicate effectively.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply the laws of indices and logarithms to solve real-life problems involving exponential growth, decay, or scientific calculations
- Use properties of surds to solve real-world situations that require precise calculations and accurate results

#### Topic 2: Equations and Inequalities (26 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates equations and inequalities to acquire mathematical computational and analytical skills applicable in the real world.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Interpret the solutions of quadratic equations in various real-life contexts involving solutions, optimisation
- Apply solutions of linear simultaneous equations in one variable to solve real-world problems involving multiple constraints
- Apply quadratic inequalities to determine feasible solution sets in real-world scenarios such as determining profit ranges and resource allocation
- Manipulate polynomial equations and identify the degree of a polynomial to solve problems in various contexts such as engineering, economics, and physics

#### Topic 3: Coordinate Geometry 1 (12 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses linear relationships to solve geometric problems and interpret spatial relationships in real-life situations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine basic Cartesian geometry concepts for solving problems related to lines, shapes, and their positions in the plane
- Apply concepts of basic coordinate geometry to solve real-world problems and interpret geometric relationships

#### Topic 4: Partial Fractions (8 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner decomposes rational expressions into partial fractions useful in integral calculus and real-world context.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Decompose proper and improper algebraic expressions into partial fractions for further learning

#### Topic 5: Trigonometry (25 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies trigonometric functions, identities, and equations to solve problems in mathematics and real-life application in navigation, construction, aviation and other fields.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the sides and angles of a right-angled triangle to deduce trigonometrical ratios, derive trigonometric identities, and solve trigonometric related problems
- Apply trigonometrical techniques to solve problems related to various fields such as navigation, construction, aviation

#### Topic 6: Descriptive Statistics (15 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses data through collection, presentation, and evaluation of population parameters and sample statistics in order to predict and plan for self and community development.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Present statistical data for prediction and further learning
- Analyse data in order to predict phenomena for purposes of decision making and community development

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 7: Scatter Diagrams and Correlations (8 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates the relationship between two quantitative variables through graphical representation and evaluation of correlation coefficients for decision making and prediction.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Determine the correlation between two variables noting learners' ability to interpret coefficients for prediction and decision making
- Argue on the relationship between two quantitative variables for prediction and informed decision making

#### Topic 8: Dynamics 1 (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner determines the resultant force and analyses the effect of forces on bodies by applying Newton's laws to solve problems in real world phenomena.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Determine the resultants of parallel and non-parallel forces for lifelong learning
- Evaluate the resultant of forces in polygons for lifelong learning
- Investigate the effects of friction on bodies in limiting equilibrium and in motion on horizontal and inclined surfaces for application in engineering
- Apply Newton's laws of motion to connected bodies to solve problems involving real-world phenomena

#### Topic 9: Probability Theory (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner evaluates probabilities through applying the probability laws and theorems to predict the occurrence of events in real life.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Determine the chances of occurrence of events in different aspects for prediction and decision making in the real-world situations
- Apply the probability theorems to predict other occurrences and plan for the future

#### Topic 10: Differentiation 1 (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies differentiation techniques to solve problems in calculus and interpret their significance in real-world contexts.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explore rates of change and slopes of curves using differentiation for further learning
- Interpret the significance of differentiation in calculus and solving real-world problems in fields like Physics, Biology, and Environmental Science

#### Topic 11: Integration 1 (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies integration techniques to solve problems involving areas under curves, accumulation of quantities, and other real-world applications.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Relate integration to differentiation and to integrate definite and infinite integrals
- Apply integration concepts in various fields focusing on the ability to determine area under curve bounded by the x-axis and y-axis, areas enclosed between two functions, volume, displacement, velocity and acceleration, mean value of a function, and volume of a solid of revolution

#### Topic 12: Permutations and Combinations (10 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies the permutations and combinations concepts to solve mathematical problems and model real-world situations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply the concepts of permutations to solve problems in Mathematics, everyday life and other fields such as computer algorithm design, scheduling, and cryptography
- Analyse concepts of combinations to select objects from objects to solve problems in Mathematics, everyday life and other infields like genetics, optimisation and statistics

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

#### Topic 13: Series (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies principles and techniques of series and binomial expansions to understand and solve mathematical and scientific problems in the real world.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse Arithmetic Progression (A.P) to apply principles to solve mathematical problems; recognise patterns and their applications in various contexts
- Analyse Geometrical Progression (G.P) to apply principles to solve problems; recognise patterns and their applications in various contexts
- Apply the logical steps to establish the truth of a mathematical statement in solving real world problems that require inductive reasoning
- Apply the binomial theorem to solve real-world problems such as stochastic models

#### Topic 14: Random Variables (30 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner models and analyses the outcomes of random phenomena through determining probabilities and expected values for prediction of uncertainties in real life.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Design sample spaces and different corresponding experiments with coins, pair of dice and record the outcomes; compute and explain properties of random variables, probability mass/density function notations and definition of a random variable
- Analyse the concept of a discrete random variable and explain the properties of its probability mass function (p.m.f); determine probabilities, parameters and draw graphs
- Analyse the concepts of a continuous random variable and explain the properties of its probability density function (p.d.f); determine probabilities, measures of central location and dispersion and sketch the p.d.f and cumulative distribution function of continuous random variables

#### Topic 15: Probability Distributions (30 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates the ability to analyse and apply binomial, rectangular, and normal distributions to solve real-world problems.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply the binomial distribution function to solve relevant probability and statistical problems in real life
- Demonstrate properties of the rectangular distribution, derive its probability distribution function, p.d.f and use it to obtain relevant probabilities and statistical problems in real life
- Analyse the normal distribution and how it can be standardised to a standard normal using graphical representations to solve relevant probability and statistical problems in real life
- Transform a binomial random variable to a normal using continuity correction
- Apply the binomial, rectangular and normal distributions to predict outcomes of random processes in real life

#### Topic 16: Error Analysis (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses errors in mathematical operations and functions to understand the role of precision and accuracy in problem-solving.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the propagation of errors in mathematical operations and functions
- Apply the knowledge of error analysis to improve the level of precision and accuracy in problem

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 1

#### Topic 17: Vectors (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies vector concepts to model and solve mathematical and contextual physical problems in 2D and 3D spaces.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Perform operations to determine magnitude and direction in 2D and 3D spaces
- Analyse properties of lines in 2D and 3D spaces for real-life applications
- Analyse properties of planes in 2D and 3D spaces for real-life applications

#### Topic 18: Differentiation 2 (40 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies differentiation techniques to non-algebraic functions, Maclaurin's series and irrational curves to analyse real-world phenomena in various fields.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply trigonometric derivatives to solve limits and derivatives of trigonometric functions from first principles; determine derivatives of trigonometric functions from first principles to solve mathematical problems applicable in fields such as engineering and Physics
- Apply differentiation rules to exponential and logarithmic functions; sketch exponential and logarithmic graphs to observe their shape and characteristics, recognising their inverse relationship
- Generate Maclaurin's series to approximate polynomial functions to solve advanced calculus problems and real-world scenarios, particularly in engineering and Physics
- Develop graphing skills to sketch curves to enhance spatial ability and visualisation in solving real-world contexts such as optimisation

#### Topic 19: Integration 2 (34 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies appropriate methods of integration to solve real-world problems.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Determine definite and indefinite integrals of different functions using various techniques of integration to solve problems in engineering, economics, etc.
- Apply the concepts of integration to solve problems of forces and moments in engineering, economics, population dynamics, statistical modelling using the normal distribution

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 2

#### Topic 20: Dynamics 2 (28 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses motion patterns of objects as observed from different reference points to predict their behaviour and solve kinematic issues.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Determine the resultant velocity of bodies in motion for prediction
- Analyse the relative velocity, displacement, course and time for collision and closest approach to simplify motion related problems
- Apply the equations of linear motion to solve problems involving projectiles in the real world

#### Topic 21: Trapezium Rule (6 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies the trapezium rule to estimate definite integrals and areas under a curve to solve problems involving numerical integration.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate definite integrals and areas under curves using the trapezium rule
- Generate a comparison between numerical and analytical solutions under a curve to solve problems

#### Topic 22: Sampling Distribution (12 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner estimates population parameters by using sample distributions to predict and eliminate variability in research and collection of statistical data.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Determine probabilities involving sample mean for a normal population for prediction and decision making
- Apply confidence intervals and point estimation to solve real-world challenges

#### Topic 23: Iterative Methods (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies iterative numerical methods to approximate solutions to problems while critically analysing accuracy and limitations of methods used in real life situations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Estimate the unknown values using gradient method for prediction and decision making
- Investigate the range within which the roots of the function lie for decision making
- Approximate the root of a non-linear function using Newton Raphson method and further linear interpolation to determine iterative schemes for decision making

#### Topic 24: Coordinate Geometry 2 (28 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies algebraic and geometric techniques to analyse spatial relationships applicable in real-world contexts.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse loci to understand geometric relationships in real-world contexts such as navigation, robotics, surveying and microphone placement
- Analyse locus of a circle to solve contextual problems in geometry, motion, engineering, and architecture
- Analyse locus of a parabola to solve contextual problems in geometry, motion, engineering and architecture
- Analyse locus of an ellipse to solve contextual problems in geometry, motion, engineering and architecture

#### Topic 25: Complex Numbers (28 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies concepts of complex numbers to solve modelling problems across different mathematical and real-world scenarios.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explain the algebra of complex numbers
- Evaluate different forms of complex numbers for application in relevant situations
- Appreciate the application of complex numbers to solve modelling problems across different mathematical and real-life scenarios

#### Topic 26: Differential Equations (26 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner models and solves problems involving rates of change to interpret the solutions for prediction in context for acquisition of analytical skills.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate the ability to solve differential equations using various techniques in real life scenarios
- Generate differential equations to model and analyse real-world situations such as population growth/decline, price variations, temperature changes, drug absorption

#### Topic 27: Flow Charts (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner develops flowcharts to organise and represent mathematical processes and problem-solving strategies by breaking down complex tasks into simpler, more manageable steps, and applies these skills to tackle emerging challenges.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Design flow charts to represent mathematical processes using dry runs to identify areas of improvement or to detect errors and to solve a given mathematical problem
- Apply flowcharts to represent and solve real world problems in designing computer algorithms, business processes, production lines in industry

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – watching learners working (good for assessing skills, values and attitudes)
2. **Conversation** – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and understanding)
3. **Product** – appraising learner's work (writing, report, calculation, presentation, model, drawing, etc.)

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, information from any one form can be checked against the other two forms of assessment for uniformity and reliability.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are assessed using:
- Observation checklists
- Scoring rubrics
- Portfolio assessment
- Project evaluation

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes are assessed over time through:
- Observation of behaviour
- Interactions with learners
- Long-term monitoring of personal development
- Anecdotal records

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

- Assessed using rubrics and observation checklists
- Focus on sustained inquiry and authentic challenges
- Evaluation of driving questions and solutions

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- End-of-topic scenario-based tasks (Activities of Integration)
- End of cycle assessment for certification

### Record-Keeping

Accurate records maintained including:
- Observation checklists
- Rubrics and scoring grids
- Learner portfolios
- Anecdotal notes
- Progress tracking files

## Mathematical Requirements

### Arithmetic Skills

- Recognition and use of expressions in decimal and standard form (scientific)
- Use of appropriate calculating aids (electronic calculator)
- Finding arithmetic means, powers, sines, cosines, tangents, exponentials and logarithms
- Taking account of accuracy in experimental work
- Making approximate evaluations of numerical expressions

### Algebra Skills

- Changing the subject of an equation
- Solving simple algebraic equations
- Substituting physical quantities into equations using consistent units
- Formulating algebraic equations as mathematical models
- Using logarithmic forms of expressions
- Manipulating and solving equations involving logarithmic and exponential functions

### Geometry and Trigonometry

- Calculating areas of triangles, circumference and area of circles, areas and volumes
- Using Pythagoras' theorem, similarity of triangles, angle sum of a triangle
- Using sines, cosines and tangents for common angles
- Understanding relationship between degrees and radians
- Using trigonometric relationships for triangles

### Vector Skills

- Finding the resultant of two coplanar vectors
- Obtaining expressions for components of a vector in perpendicular directions
- Recognizing situations where vector resolution is appropriate

### Graph Skills

- Translating information between graphical, numerical, algebraic and verbal forms
- Selecting appropriate variables and scales for graph plotting
- Determining slope, intercept and intersection for linear graphs
- Using logarithmic plots to test exponential and power law variations
- Understanding and using the slope of a tangent to a curve
- Understanding and using the area below a curve where the area has physical significance

### Statistical Skills

- Organising and presenting statistical data
- Computing measures of central tendency and dispersion
- Using probability distributions
- Applying sampling methods
- Interpreting statistical results

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** One in which learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit an individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of judging a learner's performance, by interpreting responses to tasks, in order to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills which are deployed in all subjects, and which enhance the learning of those subjects. These skills also equip young people for work and for life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences which allows each student to feel confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn at his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement which specifies what the learner should know, understand, or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired by following the programme of study in a particular learning area; enables a learner to apply the knowledge and understanding of the learning area.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity which gives a learner the opportunity to show the extent to which they have achieved the Learning Outcomes.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An aspect of the normal teaching and learning process that will enable a formative assessment to be made.
