# Literature in English - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Literature in English  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Literature in English syllabus aims to develop learners' appreciation, analysis, and creation of literary works across four main genres: Prose, Poetry, Drama/Plays, and The Novel and Short Story. It focuses on enhancing learners' ability to understand, analyse, and create literary works while fostering critical thinking, creativity, and communication skills. The course emphasizes:

- **Literary Analysis:** Understanding and analysing various literary genres and techniques
- **Creative Writing:** Developing skills in composing original literary works
- **Critical Thinking:** Analyzing themes, styles, and techniques in literature
- **Communication Skills:** Effective oral and written expression in literary contexts
- **Cultural Appreciation:** Understanding diverse cultural perspectives through literature
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Literary appreciation, cultural diversity, and ethical reasoning

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Literature in English at Advanced Level

The Advanced Level Literature in English syllabus aims to:

1. **Literary Proficiency Development**
   - Develop reading for pleasure and increase learners' language proficiency
   - Develop learners' personal life skills like problem-solving, critical thinking, decision-making, creativity, researching and managing information systematically
   - Shape the affective, develop the cognitive and apply the psychomotor skills
   - Appreciate the differences across the various genres
   - Appreciate their own cultural heritage and that of others

2. **Cultural and Personal Development**
   - Cultivate appropriate attitude to life
   - Develop and exhibit values e.g., honesty, integrity, patriotism, unity, empathy etc.
   - Promote appreciation and understanding of cultural heritage
   - Foster personal growth through literary exploration

3. **Academic and Career Preparation**
   - Prepare learners for further studies in literature, linguistics, and related fields
   - Develop transferable skills applicable in various professional contexts
   - Enable application of literary analysis skills in real-world contexts
   - Foster opportunities for creative and academic writing

4. **Communication Enhancement**
   - Develop effective communication skills through literary analysis
   - Enhance language proficiency and expression
   - Promote confidence in oral and written communication
   - Enable sophisticated literary discourse

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 9 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across four literary genres

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate through research directed by their interest
   - Solve problems through series of questions and scenarios
   - Enhances critical thinking, communication and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners actively participate in hands-on experiences during research
   - Learn through reflecting upon what they are doing
   - Leads to development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners find solutions to problems through their experience in research and projects
   - Develops critical thinking, social and research skills

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Learners refer to real world scenarios to discuss and analyse them
   - Enables them to develop critical thinking, analytical and research skills

5. **Discovery Learning**
   - Learners construct their own knowledge through active participation, exploration and inquiry
   - Encourages them to critically think, ask questions and hypothesise through research

## Detailed Syllabus Content

The syllabus treats the four main genres of Literature in English: Prose, Poetry, Drama/Plays, and The Novel and the Short Story as individual topics.

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: Prose (7 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses and creates various types of prose works considering literary elements to strengthen writing and communication skills.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the meaning, forms, and functions of literature to society
- Analyze the introduction to prose and its functions
- Examine characteristics of prose and different categories (descriptive, narrative, argumentative, expository)
- Analyze aspects of prose including title, writer/author/narrator/speaker, tone, setting, audience/readership
- Examine language in prose including diction and register
- Analyze figurative language in prose including imagery, metaphor, simile

**Sub-topics:**
- Introduction to Literature
- Forms of Literature
- Functions of Literature to the Society
- Introduction to Prose
- Functions of Prose
- Characteristics of Prose
- Categories of Prose (Descriptive, Narrative, Argumentative, Expository)
- Aspects of Prose (Title, Writer/Author/Narrator/Speaker, Tone, Setting, Audience/Readership)
- Language in Prose (Diction, Register)
- Figurative Language in Prose (Imagery, Metaphor, Simile)

#### Topic 2: Poetry (2 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses and composes various types of poems for communication, education, and entertainment.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the introduction to poetry and its functions
- Examine characteristics of poetry and different types (sonnets, free verse, elegy, epic, lyric, ballads, ode, etc.)
- Analyze aspects of poetry including title, tone, rhythm, structure, attitude
- Examine language in poetry including diction and poetic license
- Analyze sound devices and poetic devices
- Understand poet persona and various aspects of poetry

**Sub-topics:**
- Introduction to Poetry
- Functions of Poetry
- Characteristics of Poetry
- Types of Poetry (Sonnets, Free verse, Elegy, Epic, Lyric, Ballads, Ode, etc.)
- Aspects of Poetry (Title, Tone, Rhythm, Structure, Attitude)
- Language in Poetry (Diction, Poetic License)
- Poet Persona

#### Topic 3: Drama/Plays (2 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses and creates dramatic works for performance and literary appreciation.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the introduction to drama/plays and their functions
- Differentiate between a written script and a performed play
- Examine major types of plays (comedy, tragedy, tragicomedy, historical plays)
- Analyze elements of a play including setting, structure, dialogue, character and characterization
- Examine elements of drama including stage directions, props and costumes, interlude, nomenclature

**Sub-topics:**
- Introduction to Drama/Plays
- Functions of Drama/Plays
- Difference between a Written script and a Performed Play
- Major Types of Plays (Comedy, Tragedy, Tragicomedy, Historical Plays)
- Elements of a Play (Setting, Structure)
- Elements of Drama (Character and Characterisation, Dialogue)
- Elements of Drama (continued) (Stage Directions)
- Style/Dramatic Techniques (Asides, Soliloquy)
- Elements of Drama (continued) (Verse, Lighting, Monologue, Props and Costumes, Interlude, Nomenclature)

#### Topic 4: The Novel and Short Story (2 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses and creates narrative works understanding literary techniques and storytelling.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the introduction to the novel and short story and their functions
- Examine characteristics of the novel and short story
- Analyze aspects of the novel and short story including structure, setting, storyline, plot
- Examine narrative techniques including point of view (first person, second person, third person)
- Analyze narrative techniques including flashbacks, reminiscence, foreshadow, stream of consciousness

**Sub-topics:**
- Introduction to the Novel and the Short Story
- Functions of the Novel and the Short Story
- Characteristics of the Novel and the Short Story
- Aspects of the Novel (Structure, Setting, Storyline, Plot)
- Narrative Techniques (Point of View: First Person, Second Person and Third Person)
- Narrative Techniques (continued) (Flashbacks, Reminiscence, Foreshadow, Stream of Consciousness)

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 1: Prose (27 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner conducts in-depth analysis of prose works using various texts.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyze the salient aspects of prose using a variety of prose texts
- Conduct in-depth analysis of style using a variety of prose pieces of different categories and types
- Analyze figurative language and its effectiveness in prose using a variety of prose passages
- Create different categories of prose in groups and at individual level
- Compile a prose anthology at group/class level

#### Topic 2: Poetry (26 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyzes and creates various poetic forms and devices.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyze the aspects of poetry using a variety of poems from each category
- Conduct in-depth analysis of poetic devices in selected poems
- Analyze figurative language, its usage and effectiveness in poetry
- Create various forms of poetry (sonnets, free verse, elegy, ode, ballad, lyrical poem, tongue twister, dirge)
- Conduct group and individual recitation of various forms of poetry

#### Topic 3: Drama/Plays (27 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyzes dramatic techniques and creates original dramatic works.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyze the elements of drama using set books
- Analyze dramatic techniques and their effectiveness in set books
- Identify social themes for creating drama pieces
- Create real-life characters in line with play categories and themes
- Create settings for plays in line with categories and themes
- Compile one-act plays observing various categories, aspects and language

#### Topic 4: The Novel and Short Story (28 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyzes narrative techniques and creates original narrative works.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyze narrative techniques in the novel and short story
- Analyze themes, relevance, lessons and feelings in set books
- Create appropriate titles and develop effective plots for novellas
- Create, structure and present the plot of novellas in chapters and sub-chapters
- Create life-like characters for novellas
- Create suitable settings for plots and characters
- Write complete novellas observing all aspects, style and language

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., writing, presentations, performances, projects), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Project-based learning is assessed using a rubric and an observation checklist to evaluate how well learners engage with the project, their problem-solving abilities, and the final outcomes. In Literature in English, learners will be required to write their own prose pieces, poetry anthology, a one-act drama script and a novella; plus, any other customised projects given in this subject.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.

**Prose:** Written or spoken language in its ordinary form, without metrical structure

**Poetry:** Literary work in which special intensity is given to the expression of feelings and ideas by the use of distinctive style and rhythm

**Drama:** A composition intended for performance by actors on a stage

**Novel:** A long narrative work of fiction, typically published as a book

**Short Story:** A piece of prose fiction that can be read in one sitting
