# Technical Drawing - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Technical Drawing  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Technical Drawing syllabus fosters learners' creativity and innovation by integrating geometric and engineering principles to enhance the effective communication of design ideas. It emphasizes the application of mechanical and building construction techniques to address societal challenges while promoting environmental conservation. The syllabus promotes acquisition of Higher-order Thinking Skills (HOTS) such as inquiry, creativity and innovation, decision-making, critical thinking and problem-solving. It advocates for learner-centred pedagogies that engage learners in hands-on experience within real-life contexts, taking into account the diverse abilities and learning styles of individual learners. The course emphasizes:

- **Geometric Construction:** Advanced geometric principles and their applications in technical drawing
- **Building Construction:** Foundations, floors, walls, roofs, and building drawing techniques
- **Mechanical Drawing:** Machine drawing, power transmission systems, and surface development
- **3D Visualization:** Projection of solids, intersection of solids, and vector geometry
- **Design Communication:** Effective communication of design ideas through technical drawing
- **Problem-Solving:** Application of technical drawing skills to solve real-world challenges
- **Environmental Conservation:** Sustainable design practices and environmental awareness
- **Career Preparation:** Skills for engineering, architecture, and related technical fields

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### GEOMETRICAL AND BUILDING CONSTRUCTION SYLLABUS

#### Topic 1: Projection of Solids
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates understanding of projection techniques for three-dimensional objects.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply principles of orthographic projection to represent 3D objects
- Draw projections of various solid shapes and geometric forms
- Analyze spatial relationships in three-dimensional representations
- Solve complex projection problems involving multiple views

#### Topic 2: Foundations and Floors
**Competency:** The learner applies technical drawing skills to represent building foundations and floor systems.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Draw detailed foundation plans and sections
- Represent different types of flooring systems in technical drawings
- Apply building codes and standards to foundation and floor drawings
- Design appropriate foundation systems for different soil conditions

#### Topic 3: Surface Development
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates skills in developing surfaces of three-dimensional objects into two-dimensional patterns.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Develop surface patterns for various geometric solids
- Apply mathematical principles in surface development
- Create accurate templates for fabrication purposes
- Solve complex surface development problems

#### Topic 4: Walls
**Competency:** The learner applies technical drawing principles to represent wall systems and constructions.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Draw detailed wall sections and elevations
- Represent different types of wall construction systems
- Apply structural principles in wall design drawings
- Design appropriate wall systems for various building types

#### Topic 5: Vector Geometry
**Competency:** The learner applies vector geometry principles in technical drawing applications.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply vector principles to solve geometric problems
- Use vector analysis in technical drawing contexts
- Represent spatial relationships using vector notation
- Solve complex geometric problems using vector methods

#### Topic 6: Intersection of Solids
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates understanding of intersection principles between three-dimensional objects.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Draw intersections of various solid objects
- Apply geometric principles to solve intersection problems
- Represent complex intersection scenarios in technical drawings
- Analyze spatial relationships in intersecting solids

#### Topic 7: Roofs
**Competency:** The learner applies technical drawing skills to represent roof systems and structures.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Draw detailed roof plans and sections
- Represent different types of roof construction systems
- Apply structural principles in roof design drawings
- Design appropriate roof systems for various building types

#### Topic 8: Building Drawing
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates comprehensive building drawing skills integrating all construction elements.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Create complete building drawing sets including plans, elevations, and sections
- Integrate all building systems in comprehensive drawings
- Apply building codes and standards to complete building drawings
- Design complete building systems for various purposes

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., technical drawings, projects, portfolios), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Projects and project-based learning are fundamental to 21st-century education. In Technical Drawing, these approaches provide learners with hands-on experience, develop essential skills and better prepare them for a career in engineering and related fields. The learner will be required to design and draw complex systems, creating real-life objects, models or prototypes, connecting Technical Drawing to other subjects like Physics and Mathematics in single projects.

**Project Components:**
- Complex system design and drawing
- Real-life object or prototype creation
- Integration with other subjects
- Comprehensive documentation
- Final presentation and evaluation

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.

**Projection:** The representation of three-dimensional objects on a two-dimensional surface
**Surface Development:** The process of unfolding a three-dimensional surface into a two-dimensional pattern
**Vector Geometry:** The study of geometric objects using vector methods
**Intersection of Solids:** The study of where three-dimensional objects meet and overlap
**Machine Drawing:** Technical drawing of mechanical components and systems
**Power Transmission:** The transfer of mechanical power from one location to another
