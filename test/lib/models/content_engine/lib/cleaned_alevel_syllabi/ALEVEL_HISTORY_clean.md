# History - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** History  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level History syllabus aims to broaden learners' understanding of political, social, and economic developments in history from the earliest times to the present. It equips learners with the ability to evaluate information and critically analyse historical events, develop skills in gathering information and expressing historical ideas coherently and logically, enable learners to connect historical events to contemporary contexts, encourage historical research and the use of diverse source materials, stimulate critical thinking and discussion through case studies and relevant examples, and bridge the gap between the Lower Secondary Curriculum and courses offered in higher education institutions. The course emphasizes:

- **Historical Analysis:** Understanding of African and world history developments
- **Critical Thinking:** Evaluation of historical sources and evidence
- **Research Skills:** Gathering, analysing, and presenting historical information
- **Contemporary Connections:** Linking past events to present-day issues
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Historical consciousness, national unity, and social responsibility

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching History at Advanced Level

The Advanced Level History Syllabus aims to:

1. **Historical Literacy**
   - Broaden learners' understanding of political, social, and economic developments in history from the earliest times to the present
   - Equip learners with the ability to evaluate information and critically analyse historical events
   - Develop learners' skills in gathering information and expressing historical ideas coherently and logically

2. **Analytical Skills**
   - Enable learners to connect historical events to contemporary contexts
   - Encourage historical research and the use of diverse source materials
   - Stimulate critical thinking and discussion through case studies and relevant examples

3. **Contemporary Relevance**
   - Bridge the gap between the Lower Secondary Curriculum and courses offered in higher education institutions
   - Prepare learners for further studies and careers requiring historical knowledge and analytical skills
   - Foster understanding of how historical developments shape current global and national issues

4. **Career Development**
   - Prepare learners for further studies in history, education, law, journalism, international relations, and related fields
   - Develop transferable skills applicable in various professional contexts
   - Enable application of historical knowledge and skills in real-world contexts

### Generic Skills Development

The syllabus integrates the following generic skills:

- **Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving**
  - Planning and carrying out investigations
  - Sorting and analysing information
  - Identifying problems and proposing solutions
  - Predicting outcomes and making reasoned decisions

- **Co-operation and Self-Directed Learning**
  - Working effectively in diverse teams
  - Interacting effectively with others
  - Taking responsibility for own learning
  - Working independently with persistence
  - Managing goals and time

- **Creativity and Innovation**
  - Using imaginations to explore possibilities
  - Working with others to generate ideas
  - Suggesting and developing new solutions
  - Experimenting with innovative alternatives
  - Looking for patterns and making generalisations

- **Communication**
  - Listening attentively and with comprehension
  - Talking confidently and explaining ideas/opinions clearly
  - Reading and writing fluently
  - Writing and presenting coherently
  - Using a range of media to communicate ideas

- **Mathematical Computation**
  - Using numbers and measurements accurately
  - Interpreting and interrogating mathematical data
  - Using mathematics to justify and support decisions

- **Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Proficiency**
  - Using technology to create, manipulate and process information
  - Using technology to collaborate, communicate and refine work

- **Diversity and Multicultural Skills**
  - Appreciate cultural diversity
  - Respectfully responding to people of all cultures
  - Respecting positive cultural practices
  - Appreciating ethnicity as a cradle for creativity and innovation

### Cross-Cutting Issues

The syllabus addresses:

- **Environmental Awareness:** Historical environmental changes and their impact on societies
- **Health Awareness:** History of disease patterns, healthcare systems, and public health policies
- **Life Skills Development:** Historical decision-making, conflict resolution, and civic responsibility
- **Inclusivity and Mixed Abilities:** Inclusive historical education for diverse learner needs
- **Socio-economic Challenges:** Historical perspectives on development, inequality, and resource distribution
- **Citizenship and Patriotism:** National history, democratic development, and civic engagement

### Values Integration

Core values emphasized:

- Respect for humanity and the environment
- Honesty; uphold and defend the truth at all times
- Justice and fairness in dealing with others
- Hard work for self-reliance
- Integrity; moral uprightness and sound character
- Creativity and innovation
- Social responsibility
- Social harmony
- National unity
- National consciousness and patriotism

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 8 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical and practical components

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate topics through research driven by their interests, solving problems using a series of questions and scenarios
   - Enhances critical thinking, communication, and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners actively engage in hands-on experiences during research and reflect on their activities
   - Leads to the development of reflective skills

3. **Problem- and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners tackle real-world problems through research and projects
   - Develops critical thinking, social, and research skills

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Learners analyse real-world scenarios, developing critical thinking, analytical, and research skills through discussion and examination

5. **Discovery Learning**
   - Learners construct knowledge through active participation, exploration, and inquiry
   - Encourages critical thinking, questioning, and hypothesis development during research

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: The Social and Economic Systems in the Pre-Colonial Institutions in Africa (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates pre-colonial social and economic systems by analysing their structures and how they can be used to address current societal challenges.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Assess the importance of the clan in pre-colonial societies as the foundation of shared values, heritage and governance
- Analyse the role of age groups in promoting social harmony so as to understand his/her role in the community
- Evaluate the role of initiation ceremonies in instilling pride and a sense of identity to appreciate being a member of a particular society
- Analyse the role of African traditional religion in pre-colonial societies as a means of preserving traditional societal knowledge and values
- Examine the role of pre-colonial production in ensuring the survival of pre-colonial African societies and how this knowledge can be applied to address current societal challenges

#### Topic 2: The Pre-Colonial Trade Systems in Africa (28 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner evaluates pre-colonial trade systems in Africa by examining their impact to appreciate their contribution to the continent's economic development and the struggle for equality.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Describe the organisation of Trans-Saharan trade and its role in the development of African states to appreciate their contribution to the growth of modern trade systems
- Describe the organisation of the Trans-Atlantic slave trade and its impact on Africa and the world to understand the rise of African diasporas
- Analyse the process of abolition of slave trade in West Africa and its impact on African societies to appreciate the foundations of human rights and civil liberties

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 3: Islamic Revolutions in Africa (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the Islamic revolutions in Africa by examining their impact to appreciate how they have shaped cultural diversity in contemporary Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the factors that contributed to the spread of Islam in North Africa and its influence on cultural practices in modern Africa
- Explore the role of jihad movements in West Africa and their contribution to contemporary African cultural diversity
- Assess the causes and effects of the Mahdist Revolt in Sudan to appreciate the role of religion in influencing events in society

#### Topic 4: The Scramble for and Partition of Africa 1880–1914 (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses European imperialism in Africa by examining its causes, processes and impact to appreciate the contemporary African challenges.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the motives behind the 19th-century European occupation of Africa to appreciate the contemporary interests of foreign powers in Africa
- Analyse the process of European colonisation of Africa to understand how European colonialism was planted on the continent
- Evaluate the impact of European colonialism in Africa to appreciate its influence in his/her own community

#### Topic 5: Establishment of Colonial Rule in Africa (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner evaluates how European colonialism was established in Africa by examining the strategies employed by European powers to understand the diverse methods used to impose colonial rule.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate the methods used by European powers to establish colonies in Africa to understand European resolve to take over Africa so as to be able to defend its independence
- Examine the role played by colonial agents in advancing colonial interests in Africa to understand how the loss of Africa's independence was a collective effort
- Evaluate the problems faced by colonial agents in Africa to understand the challenges of African personalities who contributed to Africa's independence

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

#### Topic 6: Colonial Administration in Africa (13 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner investigates how colonies were administered by examining selected colonial administrative policies to appreciate their impact on Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the reasons behind the French administration policy in West Africa to appreciate its continued influence on African people
- Evaluate the impact of the French administration policy to understand its influence on contemporary West African societies
- Compare the French administration policy with other relevant administration policies in Africa to appreciate various administration policies in Africa today
- Analyse the factors that led to the French change of policy in West Africa to appreciate why sometimes there is need for flexibility in approach
- Analyse the African responses to colonial rule to understand their influence on the contemporary political landscape across Africa

#### Topic 7: Foreign Struggles for Economic Resources and the Unification in South Africa (18 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses Anglo-Boer relations in Southern Africa by examining their causes and impact to draw lessons that can foster unity and justice in Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the causes and effects of the Anglo-Boer conflicts between 1880 and 1902 in Southern Africa and how they promoted unity among minority groups
- Examine the impact of the Treaty of Vereeniging to understand how it gave rise to racial discrimination in Southern Africa
- Assess the significance of the 1910 Act of Union to appreciate its impact on the creation of legal structures by the 1910 Act of Union

#### Topic 8: The Growth of African Nationalism (17 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of African nationalism by analysing its growth and development to appreciate its impact on independence and post-independence struggles in Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the forms and features of African nationalism to appreciate varied African responses to European colonialism
- Examine the factors that contributed to the rise of African nationalism to understand the conditions that can cause discontent among people
- Analyse the contributions of nationalistic movements towards Africa's achievement of independence to appreciate the efforts of freedom fighters
- Evaluate the challenges of African independence struggles and reflect on the lessons for Africa's unity

### SENIOR FIVE - WORLD AFFAIRS

#### Topic 1: The French Revolution of 1789 (32 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the influence of the 1789 French Revolution by examining its causes, events and impact to appreciate how oppression and poor leadership can lead to conflicts in society.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the political and socio-economic conditions in Europe on the eve of the 1789 French Revolution to understand the conditions that can lead to a revolution in society
- Examine the causes of the 1789 French Revolution and identify conflicts in the future
- Identify the major events and progression of the 1789 French Revolution to understand how historical events unfold and evolve
- Analyse the impact of the 1789 French Revolution on France and the world to understand how people can take charge of their own destiny

#### Topic 2: The Era of Napoleon Bonaparte, 1799–1815 (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of how Napoleon Bonaparte I dominated European affairs by examining his background and policies to appreciate how leadership can shape society.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the factors that contributed to the rise of Napoleon I of France to appreciate how his leadership was influential on society
- Assess Napoleon Bonaparte's policies to appreciate the lessons that modern leaders in Africa can draw from his leadership
- Evaluate the factors leading to the end of the Napoleonic era in 1815 and their implications for ending aggressive regimes in modern times

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: The Growth of Pan-Africanism (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of the influence of the Pan-African movement on the liberation of Blacks to appreciate the need for a united and developed Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the origins of Pan-Africanism, its key events and figures to appreciate its foundation of African independence struggles
- Evaluate the factors for the rise of Pan-Africanism and present their findings to class to understand its impact on the need for African freedom, equality and fair world
- Analyse the challenges to the growth of Pan-Africanism to appreciate the challenges of modern African states
- Examine the impact of Pan-Africanism to appreciate its role in promoting unity, independence and social justice across Africa and beyond

#### Topic 2: Post-Colonial Socio-Economic Developments in East Africa (9 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of post-colonial socio-economic developments in Africa by examining the trade unions and the cooperative movement in East Africa to appreciate the need to address workers' welfare and promote development.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the evolution of trade unions to understand the beginning of worker's organisations in East Africa and take note of their role in the fight for workers' rights
- Evaluate the role of trade unions in East Africa to appreciate their contribution to the fight for workers' rights
- Analyse the history of the cooperative movement in East Africa to understand the beginning of farmer's collective efforts to uplift themselves in East Africa
- Examine the role of the cooperative movement in East Africa to understand how best trade unions and the cooperative movement can be managed today in socio-economic development

#### Topic 3: Regional Economic Groupings and International Relations (17 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of the need to address common challenges by analysing the activities of selected regional organisations and economic groupings to appreciate regional integration in Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) to understand its role in promoting the sovereignty and independence of member states
- Assess the role of the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA) to appreciate its impact on regional trade and economic integration among central African states
- Analyse the role of the Southern African Development Community (SADC) to understand its role in promoting regional trade and economic development of southern African states
- Evaluate the role of the Arab Maghreb Union (AMU) to appreciate its impact on fostering regional trade, economic integration and development among its member states

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 2

#### Topic 4: Sovereignty and Constitutionalism in East Africa (17 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of the concepts of sovereignty and constitutionalism by examining the role of parliament, the press and media to appreciate their contribution to democracy, good governance and respect for human rights in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the concepts of sovereignty and constitutionalism to understand their rights, responsibilities and their limits
- Explain how parliament represents the will of the people and reflects their voice in decision-making to promote inclusive governance
- Analyse the representation and respect of the rights of minority groups in parliament so as to appreciate the need to respect the rights of others
- Analyse the role of the press and media in the constitutional development of East Africa to appreciate its role in promoting good governance

#### Topic 5: Post-Independence Ideologies in East Africa (13 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner evaluates the efforts of the early post-independence leaders by examining the ideologies they adopted to appreciate their contribution to the transformation of people.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse Nyerere's Ujamaa policy to appreciate its contribution to the rural development initiative, unity and patriotism
- Evaluate Kenyatta's Harambee philosophy to appreciate the need for self-reliance strategies for growth and development
- Examine the activities of Asians in post-colonial East Africa to appreciate the value of inclusivity as well as their contribution to the development of East Africa

#### Topic 6: Separatism and Ethnic Nationalism in Africa (18 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of the turbulent history of Africa by analysing selected case studies of civil wars in Africa to appreciate the need for peaceful coexistence.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the causes and effects of the 1955–1972 and 1983–2005 civil wars in Sudan to appreciate the importance of inclusive government, social cohesion and the value of sharing resources equally
- Analyse the causes and outcomes of the 1990–94 Rwandan Civil War to promote peace-building initiatives and fight against ethnic divisions
- Analyse the causes and consequences of the 1960–64 Katanga Secession to understand our own resource sharing-related challenges
- Evaluate the causes and consequences of the Biafran Civil War of 1970 in Nigeria to strengthen the peace-building initiatives among the African states

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 3

#### Topic 7: Nationalisation of Education in Uganda (30 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates efforts to reform colonial education in Uganda by analysing the different education policies of the Ugandan government since independence to address the needs and aspirations of Ugandans.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the problems of colonial education in Uganda to appreciate the efforts taken to reform post-independence education systems in Uganda
- Analyse the role of education in the modernisation of Uganda to appreciate the country's effort to build a progressive society
- Evaluate the efforts that have been taken to Africanise the curriculum to appreciate efforts to align education with the country's development agenda and the global job market demands
- Evaluate one of the education policies in Uganda to appreciate how it has addressed education needs of the country

#### Topic 8: Challenges to Africa's Growth and Development (18 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of Africa's current position in terms of development by examining the key challenges facing the continent since independence to contribute to solutions to these challenges.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the reasons for Africa's huge debt burden as well as its effects to understand its impact on Africa's development and how it has caused neo-colonialism
- Investigate the reasons for the prevalence of corruption in Africa as well as its effects on Africa's development so as to be able to build an honest, transparent and accountable society
- Analyse the causes and effects of unemployment in Africa to understand how to live an independent self-sustaining life

### SENIOR SIX - WORLD AFFAIRS

#### Topic 1: The Eastern Question 1815–1913 (25 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of the interests of major powers in the Balkans by analysing the conflicts in Eastern Europe to appreciate how foreign interests can result in conflicts.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the factors behind the rise of the Ottoman Empire and its impact on Europe to understand the challenges related to state formation and the consolidation
- Examine the challenges posed by the 1831–1841 Syrian Question on Europe to understand how conflicting foreign interests can hinder the building of peaceful nations
- Analyse the causes of the 1854–1856 Crimean War and its impact on Europe to understand the current geopolitical conflicts and alliances in the world
- Assess the impact of the 1878 Congress of Berlin on settling European disputes and its relevance to modern diplomatic resolutions in Africa
- Evaluate the impact of the 1908–1913 Balkan Crisis on Europe to appreciate the need for unity, patriotism and nationalism

#### Topic 2: World War I and its Aftermath (23 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses one of the international conflicts by evaluating the causes and effects of World War I to appreciate how the war has shaped world order since 1918.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the causes and results of the 1914–1918 World War I and its relevance to contemporary conflict prevention and international relations
- Evaluate the role of the Versailles Peace Treaty of 1919 to understand its impact on Europe and its role in shaping international diplomacy
- Analyse the role of the 1920–1939 League of Nations in maintaining world peace to promote the workings of international diplomacy
- Evaluate the causes and impact of the 1929–1935 World Economic Depression to understand how bad economic policies can result in economic crises

**Learning Outcomes:**

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the causes and results of the 1939–1945 World War II and its relevance to contemporary conflict prevention and international relations
- Analyse the role of the United Nations Organisation (UNO) since 1945 to understand its effectiveness in promoting global peace, security and cooperation

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., writing, reports, calculations, presentations, maps, diagrams, models, drawings, paintings), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Unlike knowledge, understanding and skills, values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Project-based learning is assessed using a rubric and an observation checklist to evaluate how well learners engage with the project, their problem-solving abilities, and the final outcomes.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Regular termly examinations or tests are no longer required
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

### General Skills in Writing History Essays

Teaching History involves fostering strong writing skills, with a focus on:

- **Research-based writing:** Guiding learners to construct evidence-supported arguments from historical research
- **Organisational skills:** Teaching learners to structure essays logically, with clear introductions, cohesive body paragraphs, and strong conclusions
- **Language and style:** Promoting clarity, precision, and an academic tone in writing
- **Self-reflection:** Encouraging learners to revise, present, and refine their work through feedback and review

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.
