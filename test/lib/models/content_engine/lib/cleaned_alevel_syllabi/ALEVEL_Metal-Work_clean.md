# Metal Work - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Metal Work  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Metal Work syllabus provides technical skills and vocational training for learners, enhancing their employability and enabling them to pursue careers in sectors such as tool making, welding, fabrication and foundry work. It promotes the acquisition of Higher-order Thinking Skills (HOTS) such as inquiry, creativity and innovation, decision-making, critical thinking and problem-solving. The course emphasizes:

- **Material Science:** Understanding physical, chemical, and mechanical properties of metals and alloys
- **Metal Fabrication:** Technical knowledge and practical skills in cutting, shaping, joining, and finishing
- **Welding Technology:** Arc welding, gas welding, brazing, and soldering techniques
- **Foundry Practices:** Metal casting, molding, and fetling processes
- **Design and Innovation:** Creative design solutions with technical precision
- **Safety Practices:** Comprehensive safety awareness and accident prevention
- **Problem-Solving:** Critical thinking and troubleshooting in metalwork contexts
- **Career Preparation:** Skills for employment and further studies in engineering and design

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Metalwork at Advanced Level

The Advanced level Metalwork curriculum aims to develop a learner with hands-on skills that can be applied in everyday life, deep understanding of various metalwork methods, safety awareness, design and innovation skills, problem-solving abilities, and preparation for further studies and employment.

1. **Technical Skills Development**
   - Hands-on skills in cutting, shaping, joining and finishing techniques
   - Deep understanding of various methods used in metalwork including casting, welding and forging
   - Technical knowledge and practical skills in metal fabrication technology
   - Skills and knowledge for pursuing further studies in engineering and design

2. **Safety and Professional Practice**
   - Safety awareness in all metalwork activities to prevent accidents and ensure safe working environments
   - Professional attitudes and work ethics in metalworking contexts
   - Understanding of industry standards and quality requirements
   - Environmental consciousness in metalwork practices

3. **Design and Innovation**
   - Design and innovation skills required in metalwork structures and products
   - Emphasis on creativity, functionality and technical precision
   - Ability to address metalwork and design challenges while emphasizing sustainability
   - Creative application of material knowledge to solve practical problems

4. **Problem-Solving and Critical Thinking**
   - Metalwork skills that involve overcoming challenges and problem-solving
   - Critical thinking skills in choosing the right materials, troubleshooting issues, and optimizing designs
   - Analytical skills for evaluating metalwork processes and outcomes
   - Decision-making abilities in complex metalwork situations

5. **Career and Employment Preparation**
   - Skills and knowledge for employment in job markets that require metalworking expertise
   - Preparation for self-employment and entrepreneurship in metalworking sectors
   - Foundation for further education in engineering and related fields
   - Understanding of career opportunities in metalworking industries

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 8 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical knowledge and practical workshop sessions

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Project and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners design and fabricate functional metal products based on specific community needs
   - Document each step from material selection to final product
   - Acquire critical thinking, analytical and problem-solving skills
   - Examples: office table frame, portable chalkboard stand, window burglar proofing, armchair frame

2. **Experimental Learning**
   - Conduct welding of different types of metals to observe effects of various welding techniques
   - Compare results in terms of strength, appearance, and ease of welding
   - Build hands-on skills through experimentation
   - Develop understanding of material properties and process outcomes

3. **Brainstorming**
   - Group discussion sessions to generate ideas for metal products solving specific community problems
   - Discuss feasibility and select best designs
   - Improve communication, critical thinking and problem-solving skills
   - Encourage creative thinking and innovation

4. **Collaborative and Group Work Learning**
   - Small groups collaboratively work on fabricating metal products requiring different skills
   - Each member responsible for specific processes: measuring, cutting, welding
   - Build effective communication and teamwork skills
   - Develop interdependence and shared responsibility

5. **Jigsaw Method**
   - Each learner researches specific aspects of metalworking (material selection, welding, fabrication, finishing)
   - Become experts in their assigned area
   - Share findings in new groups for comprehensive understanding
   - Develop research and presentation skills

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### Topic 1: Engineering Materials
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates understanding of engineering materials and their applications in metalwork.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Classify engineering materials based on their properties and applications
- Analyze the physical, chemical, and mechanical properties of metals and alloys
- Select appropriate materials for specific metalwork applications
- Apply material knowledge to solve practical metalwork challenges

**Key Areas:**
- Material classification and properties
- Metal alloys and their characteristics
- Material selection criteria
- Sustainability in material usage

---

### Topic 2: Metal Fabrication Processes (Designing, Measuring and Cutting)
**Competency:** The learner applies metal fabrication processes including designing, measuring, and cutting techniques.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply design principles in metal fabrication projects
- Use appropriate measuring tools and techniques accurately
- Demonstrate cutting techniques for different types of metals
- Apply safety procedures in fabrication processes

**Key Areas:**
- Design principles and applications
- Measurement tools and techniques
- Cutting methods and equipment
- Safety in fabrication processes

---

### Topic 3: Metal Fabrication Process (Arc Welding and Fasteners)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates skills in arc welding and the use of fasteners in metal fabrication.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply arc welding techniques for joining metals
- Select and use appropriate fasteners for metal assembly
- Demonstrate welding safety procedures
- Evaluate weld quality and perform necessary corrections

**Key Areas:**
- Arc welding principles and techniques
- Types of fasteners and their applications
- Welding safety and quality control
- Joint design and preparation

---

### Topic 4: Metal Fabrication Process (Gas Welding, Brazing and Soldering)
**Competency:** The learner applies gas welding, brazing, and soldering techniques in metal fabrication.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate gas welding techniques for metal joining
- Apply brazing and soldering processes for appropriate applications
- Select appropriate processes for different metal types and thicknesses
- Implement safety measures for gas-related processes

**Key Areas:**
- Gas welding equipment and techniques
- Brazing processes and applications
- Soldering methods and materials
- Safety in gas welding processes

---

### Topic 5: Foundry Technology
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates understanding of foundry technology and metal casting processes.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply foundry technology principles in metal casting
- Design and prepare molds for casting processes
- Demonstrate casting techniques for different metals
- Implement safety procedures in foundry operations

**Key Areas:**
- Foundry principles and processes
- Mold design and preparation
- Casting techniques and procedures
- Foundry safety and environmental considerations

---

### Topic 6: Foundry Technology (Fettling Process)
**Competency:** The learner applies fettling processes to finish cast metal products.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply fettling techniques to finish cast products
- Use appropriate tools for removing excess material
- Demonstrate surface finishing techniques
- Evaluate quality of finished cast products

**Key Areas:**
- Fettling principles and techniques
- Tools and equipment for fettling
- Surface finishing methods
- Quality control in fettling processes

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., fabricated products, projects, portfolios), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Projects and project-based learning are part and parcel of learning in the 21st century. In Metalwork, learners will be tasked to identify a community problem, conduct a needs analysis, sketch, design and fabricate a product with metalworking skills to address the identified needs. All stages of the process will be documented in a portfolio, which will be subjected to assessment.

**Project Components:**
- Community problem identification
- Needs analysis and research
- Design and sketching
- Fabrication process
- Documentation and portfolio
- Final product evaluation

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.

**Arc Welding:** A welding process that uses an electric arc to melt and join metals
**Gas Welding:** A welding process that uses fuel gases to melt and join metals
**Brazing:** A metal-joining process that melts filler metal but not the base metals
**Soldering:** A low-temperature metal-joining process
**Foundry:** A factory where metal casting is performed
**Fettling:** The process of cleaning and finishing cast metal products
**Fabrication:** The process of constructing metal structures by cutting, bending, and assembling
