# Local Languages Framework - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Local Languages Framework  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Local Languages Framework syllabus is designed to support the teaching and learning of ten major Ugandan languages: Ateso, Dhopadhola, Leb Acoli, Leblango, Luganda, Lugbarati, Lumasaaba, Lusoga, Runyankore-Rukiga, and Runyoro-Rutooro. It focuses on developing learners' linguistic and literary competencies through mastery of local language orthography, grammar, and literary analysis. The course emphasizes:

- **Linguistic Competence:** Understanding and applying local language orthography and grammar
- **Literary Competence:** Interpreting, creating, and performing literary works
- **Cultural Appreciation:** Understanding cultural heritage through language study
- **Communication Skills:** Effective communication in local languages
- **Research Skills:** Finding and analyzing information in local languages
- **Creative Writing:** Composing original works in local languages
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Cultural preservation, national unity, and linguistic diversity

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Local Languages at Advanced Level

Native languages have been the greatest engine for development across the world. Uganda is a multilingual nation where indigenous languages are pivotal for individual, sub-regional as well as national growth and development. Teaching an indigenous language promotes national and cultural patriotism, hence providing avenues for peaceful co-existence in multilingual societies.

The rationale includes:

1. **Cultural Preservation and National Unity**
   - Teaching indigenous languages promotes national and cultural patriotism
   - Provides avenues for peaceful co-existence in multilingual societies
   - Harnesses indigenous knowledge through local language education
   - Engineers personal and national development

2. **Communication and Development**
   - Enables communication of socio-economic, political, scientific, and technological ideas
   - Enhances hands-on skills that promote sustainable development
   - Facilitates interdisciplinary concept application in simple ways
   - Promotes effective communication in diverse contexts

3. **Educational and Academic Benefits**
   - Provides foundation for further language studies
   - Develops analytical and critical thinking skills
   - Enhances understanding of linguistic structures and patterns
   - Prepares learners for careers in linguistics, translation, and cultural studies

4. **Cultural Identity and Heritage**
   - Preserves and promotes Uganda's linguistic heritage
   - Strengthens cultural identity and pride
   - Documents and transmits indigenous knowledge
   - Promotes intercultural understanding and respect

### Generic Skills Development

The syllabus integrates the following generic skills:

- **Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving**
  - Planning and carrying out investigations
  - Sorting and analysing information
  - Identifying problems and proposing solutions
  - Predicting outcomes and making reasoned decisions
  - Evaluating different solutions

- **Co-operation and Self-Directed Learning**
  - Working effectively in diverse teams
  - Interacting effectively with others
  - Taking responsibility for own learning
  - Working independently with persistence
  - Managing goals and time

- **Creativity and Innovation**
  - Using imaginations to explore possibilities
  - Working with others to generate ideas
  - Suggesting and developing new solutions
  - Experimenting with innovative alternatives
  - Looking for patterns and making generalisation

- **Communication**
  - Listening attentively and with comprehension
  - Talking confidently and explaining ideas clearly
  - Reading accurately and fluently
  - Writing and presenting information coherently
  - Using a range of media to communicate ideas

- **Mathematical Computation**
  - Using numbers and measurements accurately
  - Interpreting and interrogating mathematical data
  - Using mathematics to justify and support decisions

- **Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Proficiency**
  - Using technology to create, manipulate and process information
  - Using technology to collaborate, communicate and refine work

- **Diversity and Multicultural Skills**
  - Appreciate cultural diversity
  - Respectfully responding to people of all cultures
  - Respecting positive cultural practices
  - Appreciating ethnicity as a cradle for creativity and innovation

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 10 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across linguistic and literary competencies

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate through research directed by their interest
   - Solve problems through series of questions and scenarios
   - Enhances critical thinking, communication and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners actively participate in hands-on experiences during project activities
   - Learn by reflecting upon what they are doing
   - Leads to development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners find solutions to problems through their experience in research and projects
   - Develops critical thinking, social and research skills

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Learners refer to real-world scenarios to discuss and analyse them
   - Enables them to develop critical thinking, analytical and research skills

5. **Discovery Learning**
   - Learners construct their own knowledge through active participation, exploration and inquiry
   - Encourages them to critically think, ask questions and hypothesise through research

### Government Policy Guidelines

The syllabus aligns with Government White Paper on Education (1992) provisions:

- **Section 31:** Recognizes challenges of language diversity in achieving universal education and national unity
- **Section 32:** Endorses development of African languages as national media of communication and instruction
- **Section 33:** Acknowledges children's capacity to learn multiple languages effectively
- **Section 34:** Prevents development of language policy based on emotionalism and sectarianism

**Policy Implementation:** From senior one, students are required to take English, area language, and another Ugandan or foreign language, providing opportunity to study mother tongue and other indigenous languages.

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: The Local Language Orthography (6 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of Local Language letters of the alphabet, sounds, and word boundaries by critiquing various texts for effective communication in reading and writing.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Use Local Language letters of the alphabet and sounds to write words, phrases, clauses, and sentences in contexts
- Cooperate in reading contexts by identifying, ordering and reciting letters of alphabet and corresponding sounds
- Categorize letters of the alphabet into vowels, consonants and semi-vowels
- Articulate local language sounds in different words, phrases and sentences
- Produce Local Language texts using standard orthography rules

**Sub-topics:**
- Local Language Alphabet
- Local Language Sounds
- Word Boundaries

#### Topic 2: Grammar (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the grammatical structures in Local Language discourse to discover meanings in language use for effective communication.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Form nouns, pronouns and adjectives in writing texts about people, places and colours in their community
- Form verbs and adverbs to describe actions and qualities in various contexts
- Use conjunctions and prepositions/postpositions to connect ideas and show relationships
- Apply punctuation marks, sentences, and interjections appropriately in written communication

**Sub-topics:**
- Nouns, Pronouns and Adjectives
- Verbs and Adverbs
- Conjunctions and Prepositions/Postpositions
- Punctuation Marks, Sentences, and Interjections

#### Topic 3: Finding Information (12 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner develops skills in locating, organizing, and summarizing information from various sources for academic and practical purposes.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Develop effective note-taking skills from oral and written sources
- Create organized notes that capture essential information
- Write comprehensive summaries that maintain key information and meaning
- Apply information gathering techniques for research and study

**Sub-topics:**
- Note-taking
- Note-making
- Summary Writing

#### Topic 4: Prose (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses and creates different types of prose works for effective communication and literary expression.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyze expository prose to understand informative writing techniques
- Develop argumentative prose skills for persuasive communication
- Apply various prose structures and styles in writing
- Create original prose works for different purposes and audiences

**Sub-topics:**
- Expository Prose
- Argumentative Prose

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., writing, presentations, performances, projects), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Projects and project-based learning are part of learning in the 21st century. The learner is required to carry out a project as part of the continuous assessment activity, which will contribute to End-of-Cycle Assessment. This project promotes academic research and the development of writing skills on a topic of the learner's choice.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.

**Orthography:** The conventional spelling system of a language

**Phonetics:** The study and classification of speech sounds

**Morphology:** The study of word formation and structure

**Syntax:** The arrangement of words and phrases to create well-formed sentences

**Semantics:** The study of meaning in language

**Pragmatics:** The study of how context contributes to meaning
