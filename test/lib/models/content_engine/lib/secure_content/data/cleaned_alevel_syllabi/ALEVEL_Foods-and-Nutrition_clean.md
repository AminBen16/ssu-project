# Food and Nutrition - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Food and Nutrition  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Food and Nutrition syllabus equips learners with skills in meal management, nutrition health, and food value addition, emphasizing the use of locally available materials to create products that support import substitution and everyday use. The course fosters the development of Higher-Order Thinking Skills (HOTS) such as inquiry, creativity, innovation, decision-making, critical thinking, and problem-solving through learner-centred pedagogies and hands-on experiences in real-life situations. The course emphasizes:

- **Nutritional Science:** Understanding nutrients, metabolism, and nutritional requirements across life stages
- **Food Production:** Kitchen operations, food processing, and scientific principles in food production
- **Culinary Skills:** Meal planning, preparation, and advanced cooking techniques
- **Food Safety and Preservation:** Ensuring food safety, processing, and preservation methods
- **Health and Wellness:** Nutrition in rehabilitation and different life stages
- **Environmental Sustainability:** Food systems and environmental considerations
- **Entrepreneurial Skills:** Resource management and value addition
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Health consciousness, sustainability, and social responsibility

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Food and Nutrition at Advanced Level

The competency-based curriculum aims to produce graduates with employable skills in nutritional health management, meal management and food value addition, addressing nutritional challenges and food security. The syllabus recognizes diverse learner abilities and styles, promoting inclusive participation of all groups, including learners with special needs, girls, and boys.

1. **Health and Nutrition Development**
   - Equip learners with skills in meal management and nutrition health
   - Address nutritional challenges and food security issues
   - Promote understanding of nutrition in different life stages
   - Develop skills in nutrition rehabilitation

2. **Food Technology and Innovation**
   - Provide skills in food value addition using locally available materials
   - Support import substitution through local food production
   - Develop understanding of food processing and preservation
   - Foster innovation in food production techniques

3. **Scientific and Technical Skills**
   - Apply scientific principles in food production
   - Understand heat and thermodynamics in food production
   - Study absorption and metabolism of nutrients
   - Analyze food additives and their effects

4. **Career and Entrepreneurship Development**
   - Prepare learners for careers in food and nutrition industries
   - Develop resource management skills
   - Create pathways for entrepreneurship in food sector
   - Build skills for confectionery and bakery industries

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 9 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical knowledge and practical applications

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate through research directed by their interest
   - Solve problems through series of questions and scenarios
   - Enhances critical thinking, communication and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners actively participate in hands-on experiences and reflect on them
   - Emphasizes learning through doing, applying concepts in real-world situations
   - Leads to development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners find solutions to problems through their experience in research and projects
   - Develops critical thinking, social and research skills
   - Encourages real-life application of knowledge

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Learners refer to real-world scenarios to discuss and analyse them
   - Enables them to develop critical thinking, analytical and research skills

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: The Kitchen: Food Production and Processing Unit
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates understanding of kitchen operations and food processing techniques for effective food production.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Apply food processing techniques for quality food production
- Demonstrate proper kitchen management and organization
- Implement safety and hygiene standards in kitchen operations

#### Topic 2: Nutrients
**Competency:** The learner analyzes different nutrients and their functions in human health and nutrition.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Classify nutrients based on their chemical composition and functions
- Analyze the role of macro and micronutrients in human health
- Evaluate nutrient requirements for different population groups
- Apply knowledge of nutrients in meal planning and preparation

**Competency:** The learner applies principles of heat and thermodynamics in various food production processes.

**Learning Outcomes:**

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

- Analyze the role of scientific principles in food preservation
- Evaluate the impact of scientific innovations on food production
- Implement quality control measures based on scientific principles

#### Topic 6: Food Additives
**Competency:** The learner analyzes various food additives and their effects on food quality and human health.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Classify different types of food additives and their functions
- Evaluate the safety and regulatory aspects of food additives
- Analyze the effects of additives on food quality and shelf life
- Apply knowledge of additives in food production and preservation

#### Topic 7: Planning and Preparation of Meals (Part I)
**Competency:** The learner plans and prepares balanced meals considering nutritional requirements and resource availability.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply principles of meal planning for balanced nutrition
- Prepare meals considering dietary guidelines and recommendations
- Evaluate nutritional adequacy of prepared meals
- Adapt meal preparation to different dietary needs and preferences

#### Topic 8: Beverages
**Competency:** The learner prepares various types of beverages considering nutritional value and consumer preferences.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Classify different types of beverages and their nutritional properties
- Prepare beverages using appropriate techniques and ingredients
- Evaluate the role of beverages in nutrition and hydration
- Apply food safety principles in beverage preparation

**Competency:** The learner effectively manages resources in food production and nutrition programs.

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., prepared meals, projects, portfolios, presentations), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Projects and project-based learning are part and parcel of learning in the 21st century. Learners should be encouraged to develop projects based on various syllabus topics that connect to real-life situations within their local environment while ensuring alignment with learning outcomes and competencies.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

## Special Educational Needs Support

The Adapted A-level Curriculum is designed to empower all learners, including those with special educational needs (SEN), to reach their full potential and contribute meaningfully to the nation. Teachers are encouraged to design activities that support learners with special education needs through diverse and inclusive strategies:

- **Visual Aids:** Pictorial recipes, step-by-step diagrams, and video tutorials
- **Task Management:** Breaking down complex tasks into smaller manageable steps
- **Collaborative Learning:** Peer buddy systems for collaborative learning
- **Assistive Tools:** Magnifiers, text-to-speech software, talking scales, and ergonomic utensils
- **Accessible Workspaces:** Well-lit, adjustable, and distraction-free environments
- **Alternative Assessments:** Practical demonstrations in place of written tests where appropriate
- **Digital Technology:** Cooking apps and interactive digital tools for personalized learning
- **Differentiated Pedagogies:** Applied to content, learning products, and peer activities

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.

**Nutrients:** Substances that provide nourishment essential for growth and the maintenance of life

**Metabolism:** The chemical processes that occur within a living organism in order to maintain life

**Réchauffé:** Reheated leftover food

**Convenience Foods:** Commercially prepared foods designed for ease of consumption

**Food Systems:** The entire chain of activities from food production to consumption
