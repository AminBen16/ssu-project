# Subsidiary Mathematics - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Subsidiary Mathematics  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Subsidiary Mathematics syllabus is designed to build on basic mathematical concepts to deepen learners' understanding of the subject at Advanced Level and enhance their ability to construct mathematical models. The course emphasizes:

- **Data Analysis:** Statistical interpretation, probability theory, and predictive modeling
- **Mathematical Communication:** Effective presentation of mathematical concepts and solutions
- **Practical Problem-Solving Skills:** Application of mathematical techniques to real-world scenarios
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Mathematical integrity, precision, and analytical thinking

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Subsidiary Mathematics at Advanced Level

The Advanced Level Subsidiary Mathematics syllabus aims to:

1. **Foundation Building**
   - Build on basic mathematical concepts to deepen learners' understanding of the subject at Advanced Level
   - Equip learners to use mathematics as a clear and effective means of communication, emphasizing precision and logical thought

2. **Practical Application**
   - Inspire learners to develop a positive attitude towards mathematical problem-solving and an appreciation of logical reasoning
   - Empower learners to present and interpret mathematical information in diagrammatic, tabular, and graphic forms, preparing them for further education, vocational application, and informed decision-making

3. **Analytical Development**
   - Develop logical reasoning and problem-solving skills, fostering confidence in addressing unfamiliar real-life situations
   - Promote acquisition of Higher-Order Thinking Skills (HOTS) such as inquiry, creativity and innovation, decision-making, critical thinking and problem-solving

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 6 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical and practical components

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners are encouraged to investigate through research directed by their interest
   - Problem-solving through questions and scenarios
   - Enhancement of critical thinking, communication and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners actively participate in hands-on experiences during research
   - Learning through reflecting upon what they are doing
   - Development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners find solutions to problems through their experience in research and projects
   - Development of critical thinking, social, and research skills

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Learners refer to real-world scenarios to discuss and analyse them
   - Development of critical thinking, analytical, and research skills

5. **Discovery Learning**
   - Learners construct their own knowledge through active participation
   - Encouragement to critically think, ask questions and hypothesise through research

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: Matrices (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies matrices to represent data for solving real-world problems.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Present data from real life scenarios using matrices for informed decision making
- Solve real life problems like resource allocation, financial management using matrices

#### Topic 2: Quadratics (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies concepts of quadratic equations to solve real-life problems in different contexts.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the sum and product relationship between the sum and product of the roots of quadratic equations in expressions
- Solve real-life problems such as maximizing profits, minimizing losses using concepts of quadratic equations

#### Topic 3: Descriptive Statistics (30 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses statistical measures to effectively interpret data from real-life contexts enabling them to make informed decisions.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine measures of relative position and data variability to make informed decisions in real-life contexts
- Apply measures of dispersion to predict trends and make informed decisions in real-life contexts

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 4: Numerical Concepts (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies indices and logarithms to solve societal problems involving exponential relationships and scaling in various fields.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Solve real-world scenarios using indices and relate them to logarithms
- Interpret the solutions of logarithmic equations in linear form to address real-world contexts

#### Topic 5: Series (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies principles and techniques of series to analyse trends and business problems to enhance decision-making skills.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Distinguish between Arithmetic and Geometric Progressions by Identification, generation of business sequences and number of related and financial terms in a sequence
- Analyse business-related problems using arithmetic and geometric progressions in real-life

#### Topic 6: Permutations and Combinations (18 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies knowledge of permutations and combinations to solve problems related to counting, arranging, and selecting items in different fields.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Employ the knowledge of basic principles of counting in situations like sports team line-ups, library book displays, and line management during meal times to make informed decisions
- Apply the knowledge of permutations in arrangement of items in specific order
- Analyse the impact of different constraints in selection of items for effective organization of events and activities

#### Topic 7: Time Series Analysis (16 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the effectiveness of moving averages and index numbers on prediction of trends of events and their impact on policy decisions.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Contrast between odd and even number of moving points to plot features of odd-point and even-point moving totals and averages from real life situations
- Predict trends of events from graphs for informed decisions using real life scenarios
- Evaluate index numbers and make analysis to inform the placement of priorities in real life

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

#### Topic 8: Scatter Diagrams and Correlations (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner interprets data from real life scenarios to understand relationships between two variables in various contexts for decision making and prediction.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Interpret scatter graphs to visualise relationships and trends using real-world data from scientific experiments, economics, geography for prediction or decision making
- Analyse the type and strength of correlation between two variables for prediction and decision making

#### Topic 9: Vectors (23 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses vector concepts and operations to solve mathematical and physical problems in 2D spaces in various fields.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Perform operations to determine magnitude and direction in 2D spaces in real-world situations
- Analyses the dot product (scalar product) and angle between two vectors to solve problems in real-world contexts such as physics, engineering, and navigation

#### Topic 10: Trigonometry (29 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses trigonometric concepts to solve real-world problems in various fields.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Use trigonometric ratios and their reciprocals to solve right angled triangle problems in real-life contexts such as construction and navigation
- Apply trigonometric identities to solve practical problems in fields such as physics and engineering
- Solve quadratic trigonometric equations to sketching of sin, cos and tanθ graphs and how their properties assist in solving real-world problems in contexts like engineering and architecture

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 1

#### Topic 11: Probability Theory (32 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies the knowledge of probability theory in predictions and decision-making processes.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Estimate the chances of occurrence of two related events for prediction and decision making in real life situations
- Evaluate conditional probabilities for two related events to predict outcomes and make reasoned decisions

#### Topic 12: Differentiation (40 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies differentiation techniques in optimisation and analysing rates of change to interpret their significance in real life.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explore the gradient of a curve at a point to deduce the derivative of a function in analysing real-world problems
- Formulate functions from real life situations for optimisation
- Apply the knowledge of differentiation concepts to solve real-world problems to make informed decisions

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 2

#### Topic 13: Integration (36 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies integration techniques to solve problems involving areas, accumulation of quantities and other real-world applications.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Relate integration and differentiation to integrate definite and infinite integrals including natural logarithms in real-world contexts
- Apply integration concepts to determine areas under curve, velocity and displacement functions in real-world scenarios

#### Topic 14: Random Variables (36 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses concepts of random variables to model real-world situations in various contexts for informed decision making.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Utilise the concept of discrete random variables to solve real-world problems such as quality control and game theory
- Employ the concept of continuous random variables to solve real-world problems in contexts

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 3

#### Topic 15: Probability Distributions (36 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner interprets binomial and normal distributions for analysis of data patterns and trends in real-world contexts.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Interpret a binomial distribution to solve real-world problems involving success-failure scenarios
- Interpret the normal distribution to solve real-world problems involving normally distributed data
- Apply the normal distribution function to solve societal problems such as data visualization, economic forecasting, analysing communication networks, etc.

#### Topic 16: Differential Equations (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses problems involving rates of change for prediction of alterations in various natural and social phenomena over a period of time.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Formulate differential equations using various real-life scenarios to make informed decisions
- Analyse real world situations using differential equation techniques
- Analyse applications of differential equations in real world and present to the whole class

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – watching learners working (good for assessing skills, values and attitudes)
2. **Conversation** – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and understanding)
3. **Product** – appraising learner's work (writing, report, calculation, presentation, model, drawing, etc.)

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, information from any one form can be checked against the other two forms of assessment for uniformity and reliability.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are assessed using:
- Observation checklists
- Scoring rubrics
- Portfolio assessment
- Project evaluation

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes are assessed over time through:
- Observation of behaviour
- Interactions with learners
- Long-term monitoring of personal development
- Anecdotal records

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

- Assessed using rubrics and observation checklists
- Focus on sustained inquiry and authentic challenges
- Evaluation of driving questions and solutions

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- End-of-topic scenario-based tasks (Activities of Integration)
- End of cycle assessment for certification

### Record-Keeping

Accurate records maintained including:
- Observation checklists
- Rubrics and scoring grids
- Learner portfolios
- Anecdotal notes
- Progress tracking files

## Mathematical Requirements

### Arithmetic Skills

- Recognition and use of expressions in decimal and standard form (scientific)
- Use of appropriate calculating aids (electronic calculator)
- Finding arithmetic means, powers, sines, cosines, tangents, exponentials and logarithms
- Taking account of accuracy in experimental work
- Making approximate evaluations of numerical expressions

### Algebra Skills

- Changing the subject of an equation
- Solving simple algebraic equations
- Substituting physical quantities into equations using consistent units
- Formulating algebraic equations as mathematical models
- Using logarithmic forms of expressions
- Manipulating and solving equations involving logarithmic and exponential functions

### Geometry and Trigonometry

- Calculating areas of triangles, circumference and area of circles, areas and volumes
- Using Pythagoras' theorem, similarity of triangles, angle sum of a triangle
- Using sines, cosines and tangents for common angles
- Understanding relationship between degrees and radians
- Using trigonometric relationships for triangles

### Vector Skills

- Finding the resultant of two coplanar vectors
- Obtaining expressions for components of a vector in perpendicular directions
- Recognizing situations where vector resolution is appropriate

### Graph Skills

- Translating information between graphical, numerical, algebraic and verbal forms
- Selecting appropriate variables and scales for graph plotting
- Determining slope, intercept and intersection for linear graphs
- Using logarithmic plots to test exponential and power law variations
- Understanding and using the slope of a tangent to a curve
- Understanding and using the area below a curve where the area has physical significance

### Statistical Skills

- Organising and presenting statistical data
- Computing measures of central tendency and dispersion
- Using probability distributions
- Applying sampling methods
- Interpreting statistical results

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** One in which learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit an individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of judging a learner's performance, by interpreting responses to tasks, in order to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills which are deployed in all subjects, and which enhance the learning of those subjects. These skills also equip young people for work and for life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences which allows each student to feel confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn at his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement which specifies what the learner should know, understand, or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired by following the programme of study in a particular learning area; enables a learner to apply the knowledge and understanding of the learning area.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity which gives a learner the opportunity to show the extent to which they have achieved the Learning Outcomes.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An aspect of the normal teaching and learning process that will enable a formative assessment to be made.
