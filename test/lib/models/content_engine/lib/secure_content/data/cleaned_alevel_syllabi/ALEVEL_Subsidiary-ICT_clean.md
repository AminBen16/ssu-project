# Subsidiary Information and Communication Technology - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Subsidiary Information and Communication Technology (Sub-ICT)  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Subsidiary Information and Communications Technology (SUB-ICT) syllabus focuses on the practical application of technological knowledge and skills, empowering learners to tackle real-world challenges in their communities. It emphasizes effective communication, data management, ethical technology use, and emerging technologies, nurturing innovation and adaptability in a rapidly evolving digital landscape. The syllabus promotes the development of higher-order thinking skills, including enquiry, creativity, innovation, decision-making, critical thinking, and problem-solving. It advocates for learner-centred pedagogies that provide hands-on experience while accommodating diverse abilities and learning styles. The course emphasizes:

- **Digital Literacy:** Essential ICT skills for productivity and digital communication
- **Multimedia Production:** Creative digital content creation and management
- **Cybersecurity:** Ethical computing and digital safety practices
- **Data Management:** Computational thinking and data analysis skills
- **Emerging Technologies:** Artificial intelligence and related technologies
- **Practical Applications:** Real-world problem-solving through ICT projects
- **Ethical Technology Use:** Responsible digital citizenship and privacy protection
- **Innovation and Collaboration:** Digital creativity and teamwork skills

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Subsidiary ICT at Advanced Level

The Subsidiary ICT syllabus aims to enhance ICT literacy and practical skills for effective application in various contexts, foster innovation and collaboration through digital creativity and teamwork, and promote ethical technology use by emphasizing cybersecurity, privacy, and adherence to ICT standards.

1. **Digital Literacy and Productivity**
   - Enhance ICT literacy and practical skills for effective application in various contexts
   - Develop skills in using various ICT tools to enhance productivity
   - Demonstrate the ability to effectively perform tasks using digital communication, organization, and collaboration tools
   - Diagnose and resolve basic hardware and software issues to meet specific user needs efficiently
   - Ensure system security and performance through the application of maintenance practices

2. **Multimedia and Digital Content Creation**
   - Foster innovation and collaboration through digital creativity and teamwork
   - Cultivate creativity and technical skills in multimedia production
   - Design and produce engaging digital content, considering audience needs and design principles
   - Create, design, and manage multimedia content using tools for various platforms
   - Address real-world challenges through dynamic digital storytelling and content creation

3. **Cybersecurity and Ethical Computing**
   - Promote ethical technology use by emphasizing cybersecurity, privacy, and adherence to ICT standards
   - Understand cybersecurity principles, including data protection, threat management, and safe digital practices
   - Demonstrate ethical behaviour in ICT usage, respecting privacy, intellectual property, and responsible computing
   - Develop strategies to identify and mitigate cyber threats, promoting online safety for local and global contexts

4. **Data Management and Computational Thinking**
   - Build knowledge and skills in organising, analysing, and managing data
   - Demonstrate computational thinking by solving problems logically and innovatively
   - Explore the role of data and computing in decision-making and innovation and address societal needs
   - Apply data analysis techniques to solve real-world problems

5. **Career and Life Preparation**
   - Equip learners with essential digital skills for the modern workplace
   - Prepare learners for further education in ICT and related fields
   - Develop problem-solving skills applicable to various career paths
   - Foster adaptability in a rapidly evolving digital landscape

### Generic Skills Development

The syllabus integrates the following generic skills:

- **Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving**
  - Planning and carrying out investigations

  - Identifying problems and proposing solutions

- **Co-operation and Self-Directed Learning**

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 6 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical knowledge and practical ICT applications

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners explore ICT concepts and tools through self-directed research driven by interest
   - Solve problems by posing questions, conducting experiments, and analysing scenarios
   - Enhances critical thinking, communication, and problem-solving abilities

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners engage in hands-on ICT projects, such as building databases or creating presentations
   - Reflect on these experiences to deepen understanding
   - Fosters the development of technical skills and reflective thinking

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners address real-world ICT challenges by working on projects that require applying knowledge in practical contexts
   - Enhances critical thinking, teamwork, and research skills
   - Designs solutions and presents findings

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Learners analyse real-world ICT scenarios, such as cybersecurity threats or emerging technologies
   - Develop critical thinking and analytical skills
   - Apply theoretical knowledge to practical situations

5. **Discovery Learning**
   - Learners construct understanding of ICT concepts through active exploration and inquiry
   - Experiment with software, hardware, and emerging technologies
   - Develop problem-solving skills and hypothesise and test solutions independently

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., digital projects, presentations, databases), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Projects and project-based learning are integral to 21st-century education. In this syllabus, learners will undertake practical projects that align with the subject's focus on technology application and problem-solving. These projects promote hands-on learning, critical thinking, and the development of ICT skills. Teachers are encouraged to guide learners in selecting projects that connect to their local environment and real-world challenges.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

## Special Educational Needs Support

The Advanced Secondary Curriculum is designed to empower all learners, including those with Special Educational Needs (SEN), to achieve their full potential and make meaningful contributions to society. By incorporating inclusive strategies, the curriculum ensures equitable access to high-quality learning opportunities while maintaining rigorous academic standards. A key emphasis is placed on creating an inclusive learning environment that meets the diverse needs of Special Education Needs (SEN) learners.

- **Adaptive Technologies:** Screen readers, voice recognition software, alternative input devices
- **Visual Aids:** Enhanced contrast displays, magnification tools, tactile indicators
- **Assistive Software:** Text-to-speech, speech-to-text, predictive text applications
- **Differentiated Instruction:** Tailored approaches for different learning styles and abilities
- **Inclusive Learning Environment:** Accessible workstations and adaptive equipment
- **Peer Support:** Collaborative learning and mentorship programs

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.

**ICT:** Information and Communications Technology
**Digital Literacy:** The ability to use digital technology, communication tools, or networks to locate, evaluate, use, and create information
**Cybersecurity:** The practice of protecting systems, networks, and programs from digital attacks
**Artificial Intelligence:** The simulation of human intelligence in machines programmed to think and learn
**Multimedia:** The use of computers to present text, graphics, video, animation, and sound in an integrated way
