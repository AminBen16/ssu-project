# Christian Religious Education - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Christian Religious Education  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Christian Religious Education syllabus is designed to develop spiritual growth, compassion, and respect for one another. It instils values like justice and integrity, fosters empathy and tolerance, and promotes understanding of various beliefs. This holistic approach prepares individuals to navigate life with wisdom, compassion, and ethical responsibility, rooted in biblical teachings. The course emphasizes:

- **Biblical Knowledge:** Understanding of Old and New Testament teachings and their application
- **Moral Development:** Cultivation of Christian virtues and ethical decision-making
- **Contemporary Application:** Addressing modern social issues through biblical perspectives
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Christian integrity, justice, compassion, and social responsibility

---

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Christian Religious Education at A-Level

The Advanced Level CRE syllabus aims to enable learners to:

1. **Spiritual Development**
   - Develop an awareness and knowledge of God's presence and purpose in the world as revealed through His creation, the Bible, the Christian community, the life and teaching of Jesus Christ and the Holy Spirit living in the church today
   - Cultivate Christian virtues and morals of love, joy, peace, service, honesty, empathy, sharing, tolerance and justice
   - Live a committed Christian life following in the footsteps of Jesus Christ

2. **Biblical Understanding**
   - Acquire knowledge of the teachings of the Bible and apply it in their lives or churches today
   - Appreciate the common elements in traditional religion, other religions and Christian beliefs

3. **Contemporary Application**
   - Understand that marriage is a sacred institution that elevates the roles of men and women and emphasises the responsibilities of bearing and nurturing children
   - Develop communication and conflict resolution skills and values such as commitment and responsibility to build and maintain healthy, respectful relationships
   - Understand the relationship between work, leisure, and money across different contexts through examining current economic activities and ethical practices

4. **Social Responsibility**
   - Gain a comprehensive understanding of law and order, recognizing their importance in maintaining societal harmony and justice
   - Gain knowledge about rights, responsibilities, and the roles of community leaders
   - Develop critical thinking and ethical reasoning skills through analysing real-life scenarios and case studies

### Generic Skills Development

The syllabus integrates the following generic skills:

- **Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving**
  - Planning and carrying out investigations
  - Sorting and analysing information
  - Identifying problems and proposing solutions
  - Predicting outcomes and making reasoned decisions

- **Co-operation and Self-Directed Learning**
  - Working with others effectively
  - Taking responsibility for own learning
  - Working independently with persistence
  - Managing goals and time

- **Creativity and Innovation**
  - Using imaginations to explore possibilities
  - Working with others to generate ideas
  - Suggesting and developing new solutions
  - Experimenting with innovative alternatives
  - Looking for patterns and making generalisations

- **Communication**
  - Listening attentively and with comprehension
  - Talking and discussing concepts
  - Reading accurately and fluently
  - Writing and presenting information coherently
  - Using a range of media to communicate ideas

- **Mathematical Computation**
  - Using numbers and measurements accurately
  - Interpreting and interrogating mathematical data
  - Using mathematics to justify and support decisions

- **Digital Literacy**
  - Using technology to create, manipulate and process information
  - Using technology to access, manage and share information

- **Diversity and Multicultural Skills**
  - Appreciating cultural diversity
  - Respectfully responding to people of all cultures
  - Respecting positive cultural practices
  - Appreciating ethnicity as a cradle for creativity and innovation

### Cross-Cutting Issues

The syllabus addresses:

- **Environmental Awareness:** Biblical stewardship of creation and environmental responsibility
- **Health Awareness:** Christian perspectives on health, healing, and well-being
- **Life Skills:** Decision-making, responsibility, ethical considerations in personal and social contexts
- **Socio-economic Challenges:** Christian approaches to poverty, wealth, work, and economic justice
- **Citizenship and Patriotism:** Christian citizenship, national unity, and social harmony

### Values Integration

Core values emphasized:

- Respect for humanity and environment
- Honesty; uphold and defend the truth at all times
- Justice and fairness in dealing with others
- Hard work for self-reliance
- Integrity; moral uprightness and sound character
- Creativity and innovation
- Social responsibility
- Social harmony
- National unity
- National consciousness and patriotism

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 9 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical and practical components

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate through research directed by their interest
   - Problem-solving through questions and scenarios
   - Enhancement of critical thinking, communication and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners actively participate in hands-on experiences during research
   - Learning through reflecting upon what they are doing
   - Development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners find solutions to problems through their experience in research and projects
   - Development of critical thinking, social and research skills

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Learners discuss and analyse real world scenarios in reference to Biblical and traditional African scenarios
   - Development of critical thinking, analytical and research skills

5. **Discovery Learning**
   - Learners construct their own knowledge through active participation, exploration and inquiry
   - Encouragement to think critically, ask questions and hypothesise through research

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: The Pentateuch (51 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of the Pentateuch by analysing the centrality of God and mankind, divine election and salvation, the significance of covenants, and the role of priests in order to guide spiritual, moral and ethical issues in modern times.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the creation accounts in Genesis 1 and 2 to understand the biblical perspective on the origin of man and the universe as desired by God
- Demonstrate appreciation for God's creation by exploring His attributes as revealed through His works in the Pentateuch, and develop a deeper reverence for Him
- Analyse the nature of man as presented in the Pentateuch by recognising the uniqueness, purpose, and shortfalls of mankind
- Analyse the theme of divine election and salvation as presented through key personalities and events in the Pentateuch, providing a foundation for evaluating modern ideas and responsibilities
- Evaluate the concept of priesthood in Israel as described in Leviticus, examining the responsibilities and significance of priests in religious and social contexts, and reflecting on their influence on ancient Israelite society and their enduring impact on society

#### Topic 2: Theocracy in Israel (21 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the Israelites' transition from migrant to settled life by focusing on the conquest, settlement and the role of judges in upholding the covenant faith and theocratic rule, in order to relate to the judicial roles in contemporary society.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate Canaanite religion and the role of judges in ancient Israel, evaluating their impact on the moral, social, and political development of the Israelite community and its relevance to contemporary society
- Evaluate the priestly leadership of Eli and his family by using biblical scriptures to examine the consequences of spiritual complacency and apply these insights to contemporary society
- Evaluate Samuel's leadership by examining his roles as a priest, prophet and judge to relate and guide modern leaders

#### Topic 3: Monarchism in Israel (45 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner develops an understanding of monarchism in ancient Israel by analysing the establishment, leadership, and impact of key monarchs, and assessing the socio-political, religious, and cultural implications for ancient Israelite society, and relates it to modern governance and leadership.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the demand for human kingship in Israel by exploring the societal, political, and religious factors that drove this shift from theocratic leadership, evaluating its impact on Israelite governance and religious identity
- Analyse the leadership styles and governance of human kings in Israel by exploring how Kings Saul, David and Solomon rose to power, analysing their achievements and failures, and relating their leadership styles to modern political leaders today
- Analyse the division of the kingdom of Israel by examining biblical scriptures to discuss the background to the division, identify key players, and analyse the historical and political context and their ability to articulate these ideas
- Evaluate the reforms enacted by kings in Israel, examining the social, political, and religious changes they implemented and assessing their impact on the development and preservation of Israelite society

#### Topic 4: Prophetism in Israel (45 Periods)
**Competency:** Learners appreciate the significance of prophetic work in ancient Israel, by examining the social, political, and economic contexts of the time, exploring the theme of the broken covenant, and analysing the general messages delivered by the selected prophets in order to build a contemporary understanding of justice, leadership, and community values.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the social, political, and economic contexts of the prophets' time, assessing their relevance and connections to contemporary issues
- Analyse the themes of the broken covenant, call for repentance, judgement and the day of the Lord, hope and individual responsibility as addressed by selected prophets, relating their messages to contemporary social, religious, economic and spiritual issues
- Synthesise the core prophetic messages of Old Testament prophets by analysing their prophetic books to identify major themes, and exploring their relevance to inform ethical, social, and spiritual issues

#### Topic 5: Wisdom Literature (18 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates appreciation of perseverance and worship in ancient Israel through applying the teachings of Job and Psalms; analysing the themes of suffering, faith, and divine justice in Job; and exploring the expressions of worship, lament, and thanksgiving in Psalms to enable people in the modern society connect these ancient insights to contemporary issues.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the themes of suffering, justice, and patience in the book of Job, relating them to contemporary challenges and discuss ways of overcoming them
- Appreciate the emotional and spiritual expressions in Psalms by analysing selected Psalms to discover the rationale of using Psalms in ancient Israel and shape contemporary worship practices and personal devotional life

#### Topic 6: The Pre-Gospel Period (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner examines the historical, cultural, and religious context of the apostolic period, by evaluating the New Testament canonisation process, and reflecting on its significance and influence on modern Christian practice.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate the link between the Old and New Testaments, focusing on the theme of Messiahship, to understand how the Old Testament foreshadows and lays the groundwork for the New Testament
- Explore the features of the early church by analysing the message of the original gospel and early worship practices to gain insight into the birth of Christianity and how it shapes modern Christian practices
- Assess the origin, development, and canonisation of the New Testament canon by examining the criteria underlying the compilation of the New Testament canon, recognising its divine inspiration to deepen reverence of the New Testament as the inspired Word of God that is relevant to the contemporary church

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 7: The Gospels (84 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of the selected themes and perspectives in the Gospels of John and Mark, recognising their unique contributions to the development of Christian faith, and applies them to address contemporary issues.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the preaching of John the Baptist as depicted in the Gospels according to Mark and John, assessing his messages of repentance and preparing the way for Jesus, and reflecting on the relevance of John the Baptist's teachings to Christians today
- Examine the different methods used by Jesus to convey his message about the kingdom of God in the Gospels through analysing parables and discourses in John and Mark, drawing their relevance to modern Christians
- Evaluate the significance of miracles and signs in the Gospel of Mark and John by analysing their implications and exploring how these events can inspire faith and hope in the contemporary world
- Analyse the instances of acceptance, controversies, and opposition that Jesus encountered in his public ministry by examining the reactions of different categories of people to Jesus' ministry, explaining the reasons why Jesus faced opposition and rejection in his ministry
- Analyse the passion, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ by examining the biblical texts in Mark to understand how these events can inspire resilience, empathy and compassion in their own lives and communities
- Investigate Jesus' reaction in the face of opposition and rejection to explain the lessons Christians can draw from people's responses and Jesus' reaction to opposition and rejection

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

#### Topic 8: The Epistles (54 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner develops an appreciation of key apostolic teachings through examining the Letters of 1 Corinthians, 1 Peter, and James, following selected themes to explore how these teachings can provide practical guidance and inspiration for addressing modern issues.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the role of the Holy Spirit as presented in the epistles through exploring themes on various aspects of the Holy Spirit in the early church and modern Christian practices and choices
- Critique the unethical behaviour of Christians in the early church by examining themes in the epistles to guide Christian behaviour today, pointing out lessons Christians can learn from the scriptures
- Appreciate the virtue of charity by analysing the teachings of charity principles in the epistles through focusing on generosity, kindness and love to inspire contemporary Christian practices and attitudes toward the needy
- Analyse the teachings on faith and perseverance in the epistle of James through exploring themes of trust, belief in God's promises, and perseverance, reflecting on how James' teachings strengthen Christian faith and practice today
- Analyse the themes of prayer and patience and the proper use of speech in the epistle of James through evaluating these teachings and how they can be applied to address contemporary issues
- Appreciate the teachings about wealth and poverty by analysing scriptures in the letter of James to gain insights on stewardship, contentment, generosity, and social justice in modern churches
- Examine the theme of holy living through analysing texts in 1 Peter to identify and discuss the teachings on righteousness and positive living among modern Christians
- Examine the teachings on suffering and endurance by analysing texts in 1 Peter to discuss the teachings on suffering, endurance and faith in adversity, relating them to modern Christians
- Analyse human relationships addressed in 1 Peter by examining the teachings on various relationships and derive their relevance to the contemporary world

#### Topic 9: Sex and Sexuality (27 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner develops an appreciation for the purpose of sex and sexuality basing on the traditional African, contemporary, and biblical dimensions in order to develop right attitudes and proper decision-making skills.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse African traditional perspectives on sex and sexuality by exploring their cultural, social, and spiritual dimensions, and reflect on their influence on modern attitudes and decision-making regarding sexuality
- Assess the modern understanding of sex and sexuality in order to develop respect and a positive attitude towards sex and sexuality
- Analyse deviant sexual practices in contemporary society to enhance an enriched understanding of the dangers of sex misuse and promote informed decision making
- Analyse the biblical teaching about sex and sexuality in order to appreciate the proper understanding of sex as a divine gift to mankind

#### Topic 10: Marriage (27 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates marriage as a sacred institution which elevates the roles of both men and women based on the traditional, contemporary and Christian dimensions thereby assigning them the vital responsibilities of bearing and nurturing children to become responsible citizens.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the traditional African perspectives on dating and courtship by evaluating the values and challenges of dating and courtship in both traditional and modern societies, and discuss aspects of traditional African courtship that are agreeable with Christianity
- Evaluate the value of bride wealth in the traditional African and contemporary society by analysing the traditional aspects of bride wealth that are agreeable with Christianity
- Examine the modern understanding of marriage in order to uphold the institution of marriage in modern society
- Analyse the biblical teaching about marriage by interpreting Biblical scriptures in order to appreciate and uphold marriage as a divine institution and suggest ways in which today's marriage challenges can be resolved using biblical marriage virtues

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 1

#### Topic 11: Family Life (27 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates family as a divine institution and a foundation of human civilisation basing on traditional, contemporary and Christian dimensions to evaluate and guide contemporary family trends.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the traditional African approaches to family life by using the internet, newspapers, local proverbs, dialogue with elders in the community or visits to cultural institutions to research and discover the meaning and values of African traditional family
- Analyse the modern trends of family life to arrive at a clear understanding of the values of the family today and the challenges they can be mitigated
- Evaluate modern parenting styles to suggest practises that can shape positive and responsible character traits among children living in a highly challenging technological environment
- Analyse the biblical teachings about family life in order to appreciate God's will in contemporary Christian families and the Christian virtues of family life

#### Topic 12: Work (33 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the divine origin of work, by examining its historical significance, and humanity's creative ability basing on traditional, contemporary and biblical teachings in order to foster a positive attitude towards work and develop the world.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explore the traditional African concept of work by using the internet, mass media or consultation with elders to research and discover the skills and ideals that can transform modern attitudes towards work
- Examine the complexities of work and vocation in contemporary society by using the internet and mass media to research and explore the meaning and understanding of work today, discussing the value of work and strategies to improve work relations
- Assess ideologies about work through analysing capitalism and socialism in order to promote fairness in the field of work
- Evaluate the standards of research behaviour and competences expected in work by analysing professional ethics in medical, legal and teaching professions in order to foster professional integrity and compliance with the laws, regulations and codes that govern professions
- Appreciate biblical teachings on work by analysing Genesis 1:28, 2:15, Exodus 20:9, 35:30-33, Jeremiah 22:13, Psalm 104:23, Proverbs 6:6-11, Mark 6:3, Luke 19:11-27, Ephesians 4:28, 2 Thessalonians 3:10 and other relevant passages to enhance their understanding of God's purpose and plan for work, and apply principles of ethics, integrity, and morality in modern workplace environments

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 2

#### Topic 13: Leisure (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of leisure in view of traditional, contemporary and biblical teachings in order to promote a positive attitude towards the use of leisure.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the understanding of leisure in traditional African society by engaging in research using the internet, mass media, and consultations with resource people and cultural institutions to discover the concept and virtues of leisure in traditional African society in order to uphold the expected morals regarding the use of leisure
- Explore the contemporary leisure industry by researching various sectors of the modern leisure industry such as sports, tourism and entertainment using online resources and print media to develop informed judgment about the current trends of leisure
- Analyse biblical teachings on leisure by discussing biblical perspectives on leisure through Bible verses such as Genesis 2:2, Exodus 20:8, Numbers 28:9-11, Ezekiel 46:3-5, Ecclesiastes 3:1-8, Mark 1:35, 2:27, 6:31, John 2:1-12, Luke 5:29 among others to identify and interpret key ideas from the verses related to leisure and develop insights into God's design for rest, relationships, and responsible leisure practices

#### Topic 14: Money and Wealth (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the acceptable means of acquiring and spending money and wealth, its impact on society in relation to traditional, contemporary and biblical teachings, in order to develop the rightful attitude towards money and wealth.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the traditional African understanding of money and wealth by investigating traditional African perspectives on money acquisition and utilisation, brainstorming the sources and utilisation of money and wealth in traditional African societies, and debating the right ways of acquiring and spending wealth in traditional African society
- Assess the sources and importance of money and wealth in contemporary society by discussing different economic activities as a source of money and wealth in today's society, brainstorming the sources and utilisation of money and wealth, and discussing the expected values regarding the acquisition and use of money and wealth to comply with honest means of earning and spending money
- Examine biblical teachings on money and wealth by analysing scriptures such as Genesis 1:28, 9:1-3, 13:2, Leviticus 26:3-13, Proverbs 10:4, 10:22, 11:28, 12:11, 15:16, 24:3-4, 30:8-9, Amos 9:13-15, Psalm 23:1, 49:8, Matthew 19:27-30, 21:12-13, Luke 21:1-4, Acts 4:34-37, 11:29-30, 18:3 and 20:34 to gain insights into responsible earning and stewardship practices
- Compare Christian perspectives on money and wealth with contemporary understandings to suggest advice to address the abuses related to money and wealth

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 3

#### Topic 15: Law, Crime and Punishment (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the importance of obedience to God's law and state laws by examining traditional African, contemporary and biblical teachings in order to cultivate harmonious relationships between man and fellow man, man and God and man and the environment.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Appreciate the purpose of the law in promoting moral uprightness by evaluating traditional and contemporary societal conduct and its impact on maintaining social order and harmony
- Analyse the causes and impact of crime in order to deduce solutions to crime and promote observance of the laws
- Evaluate the forms, purpose and determinants of punishment to create a crime-free society and promote justice and harmony in society
- Analyse the biblical teachings about law, crime and punishment in order to gain insights into God's justice and promote justice and harmony in society

#### Topic 16: The State and the Citizen (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the duties and rights of the state and citizens, basing on contemporary and biblical perspectives to create harmonious co-existence between the church and the state.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the duties and rights of the state and citizens in order to demonstrate the spirit of patriotism and responsible living
- Evaluate the relationship between the church and the state through analysing the roles of the church and the state in contemporary society in order to defend and promote a harmonious relationship between the two
- Analyse the concept of prejudice and discrimination in order to inspire and promote the spirit of togetherness and respect for one another
- Appreciate the value of unity by researching and discussing the role of ecumenical movements with reference to Uganda Joint Christian Council, and Inter-Religious Council of Uganda, National Council of Churches of Kenya and Tanzania Episcopal Conference, and discuss the strategies the church uses to promote unity and cooperation in diversity

#### Topic 17: International Order (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the causes of armed conflict with their associated problems through analysing the different international aims and objectives in order to promote harmonious international relations.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the causes of armed conflict, their impact, and modern ways of achieving effects on society by researching and finding out the causes of modern armed conflicts, just war and peace and harmony, and checking international peace dialogues for evidence of cooperation and self-directed learning
- Examine the refugee problem and internal displacement by using the internet and mass media to research and discuss the causes of the refugee displacement in crisis and internal displacement in crisis, working as a team to generate ideas, discuss the challenges and impact of refugee crisis and internal displacement in society, and propose solutions to the refugee challenges in life crisis and internal displacement, promoting peace in accordance with international standards and God's will

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – watching learners working (good for assessing skills, values and attitudes)
2. **Conversation** – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and understanding)
3. **Product** – appraising learner's work (writing, report, calculation, presentation, model, drawing, etc.)

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, information from any one can be checked against the other two forms of assessment for uniformity and reliability.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are assessed using:
- Observation checklists
- Scoring rubrics
- Portfolio assessment
- Project evaluation

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes are assessed over time through:
- Observation of behaviour
- Interactions with learners
- Long-term monitoring of personal development
- Anecdotal records

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

- Assessed using rubrics and observation checklists
- Focus on sustained inquiry and authentic challenges
- Evaluation of driving questions and solutions

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- End-of-topic scenario-based tasks (Activities of Integration)
- End of cycle assessment for certification

### Record-Keeping

Accurate records maintained including:
- Observation checklists
- Rubrics and scoring grids
- Learner portfolios
- Anecdotal notes
- Progress tracking files

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** One in which learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit an individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of judging a learner's performance, by interpreting responses to tasks, in order to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills which are deployed in all subjects, and which enhance the learning of those subjects. These skills also equip young people for work and for life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences which allows each student to feel confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn at his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement which specifies what the learner should know, understand, or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired by following the programme of study in a particular learning area; enables a learner to apply the knowledge and understanding of the learning area.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity which gives a learner the opportunity to show the extent to which they have achieved the Learning Outcomes.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An aspect of the normal teaching and learning process that will enable a formative assessment to be made.
