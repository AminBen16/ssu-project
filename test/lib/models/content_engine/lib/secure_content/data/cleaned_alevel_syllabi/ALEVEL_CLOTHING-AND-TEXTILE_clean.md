# Clothing and Textiles - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Clothing and Textiles  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Clothing and Textiles syllabus equips learners with practical skills in garment construction and textile value addition. It promotes the use of locally available raw materials to create unique, functional textiles, supporting import substitution and encouraging the production of locally made textiles for everyday use. The course emphasizes:

- **Textile Science:** Understanding fibers, yarns, and fabric construction
- **Garment Construction:** Pattern making, sewing techniques, and garment finishing
- **Design Principles:** Elements and principles of design in clothing and textiles
- **Practical Skills:** Hands-on experience in sewing, fabric decoration, and garment making
- **Entrepreneurial Skills:** Marketing, quality control, and business management
- **Cultural Heritage:** Appreciation of traditional and contemporary textile arts
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Innovation, cultural preservation, and sustainable practices

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Clothing and Textiles at Advanced Level

The Advanced Level Curriculum integrates both theoretical and practical aspects of Clothing and Textiles in a structured manner to:

1. **Personal Development**
   - Develop an appreciation of the sociological and psychological impact of Clothing and Textiles as a means of self-identification and expression
   - Highlight the importance of personal image while fostering creativity in dressing, clothing management, and care
   - Instil positive attitudes towards productive work and respect for the dignity of labour

2. **Technical Skills Development**
   - Provide a logical and sequential understanding of the fundamental processes involved in garment construction
   - Develop decision-making skills related to consumer choices and construction challenges in clothing
   - Equip individuals with essential scientific, technological, and commercial skills for self-employment

3. **Cultural and Economic Development**
   - Promote an appreciation and understanding of Uganda's cultural heritage
   - Support import substitution through local textile production
   - Encourage the production of locally made textiles for everyday use
   - Foster creativity and innovation in textile and garment design

4. **Career Preparation**
   - Prepare learners for further education in textile and fashion-related fields
   - Develop entrepreneurial skills for small business development
   - Create pathways for careers in fashion design, textile manufacturing, and related industries

### Generic Skills Development

The syllabus integrates the following generic skills:

- **Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving**
  - Planning and carrying out investigations

- **Co-operation and Self-Directed Learning**

  - Managing goals and time

- **Creativity and Innovation**
  - Using imaginations to explore possibilities
  - Working with others to generate ideas
  - Suggesting and developing new solutions
  - Experimenting with innovative alternatives
  - Looking for patterns and making generalisation

- **Communication**
  - Listening attentively and with comprehension
  - Talking confidently and explaining ideas clearly
  - Reading accurately and fluently
  - Writing and presenting information coherently
  - Using a range of media to communicate ideas

- **Mathematical Computation**
  - Using numbers and measurements accurately
  - Interpreting and interrogating mathematical data
  - Using mathematics to justify and support decisions

- **Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Proficiency**
  - Using technology to create, manipulate and process information

- **Diversity and Multicultural Skills**
  - Appreciate cultural diversity

  - Respecting positive cultural practices
  - Appreciating ethnicity as a cradle for creativity and innovation

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 9 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical knowledge and practical skills

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate through research directed by their interest
   - Solve problems through series of questions and scenarios
   - Enhances critical thinking, communication and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners actively participate in hands-on experiences and reflect on them
   - Emphasizes learning through doing, applying concepts in real-world situations
   - Leads to development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners find solutions to problems through their experience in research and projects
   - Develops critical thinking, social and research skills
   - Encourages real-life application of knowledge

4. **Case-Based Learning**
   - Learners refer to real-world scenarios to discuss and analyse them
   - Enables them to develop critical thinking, analytical and research skills

5. **Flipped Classroom**
   - Learners study foundational material outside the classroom
   - Classroom time focuses on active collaboration and practical application
   - Highly recommended for Clothing and Textiles due to project completion requirements

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: Fiber to Yarn (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner processes raw fibres into yarns to influence the quality and characteristics of the final textile products.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the polymerisation, environment, classification, properties of natural and synthetic fibres for effective use and care
- Select yarn based on fibre source, yarn structure and suitability for the production of textile articles or fabrics

**Sub-topics:**
- Identification and Classification of Fibres (12 Periods)
- Types of Yarns and Their Suitability (10 Periods)

#### Topic 2: Fabric Construction and Decoration (74 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies fabric formation and decoration techniques to create textiles articles that impact the quality and performance of final products.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate the elements and principles of design used in enhancing appearance creation on fabrics, garment and aesthetic soft furnishings construction
- Analyse techniques used in fabric construction (weaving, knitting, crocheting, and braiding) and include illustrations of weave paper point, knitting, crotchet diagrams and their patterns
- Discover the physical and structural characteristics of various fabrics, taking note of stitch notations in crocheting and knitting
- Discuss the effects of characteristics of various fabric construction techniques on fabric end-use
- Construct fabrics using various techniques to enhance functionality
- Analyse other decorative techniques, including appliqué, quilting, bead work, pile work, macramé, patchwork, needlepoint, lacing, binding, embroidery, piping, and smocking
- Generate drawings, sketches and images of aesthetic designs
- Demonstrate each aesthetic design skill on a piece of fabric following documented procedures
- Compile a file of the aesthetic design skills
- Explore fabric finishes, the method of function, fabric use, and care

**Sub-topics:**
- Elements and Principles of Design (6 Periods)
- Methods of Fabric Construction (24 Periods)
- Methods of Fabric Decoration (20 Periods)
- Fabric Finishes (10 Periods)
- Care of Fabrics and Laundry Processes (14 Periods)

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 3: Basic Sewing Processes (108 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies basic sewing techniques to construct simple garments and household items.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Identify and properly use tools and equipment used in garment construction
- Construct different types of stitches for various sewing purposes
- Create different types of seams for garment construction
- Apply techniques for disposal of fullness in garments
- Construct various types of openings and fastenings
- Create pockets, sleeves and collars for garments
- Apply appropriate edge finishes to garments
- Design aesthetic designs for garments and household furnishings

**Sub-topics:**
- Tools and Equipment Used in Garment Construction (14 Periods)
- Construction of Different Stitches (12 Periods)
- Seams (8 Periods)
- Disposal of Fullness (10 Periods)
- Openings and Fastening (10 Periods)
- Pockets, Sleeves and Collars (14 Periods)
- Edge Finishes (6 Periods)
- Aesthetic Designs for Garments and Household Furnishings (34 Periods)

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

#### Topic 4: Pattern Making and Style Interpretation (22 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner interprets styles and creates patterns for garment construction.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Interpret different garment styles and designs
- Take accurate body measurements for pattern making
- Use commercial patterns effectively
- Draft basic patterns for simple garments
- Adapt patterns to fit different body types and styles

**Sub-topics:**
- Style Interpretation and Body Measurements (6 Periods)
- Commercial and Drafted Paper Patterns (12 Periods)
- Pattern Drafting and Adaptation (4 Periods)

#### Topic 5: Garment Construction – Part 1 (108 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner constructs children's wear garments using appropriate techniques and finishes.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Construct various types of children's wear
- Apply appropriate garment finishing techniques
- Ensure proper fitting of constructed garments
- Demonstrate quality construction standards

**Sub-topics:**
- Children's Wear (86 Periods)
- Garment Finishing and Fitting (22 Periods)

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 1

#### Topic 6: Garment Construction - Part II (108 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner constructs adult garments with advanced techniques and professional finishes.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Construct women's garments using advanced techniques
- Construct men's garments with professional standards
- Perform alterations on garments for better fit
- Apply neatening and modeling techniques
- Demonstrate advanced construction skills

**Sub-topics:**
- Adults' Wear: Women's Garments; Men's Garments (72 Periods)
- Alterations, Neatening and Modelling (36 Periods)

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 2

#### Topic 7: Soft Furnishings (52 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner designs and constructs soft furnishings for interior spaces.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply construction techniques for soft furnishings
- Design interior spaces using appropriate textiles
- Create various soft furnishing items
- Demonstrate understanding of interior design principles

**Sub-topics:**
- Construction Techniques of Soft Furnishings (40 Periods)
- Designing Interior Spaces (12 Periods)

#### Topic 8: Development of Textile, Clothing, and Fashion Industry (56 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the development and trends in the textile, clothing, and fashion industry.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse fashion industry trends and fashion cycles
- Study the history of textiles and clothing worldwide and in Uganda
- Examine the evolution of textiles from different cultures
- Understand the impact of globalization on fashion industry

**Sub-topics:**
- Fashion Industry Trends and Fashion Cycles (18 Periods)
- History of Textiles and Clothing Worldwide and in Uganda (18 Periods)
- Evolution of Textiles from Different Cultures (20 Periods)

**Competency:** The learner markets quality textile products effectively using appropriate business strategies.

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., garments, projects, portfolios, presentations), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Project-based learning is an essential component of 21st-century education. Learners should be encouraged to develop projects based on various syllabus topics. Teachers should collaborate with learners to design projects that connect to real-life situations within their local environment while ensuring alignment with learning outcomes and competencies.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

## Special Educational Needs Support

The Advanced Secondary Curriculum is designed to empower all learners, including those with Special Educational Needs (SEN), to reach their full potential and contribute meaningfully to society. Teachers should create inclusive and adaptive activities in Clothing and Textiles for learners with Special Education Needs, including:

- **Assistive Tools:** Magnifiers, text-to-speech software, adaptive sewing machines, soft-grip needle holders, talking measuring tools, and ergonomic tools
- **Visual Aids:** Step-by-step instructions, video tutorials, breaking tasks into smaller steps
- **Accessible Workspaces:** Adjustable tables, wide pathways, good lighting, and low-distraction areas
- **Adaptive Materials:** Tools labeled with large print or Braille, tactile markers or colour coding
- **Flexible Teaching Strategies:** Simplified lessons, clear language, hands-on demonstrations, and visual aids
- **Inclusive Assessment:** Focus on progress and skill acquisition, using oral evaluations, project-based assessments, or flexible formats

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.

**Fiber:** The basic unit from which yarns and fabrics are made

**Textile:** Any flexible material made by creating an interlocking network of fibers

**Garment:** An item of clothing

**Pattern:** A template from which parts of a garment are traced onto fabric before being cut out and assembled

**Soft Furnishings:** Textile products used in home decoration such as curtains, cushions, and bed linens
