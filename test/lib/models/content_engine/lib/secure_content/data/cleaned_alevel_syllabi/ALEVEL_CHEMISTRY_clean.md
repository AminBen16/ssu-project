# Chemistry - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Chemistry  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Chemistry syllabus is designed to enable learners to develop a deep understanding of chemical principles, experimental skills, and real-world applications. The course emphasizes:

- **Theoretical Understanding:** Atomic structure, bonding, thermodynamics, kinetics, and organic chemistry
- **Practical Skills:** Laboratory techniques, experimental design, and data analysis
- **Real-World Applications:** Industrial processes, environmental chemistry, and healthcare applications
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Scientific integrity, environmental responsibility, and safety consciousness

---

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Chemistry at Advanced Level

The Advanced Level Chemistry syllabus aims to enable learners to:

1. **Knowledge and Understanding**
   - Understand basic principles and concepts in Chemistry
   - Know how theories and models explain chemical concepts
   - Recognize resources for discovering unfamiliar principles
   - Apply chemistry principles in everyday life situations

2. **Experimental Skills**
   - Develop experimental attitudes through laboratory work
   - Familiarize with scientific methods
   - Design and carry out practical investigations
   - Develop skills for practical investigation and exploration

3. **Higher Order Thinking**
   - Develop initiative for inventiveness
   - Build capacity to design models and analytical schemes
   - Apply knowledge to solve complex chemical problems

4. **Career Preparation**
   - Prepare for further studies in Chemistry and related fields
   - Appreciate chemistry applications in other disciplines
   - Understand industrial and environmental relevance

### Generic Skills Development

The syllabus integrates the following generic skills:

- **Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving**
  - Planning and carrying out investigations
  - Sorting and analysing information
  - Identifying problems and proposing solutions
  - Predicting outcomes and making reasoned decisions

- **Co-operation and Self-Directed Learning**
  - Working with others effectively
  - Taking responsibility for learning
  - Working independently with persistence
  - Managing goals and time

- **Creativity and Innovation**
  - Using imagination to explore possibilities
  - Generating ideas collaboratively
  - Developing new solutions
  - Experimenting with innovative alternatives

- **Communication**
  - Listening attentively and comprehending
  - Reading accurately and fluently
  - Writing and presenting information coherently
  - Using various media to communicate ideas

- **Mathematical Computation**
  - Using numbers and measurements accurately
  - Interpreting mathematical data
  - Using mathematics to justify decisions

- **Digital Literacy**
  - Using technology to create and process information
  - Applying digital tools for chemical analysis

- **Diversity and Multicultural Skills**
  - Appreciating cultural diversity
  - Respecting all cultures
  - Valuing positive cultural practices

### Cross-Cutting Issues

The syllabus addresses:

- **Environmental Awareness:** Conservation, pollution management, sustainable practices
- **Health Awareness:** Chemical safety, health applications of chemistry
- **Life Skills:** Decision-making, responsibility, ethical considerations
- **Socio-economic Challenges:** Industrial applications, resource management
- **Citizenship and Patriotism:** National development through chemistry

### Values Integration

Core values emphasized:

- Respect for humanity and environment
- Honesty and integrity in scientific work
- Justice and fairness

- Creativity and innovation
- Social responsibility

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 9 periods per week
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical and practical components

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Investigation through research aided by ICT tools
   - Problem-solving through questions and scenarios
   - Enhancement of critical thinking and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Hands-on experiences during research
   - Learning through reflection on activities
   - Development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: Moles and Equations (51 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses stoichiometric relationships, evaluates experimental methods, and synthesises solutions to complex chemical problems.

**Sub-topic 1.1: Masses of Atoms and Molecules, Accurate Relative Atomic Masses (9 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate the relationship between atomic masses and isotopic abundances in calculating accurate atomic masses
- Synthesise methods to determine accurate relative atomic mass using given isotopic data

**Sub-topic 1.2: Amount of Substance, Mole Calculations (12 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the concept of the mole as a fundamental unit of measurement for chemical substances
- Formulate strategies for calculating moles in compounds and reactions

**Sub-topic 1.3: Chemical Formulae and Chemical Equations (9 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Construct balanced chemical equations for given reactions, including synthesis, decomposition, and combustion reactions
- Critically examine the relationships between empirical and molecular formulae in representing compounds

**Sub-topic 1.4: Solutions and Concentration, Calculations Involving Gas Volumes (21 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply the principles of concentration and gas laws to accurately calculate molarity and gas volumes in various solutions and reactions

#### Topic 2: Atomic and Electronic Structure (27 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner deduces electronic configurations, evaluates their implications for chemical properties and bonding, and synthesises models to predict atomic behaviour in various contexts.

**Sub-topic 2.1: Electron Configurations of Atoms and Ions (12 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply the Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle, and Hund's rule to write electronic configurations of atoms and ions
- Use electronic configurations to predict reactivity

**Sub-topic 2.2: Radioactivity and Its Applications (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the nature and types of radioactive decay and apply this knowledge to predict decay rates and stability
- Evaluate the principles of radioactivity in real-life applications, such as in medicine, industry, and environmental science

#### Topic 3: Bonding and Structure (30 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the types of chemical bonds and molecular structures, and relates them to the properties and uses of substances in real-life contexts.

**Sub-topic 3.1: Formation of Ionic and Metallic Bonds (12 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Justify the formation of ionic and metallic bonds based on electron transfer and electrostatic forces
- Evaluate the properties of ionic and metallic compounds in relation to bond strength and structural composition

**Sub-topic 3.2: Covalent Bonds and Molecular Structures (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the formation of covalent bonds including dative covalency and explain molecular structures by applying VSEPR theory
- Analyse the polarity of molecules based on molecular structure and electron distribution

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 4: Periodicity I (33 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the trends and periodic properties of elements, to explain and predict the reactivity and properties of elements in the Periodic Table.

**Sub-topic 4.1: The Periodic Table (6 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate how the Periodic Table is organised based on atomic number and properties, recognising its historical significance
- Analyse the position of elements to predict their chemical behaviour, considering periodic trends

**Sub-topic 4.2: Variation in Trends of Properties Across Periods and the Diagonal Relationships (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the periodic physical and chemical properties in the Periodic Table, identifying patterns and exceptions
- Justify the variation in physical and chemical properties across period 3, incorporating diagonal relationships

**Sub-topic 4.3: Trends in Properties of Group 2 Elements (9 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate the reactivity and properties of Group 2 elements and their compounds in relation to their applications in industry

#### Topic 5: Thermochemistry (45 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses and evaluates thermodynamic principles and processes to predict the spontaneity and feasibility of chemical reactions and processes.

**Sub-topic 5.1: Enthalpy Changes and Energy Profiles (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Synthesise energy profiles for exothermic and endothermic reactions, explaining the concept of enthalpy change
- Evaluates the role of activation energy in reaction mechanisms using energy profile diagrams

**Sub-topic 5.2: Types of Enthalpy Changes and Hess's Law (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse different types of enthalpy changes and the conditions under which they occur
- Apply Hess's law to calculate unknown enthalpy changes using energy cycles and thermochemical equations

**Sub-topic 5.3: Born-Haber Cycles and Lattice Energy (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the concept of lattice energy, its role in the formation of ionic compounds, and describe the steps involved in constructing Born-Haber cycles
- Apply Born-Haber cycles to solve problems involving the calculation of lattice energies and related thermodynamic quantities

#### Topic 6: Organic Chemistry I (57 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the structures, functional groups, and reactivity of organic compounds, and applies knowledge of organic reactions and mechanisms to synthesise organic molecules.

**Sub-topic 6.1: Introduction to Organic Compounds (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply principles of organic chemistry to understand the classification and general characteristics of organic compounds, including functional groups, homologous series, and the concept of isomerism
- Use IUPAC nomenclature to name different organic compounds

**Sub-topic 6.2: Alkanes, Alkenes and Alkynes (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply principles of organic chemistry to differentiate between alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes, and explain their physical properties based on molecular structure
- Predict the reactivity and reaction mechanisms of alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes

**Sub-topic 6.3: Halogen Compounds (Alkyl Halides) (12 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the structure and properties of alkyl halides in terms of their reactivity with different reagents, identifying substitution and elimination reactions
- Evaluate the uses and impacts of alkyl halides in industrial processes

**Sub-topic 6.4: Benzene and Methyl Benzene (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Compare benzene and methyl benzene in terms of structure, stability, and reactivity in electrophilic substitution reactions
- Analyse the environmental and health impacts of benzene derivatives

---

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

#### Topic 7: Equilibria I (81 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses principles of chemical equilibrium and applies them to explain and solve problems related to industrial processes, ionic equilibria, buffer systems, and solubility equilibria in various chemical and real-world contexts.

**Sub-topic 7.1: The Concept of Chemical Equilibrium (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate the dynamic nature of chemical equilibrium and determination of equilibrium constants
- Predict how closed systems respond to stress, by applying Le Chatelier's principle

**Sub-topic 7.2: Equilibria and the Chemical Industry (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate how temperature, concentration, and pressure influence chemical equilibrium and propose methods to optimise reaction conditions in industrial applications

**Sub-topic 7.3: Ionic Equilibrium, Hydrolysis of Salts and Buffer Solutions (30 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply the concepts of ionic equilibrium, including the dissociation of acids and bases, the pH scale, the hydrolysis of salts, and how buffer solutions maintain pH stability in chemical systems
- Evaluate the role of buffer solutions in biological and environmental systems

**Sub-topic 7.4: Solubility Equilibria (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate the factors affecting solubility equilibria, calculate solubility product, and predict precipitation in different ionic solutions
- Apply the concept of solubility equilibria to real-life scenarios

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 1

#### Topic 8: Equilibria II (45 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner applies principles of physical equilibria and colligative properties to analyse and predict changes in physical systems, including phase transitions and effects of solute concentration on solution behaviour, in scientific and real-world contexts.

**Sub-topic 8.1: Physical Equilibria (27 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate the principles of Raoult's law in ideal and non-ideal solutions, distinguishing their behaviours under varying conditions
- Evaluate properties of two component systems involving immiscible liquid mixtures and their applications in real-life
- Analyse the behaviour of solid-liquid mixtures and their applications in industry

**Sub-topic 8.2: Colligative Properties (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse colligative properties such as boiling point elevation and freezing point depression, and relate them to solute concentration in different solutions

#### Topic 9: Organic Chemistry II (63 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses reaction mechanisms, evaluates reaction pathways and conditions, and designs multi-step syntheses to achieve target compounds.

**Sub-topic 9.1: Alcohols and Phenols (24 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the structure, nomenclature, physical and chemical properties of alcohols and phenols
- Evaluate the applications of alcohols and phenols in real life

**Sub-topic 9.2: Carbonyl Compounds (Aldehydes and Ketones) (24 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the structure, preparation and reactivity of aldehydes and ketones, identifying how functional groups affect chemical behaviour in different environments
- Evaluate the different applications of carbonyl compounds in real life

**Sub-topic 9.3: Carboxylic Acids and Derivatives (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the structure, nomenclature, preparation and properties of carboxylic acids and their derivatives (esters, acyl chlorides)
- Evaluate the different applications of carboxylic acids and their derivatives in real life

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 2

#### Topic 10: Electrochemistry (54 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses redox reactions, electrochemical processes and electrochemical cells, applies the principles of electrode potentials to predict reaction spontaneity and electrolysis outcomes.

**Sub-topic 10.1: Redox Reactions and Oxidation Numbers (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the concept of redox reactions, including the identification of oxidation and reduction processes, and assign oxidation numbers to binary compounds and ions
- Apply the rules for determining oxidation numbers to balance redox equations (in acidic and basic media) and predict the products of redox reactions in various chemical contexts, including redox titrations
- Evaluate the importance of redox reactions in industrial and biological systems, explaining their impact on processes like respiration and corrosion

**Sub-topic 10.2: Electrochemical Cells and Applications (21 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the structure and functioning of electrochemical cells, including galvanic (voltaic) and electrolytic cells, and describe the role of electrodes, electrolytes, and the movement of ions in these cells
- Apply principles of electrochemistry to calculate cell potentials, predict the feasibility of redox reactions, and evaluate the industrial applications of electrochemical cells, such as in batteries, electroplating, and corrosion prevention

**Sub-topic 10.3: Electrolysis and Faraday's Laws (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply Faraday's laws to calculate the quantities of substances produced during electrolysis in various electrolytes

#### Topic 11: Periodicity II (54 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the trends in the physical and chemical properties of Group 14 elements, Group 17 elements and d-block elements and relates these trends to their applications in industrial and environmental contexts.

**Sub-topic 11.1: Trends in Properties of Group 14 Elements and their Compounds (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the general trends in physical properties, chemical reactivity, oxidation states, and bonding within Group 14 elements, with emphasis on their unique characteristics and variations down the group
- Analyse the properties of chlorides and oxides of Group 14 elements and use them to explain the stability of the different oxidation states
- Evaluate the applications and limitations of Group 14 elements and compounds in technology and industry

**Sub-topic 11.2: Trends in Properties of Group 17 Elements and their Compounds (15 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the trends in electronegativity, boiling points, and reactivity of Group 17 elements and their compounds, relating them to their physical and chemical properties
- Evaluate the impact of Group 17 elements in daily life and industry, particularly in disinfection and manufacturing, considering both advantages and environmental concerns

**Sub-topic 11.3: The d-Block Transition Elements (24 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the trends of the physical properties of the first series of the d-block transition elements (atomic radius, melting point, ionisation energy, density) across the period and compare them with s-block elements
- Evaluate the properties of transition elements, explaining concepts like electronic structure, variable oxidation states, magnetic properties and complex formation, coloured ions, magnetic properties and catalytic behaviour
- Critique the role of transition metals in industrial processes, medicine, and biological systems, assessing both their utility and environmental or health risks

---

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 3

#### Topic 12: Organic Chemistry III (36 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses the chemical properties, industrial applications, and environmental impacts of amines and polymers, evaluates their social and ecological implications, and proposes innovations and sustainable solutions for addressing challenges in their use and disposal.

**Sub-topic 12.1: Amines (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the structure, nomenclature, preparation and properties of amines in relation to their functional group
- Evaluate the industrial and biological significance of amines, and assess their roles in real life

**Sub-topic 12.2: Polymers and Polymerisation (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Synthesise information on the types of polymerisation (addition and condensation), including the mechanisms involved in forming polymers from monomers, and distinguish between natural and synthetic polymers based on their structures and properties
- Evaluate the environmental impact of polymers with emphasis on artificial polymers, proposing sustainable alternatives and analysing recycling techniques

#### Topic 13: Reaction Kinetics (36 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner evaluates factors influencing reaction rates, and applies the principles of reaction mechanisms and rate laws to predict the speed of chemical reactions under various conditions.

**Sub-topic 13.1: Rate Equations and Orders of Reaction (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Deduce rate equations from experimental data, determine the order of reaction, molecularity of reaction, and explain the significance of rate constants
- Use kinetic data to determine the order of reaction, rate constant and half-life

**Sub-topic 13.2: Factors Affecting Rates of Reactions (18 Periods)**
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate how temperature, concentration, surface area, and catalysts affect reaction rates by applying collision theory to support conclusions
- Evaluate the influence of factors affecting reaction rates in industrial contexts, critically analysing how optimising reaction rates benefits production processes

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – watching learners working (good for assessing skills, values and attitudes)
2. **Conversation** – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and understanding)
3. **Product** – appraising the learner's work (writing, report, calculation, presentation, model, drawing, etc.)

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, information from any one can be checked against the other two for uniformity and reliability.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are assessed using:
- Observation checklists
- Scoring rubrics
- Portfolio assessment
- Project evaluation

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes are assessed over time through:
- Observation of behaviour
- Interactions with learners
- Long-term monitoring of personal development
- Anecdotal records

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

- Assessed using rubrics and observation checklists
- Focus on sustained inquiry and authentic challenges
- Evaluation of driving questions and solutions

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- End-of-topic scenario-based tasks (Activities of Integration)
- End of cycle assessment for certification

### Record-Keeping

Accurate records maintained including:
- Observation checklists
- Rubrics and scoring grids
- Learner portfolios
- Anecdotal notes
- Progress tracking files

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** One in which learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit an individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of judging a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills which are deployed in all subjects and enhance learning; also equip young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences which allows each student to feel confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement which specifies what the learner should know, understand, or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired by following the programme of study in a particular Learning Area; enables a learner to apply knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity which allows a learner to show the extent to which they have achieved the Learning Outcomes.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An aspect of the normal teaching and learning process that will enable a formative assessment to be made.
