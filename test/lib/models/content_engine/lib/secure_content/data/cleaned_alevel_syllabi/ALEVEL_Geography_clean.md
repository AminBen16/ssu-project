# Geography - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Geography  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Geography syllabus equips learners with skills necessary for harnessing natural resources sustainably, developing learners' ability to think systemically, fostering critical thinking and the ability to present structured, coherent and evidence-based arguments, enhancing learners' ability to interpret the world from a spatial perspective, and cultivating learners' understanding of personal and collective responsibility in addressing contemporary challenges. The course emphasizes:

- **Geographic Analysis:** Understanding of physical and human geography processes
- **Spatial Interpretation:** Collection, analysis, and interpretation of geographic data
- **Environmental Management:** Assessment of environmental impacts and conservation strategies
- **Development Planning:** Analysis of development patterns and planning strategies
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Environmental responsibility, social consciousness, and national development

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Geography at Advanced Level

The Advanced Level Geography syllabus aims to:

1. **Resource Management**
   - Equip learners with skills necessary for harnessing natural resources sustainably
   - Develop learners' ability to think systemically, enabling them to appreciate the complex relationships between human actions across different parts of the world and their impact on local and global environments

2. **Analytical Skills**
   - Foster learners' critical thinking and the ability to present structured, coherent and evidence-based arguments
   - Enhance learners' ability to interpret the world from a spatial perspective
   - Build learners' capacity to interpret and evaluate primary and secondary, qualitative and quantitative data, enabling them to draw well-reasoned conclusions about geographical phenomena

3. **Social Responsibility**
   - Cultivate learners' understanding of personal and collective responsibility in addressing contemporary challenges, including environmental, health and socio-economic crises
   - Prepare learners for further studies and gainful employment in fields that require a strong foundation in geography and geographic methods of analysis and reasoning

4. **Career Development**
   - Prepare learners for further studies and gainful employment in fields requiring a strong foundation in geography
   - Enable application of geographic knowledge and skills in real-world contexts

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 9 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** Balanced across theoretical and practical components

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Inquiry-Based Learning**
   - Learners investigate topics through research driven by their interests and solve problems using a series of questions and scenarios
   - Enhances critical thinking, communication, and research skills

2. **Experiential Learning**
   - Learners actively participate in hands-on experiences during research and develop understanding through reflection on their actions
   - Leads to the development of reflective skills

3. **Problem and Project-Based Learning**
   - Learners find solutions to real-world problems through field investigations, research, and project work
   - Develops critical thinking, social and research skills

4. **Using and Creating Case Studies**
   - Learners analyse and discuss real-world scenarios at local, regional, and global levels
   - Deepens understanding of geographical concepts

5. **Discovery Learning**
   - Learners construct their own knowledge through active participation, exploration, and inquiry
   - Encourages critical thinking and problem-solving

6. **Integration of Geographic Skills**
   - Learners acquire and apply key geographical skills, including mapping and map interpretation, photograph analysis, and statistical evaluation
   - Applied across all topics

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1: The Structure of the Earth (12 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates appreciation of the structure of the Earth and its influence on geomorphic processes and formation of some natural resources by examining the properties of each of its layers to better understand the geologic foundation of human development.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the Earth by examining its nature, shape, and external features as a basis for exploring its geologic foundation
- Assess the significance of the internal properties of the Earth in understanding the evolution of ideas about tectonic landforms of shock waves produced by earthquakes, material erupted by volcanoes, meteorites, Earth's magnetic field and other clues to find out about the interior of the Earth

#### Topic 2: The Origin of Continents and Ocean Basins (34 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of the role of theories in exploring geomorphic concepts by critically evaluating the continental drift theory based on scientific evidence to inform further geographical inquiry and to acquire problem-solving skills.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate the continental drift theory using scientific evidence (u, v/a)
- Predict the future shapes, sizes, and positions of world's continents by applying knowledge of continental drift (u, s, v/a)
- Demonstrate appreciation of how their predictions about scientific inquiry in geomorphic processes and their influence on the way we live on earth (u, gs, v/a)

#### Topic 3: World Development (20 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates an understanding of development by analysing spatial development patterns, development priorities, and factors influencing development, while proposing interventions to address disparities in development at the country or regional level.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Form opinions about world development by analysing statistics, maps, and other relevant information (s, gs, v/a)
- Analyse the level of development in the local area and home country by applying various indicators they use to describe development (u, s, gs, v/a)
- Analyse conditions which may slow down or totally affect holistic regional or national development disparities exist in their country and between countries and to how they relate the disparities to historical and current factors (u, s, gs, v/a)
- Propose a suitable development strategy for Uganda and/or Africa to achieve fast, overall human development (u, gs, v/a)

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 2

#### Topic 4: Agriculture and Development (57 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner gives written or oral advice to a certain community or country on appropriate farming types and techniques to be adopted to promote development through appraising the different types and methods of farming.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Evaluate plans and strategies aimed at promoting agriculture in Uganda and other developing countries (s, gs, v/a)
- Demonstrate understanding of subsistence farming by articulating its types, characteristics and challenges affecting it (u, s, gs)
- Evaluate policies and strategies aimed at improving subsistence agriculture in Uganda and other countries (u, s, gs, v/a)
- Propose a suitable type of commercial agriculture to be adopted in a given locality based on the prevailing conditions to promote societal transformation (u, gs, v/a)
- Evaluate programmes and policies aimed at modernising agriculture in Uganda and other countries to make informed decisions about transforming farming communities (v/a)

#### Topic 5: Soil Development and Management (51 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner proposes feasible ways of using and managing soil through gathering and analysing information about soil properties, formation, and quality to promote sustainable development.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the significance of the composition and properties of soil to land use and environmental development (u, v/a)
- Analyse soil formation in a selected locality by examining soil forming processes, types and factors influencing soil formation, and their relative significance (s, gs, v/a)
- Deduce the arrangement of soil layers in a selected area using information from secondary sources and field observations in order to determine the soil profile for possible developments that can be carried out in the area (u, s, gs, v/a)
- Infer suitable land use options for certain areas using the knowledge of the soil profile and soil fully developed idealised soil profile (u, gs, v/a)
- Analyse factors affecting soil productivity to propose sustainable practices to enhance soil quality for long-term agricultural success and environmental health (k, u, s, v)
- Propose feasible soil conservation strategies for a selected area to promote sustainable development by analysing the causes and effects of soil degradation (u, s, gs, v/a)

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 3

#### Topic 6: Population and Development (38 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner proposes strategies for managing population as a key resource in development through analysing its dynamics and their implications for development at different scales.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Investigate the distribution and density of population using topographic maps, contour lines to identify features and establish geographical relationships (s)
- Propose ways of regulating population growth in developed and developing countries based on statistics and other relevant knowledge (u, s, gs)
- Infer the likely impact of population structure of a town/country or region on resource utilisation and patterns of people's wellbeing to suggest ways of managing the population (v/a)
- Predict occurrence and impact of population migrations in Uganda and other places based on the knowledge of migrations (c)
- Develop a plan/manifesto for regulating or promoting a certain type of population in Uganda (u, s, gs, v/a)

#### Topic 7: Drainage and the Role of Rivers in Shaping the Landscape (32 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner devises sustainable ways of harnessing drainage features and related resources for development through analysing their nature and impact on landscape, ecosystems, and human activities.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate understanding of Uganda's drainage system by using relevant maps and diagrams to illustrate its aspects (u, s)
- Assess the role of Pleistocene Earth movements in the evolution of Uganda's drainage (v/a)
- Infer, from photographs, topographic survey maps and field observations, the river channel processes taking place in the areas shown and their effects on landscape (s)
- Develop a plan for exploiting the resource potential presented by a drainage feature to improve peoples' lives by applying resources associated with drainage features (u, s, gs, v/a)

#### Topic 8: Energy Production (38 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner devises innovative ways of developing and using energy through analysing the production, consumption and sustainability of the various sources of energy to balance energy use and environmental stewardship.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate understanding of energy resources and their spatial distribution to the formation, abundance, and sustainability of the energy source (u, s, gs)
- Compare energy production and consumption in countries at different levels of development to understand the need for wise use of energy resources (v/a)
- Analyse the influence of economic, environmental, and social factors on energy resource development (u, v/a)
- Assess the impact of energy development on the environment by examining the extraction of energy sources or an energy project (u, v/a)
- Evaluate policies and strategies aimed at ensuring access to sustainable sources of energy to understand Uganda's efforts to ensure sustainable energy use (v/a)
- Argue out the case for sustainable utilisation of energy resources at local, regional, and global scales to promote energy security (v/a)

#### Topic 9: Climatology and Meteorology (75 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates understanding of climate and weather through analysing atmospheric dynamics, climate data, and predicting weather patterns and their effects to propose evidence-based strategies for adapting human activities to climate.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the atmosphere and its influence on weather, climate and life on earth by examining the sources of solid particles found in the atmosphere (u, c, s, gs, v/a)
- Demonstrate understanding of solar radiation by analysing its level of environmental awareness and ability to disaggregate solar radiation into light and suntan/heat energy and its effects on weather, climate and life on Earth (u, gs, v/a)
- Discuss factors influencing the amount of solar radiation received at a given place and appreciate temperature variations and their effects on human activities and ecosystems (u, v/a)
- Analyse the causes, effects and explain why temperature decreases as we rise towards the local weather patterns, air quality and human life (u, v/a)
- Demonstrate understanding of the various relationships between humidity and indoor and outdoor weather conditions to appreciate their contributions to maintaining life on earth (u, v/a)
- Predict the occurrence and intensity of precipitation using the knowledge of humidity (s, gs, v/a)
- Analyse the relationship between temperature, atmospheric pressure and wind systems to understand atmospheric wind movement (u, v/a)
- Demonstrate understanding of winds at different scales by examining their location, types, causes, and effects on weather, climate, and people's ways of life (u)
- Predict weather conditions and their effect at a given place through interpreting weather maps and satellite images (u, s)
- Articulate the role of ocean currents on the climate of coastal areas, adjacent landmasses and human activities (v/a)
- Analyse climatic changes through time and at different scales to appreciate the origin and nature of ongoing climate change (v/a)
- Assess the contribution of human natural processes and activities to climate change by analysing statistics and their ability to listen attentively, comprehend the material, and summarise key points appropriately (u, v/a)
- Demonstrate understanding of the effects of climate change by examining its impact on certain sectors and ecosystems (k, u, v/a)
- Develop an action plan or manifesto to address climate change and its effects in a certain locality based on their knowledge of measures to mitigate climate change (u, s, gs, v/a)

#### Topic 10: Settlement and Urban Growth (33 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner proposes plans for developing and re-developing settlements and measures for their environments through collecting, processing, and analysing information about them to promote the welfare and sustainable development of their inhabitants.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate understanding of settlements by examining their types, characteristics, and patterns (u, s, v/a)

- Analyse the changing types, patterns and functions of rural settlements in Uganda and other countries and the impacts these have on the surrounding areas (v/a)
- Analyse the types, patterns and functions of urban settlements in Uganda and other countries and the impact these have on the surrounding areas to make informed decisions on managing settlements (s, v/a)
- Propose innovative solutions for addressing settlement-related challenges through collecting and analysing data and information about them (u, v/a)
- Propose a feasible land use plan to manage urban growth in a way that balances economic development, social equity, and environmental impact (u, b, v/a)

#### Topic 11: Slope Development (42 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses spatial and temporal variations of slope processes by examining their nature, factors influencing their occurrence and impact to devise strategies for managing slope-related hazards.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Assess the influence of slope development on the physical environment and human activities (u)
- Appraise programs and actions aimed at mitigating and responding to the geographic occurrence of mass wasting in the highland areas of Uganda and the rest of East Africa (u, v/a)
- Predict the occurrence of mass wasting in highland areas by analysing and applying the relevant geographical information (v/a)
- Propose strategies for preventing mass wasting in the highland areas of Uganda by applying relevant information (v/a)

### SENIOR SIX - TERM 2

#### Topic 12: Problems of Food Supply (24 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses local and global food supply problems as well as strategies adopted to address them using relevant information and geographic tools, and proposes innovative solutions to ensure food security for all.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Examine the relationship between the level of economic development and food supply in a country or region by using statistics and other information (s, gs, v/a)
- Demonstrate understanding of food shortages in Sub-Saharan Africa and other developing countries by evaluating the food supply situation using relevant information (u)
- Evaluate policies and programmes aimed at addressing food shortages in Sub-Saharan Africa and other developing countries (v/a)
- Develop an action plan or manifesto for addressing food shortages in Africa or any other area based on the causes and effects of the situation (gs, v/a)

#### Topic 13: Lake and Sea Coasts (42 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner demonstrates appreciation of coasts as dynamic physical environments through examining their nature, geomorphic processes modifying them, and resultant features, as well as benefits and challenges of living in coastal areas to inform decisions aimed at developing such areas.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Analyse the formation and characteristics of coastal and lake landforms and wave action, tidal influence, sediment transport, and human activities shaping coastal landscapes over time (u)
- Predict the future relationship between land and sea or lake coast, using knowledge of current crustal stability and the effects of ongoing changes on named regions to raise awareness about natural geomorphic events (v/a)
- Analyse the influence of coasts on human activities and resource activities, focusing on their potential use of resources associated with coastal landforms and how this can be tapped for development (v/a)
- Propose a plan for developing a coast based on its actual and potential landform resources and challenges as a way of facilitating sustainability of resource utilisation and community development (s, gs)

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., writing, reports, calculations, presentations, maps, diagrams, models, drawings, paintings), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. For example, evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and are part of the Learning Outcomes. Therefore, there is no need to assess them separately. The increasing complexity of subject content fosters the progression of these skills, and they are assessed as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes. The assessment of generic skills is supported through observation checklists and scoring rubrics.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Assessing values and attitudes differs from evaluating knowledge, understanding, and skills, as they are personal, variable, and long-term aspirations. This does not diminish their importance; rather, it highlights that they cannot be easily assessed through tests and examinations. Values and attitudes can be assessed over time through ongoing observation and interaction with learners.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Project-based learning is assessed using rubrics and observation checklists to evaluate how well learners engage with the project, their problem-solving abilities, and the final outcomes.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Traditional end-of-term examinations and tests will no longer be administered
- Assessments will focus on ongoing teacher evaluations based on Activities of Integration
- Learners will also undergo End of Cycle Assessment for certification, with further details provided in the assessment guidelines document

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, maintaining accurate and comprehensive records is essential for tracking learners' progress and achievements. Teachers and schools are responsible for keeping precise records of learners' performance. Various assessment tools, such as observation checklists, rubrics, and scoring grids, are employed to capture learners' abilities and achievements, providing a comprehensive picture of their strengths, weaknesses, and areas for improvement.

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** One in which learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to suit an individual learner's needs, strengths, preferences, and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of judging a learner's performance, by interpreting responses to tasks, in order to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills which are deployed in all subjects, and which enhance the learning of those subjects. These skills also equip young people for work and for life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences which allows each learner to feel confident, respected and safe and equipped to learn at his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement which specifies what the learner should know, understand, or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired by following the programme of study in a particular subject; enables a learner to apply the knowledge and understanding of the Learning Area.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity which gives a learner the opportunity to show the extent to which s/he has achieved the Learning Outcomes.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An aspect of the normal teaching and learning process that will enable a formative assessment to be made.
