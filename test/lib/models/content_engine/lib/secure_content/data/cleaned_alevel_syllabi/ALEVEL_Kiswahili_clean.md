# Kiswahili (Fasihi ya Kiswahili) - A-Level Syllabus

**Source:** National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC), Uganda  
**Level:** Advanced Secondary (A-Level)  
**Subject:** Kiswahili (Fasihi ya Kiswahili)  
**Curriculum:** Competency-Based Learning  
**Year:** 2025  

---

## Course Overview

The A-Level Kiswahili (Fasihi ya Kiswahili) syllabus aims to develop learners' proficiency in Kiswahili literature through comprehensive study of literary genres, critical analysis, and creative writing. It focuses on enhancing learners' ability to understand, analyse, and create Kiswahili literary works while fostering appreciation of Kiswahili culture and literature. The course emphasizes:

- **Literary Analysis:** Understanding and analysing Kiswahili literary genres and works
- **Creative Writing:** Developing skills in composing various literary forms
- **Cultural Appreciation:** Understanding Kiswahili culture through literature
- **Critical Thinking:** Analyzing themes, styles, and techniques in literary works
- **Communication Skills:** Effective oral and written expression in Kiswahili
- **Generic Skills:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and collaboration
- **Values:** Cultural appreciation, linguistic diversity, and literary appreciation

## Subject Rationale and Aims

### Rationale for Teaching Kiswahili at Advanced Level

According to the Constitution of the Republic of Uganda Article 6(2), Kiswahili is the second official language in Uganda and has been recognized as a lingua franca under Article 137 of the East African Community (EAC) Treaty. Additionally, the July 22, 2022 meeting of EAC heads of state approved Kiswahili as one of the official languages of the Community. As a member state, Uganda has an obligation to promote the development of the Kiswahili language for sustainable regional development.

The A-Level Kiswahili syllabus aims to:

1. **Literary Proficiency Development**
   - Develop learners' ability to express themselves fluently and confidently
   - Enhance understanding of Kiswahili literary genres and techniques
   - Foster critical analysis and appreciation of literary works
   - Promote creative writing skills in various literary forms

2. **Cultural Competence**
   - Develop understanding and appreciation of Kiswahili culture and literature
   - Foster awareness of Kiswahili-speaking communities and their contributions
   - Promote intercultural understanding and regional integration
   - Enable engagement with Kiswahili literary heritage

3. **Academic and Career Preparation**
   - Prepare learners for further studies in Kiswahili literature and linguistics
   - Develop transferable skills applicable in various professional contexts
   - Enable application of Kiswahili language skills in regional and international contexts
   - Foster opportunities for cultural and literary collaboration

4. **Personal Development**
   - Develop cognitive skills through literary analysis
   - Promote confidence in cross-cultural communication
   - Foster appreciation of linguistic and literary diversity
   - Enable personal growth through cultural exploration

### Generic Skills Development

The syllabus integrates the following generic skills:

- **Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving**
  - Planning and carrying out investigations
  - Sorting and analysing information
  - Identifying problems and proposing solutions
  - Predicting outcomes and making reasoned decisions
  - Evaluating different solutions

- **Co-operation and Self-Directed Learning**
  - Working effectively in diverse teams
  - Interacting effectively with others
  - Taking responsibility for own learning
  - Working independently with persistence
  - Managing goals and time

- **Creativity and Innovation**
  - Using imaginations to explore possibilities
  - Working with others to generate ideas
  - Suggesting and developing new solutions
  - Experimenting with innovative alternatives
  - Looking for patterns and making generalisation

- **Communication**
  - Listening attentively and with comprehension
  - Talking confidently and explaining ideas clearly
  - Reading accurately and fluently
  - Writing and presenting coherently
  - Using a range of media to communicate ideas

- **Mathematical Computation**
  - Using numbers and measurements accurately
  - Interpreting and interrogating mathematical data
  - Using mathematics to justify and support decisions

- **Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Proficiency**
  - Using technology to create, manipulate and process information
  - Using technology to collaborate, communicate and refine work

- **Diversity and Multicultural Skills**
  - Appreciating cultural diversity
  - Responding respectfully to people of all cultures
  - Respecting positive cultural practices
  - Recognizing ethnicity as a source of creativity and innovation

## Programme Structure

### Time Allocation

- **Total Periods:** 9 periods per week, each of 40 minutes
- **Duration:** Two years (Senior Five and Senior Six)
- **Distribution:** 3 periods for Nathari, 3 periods for Ushairi, 3 periods for Tamthilia and Riwaya

### Teaching Approaches

1. **Communicative Language Teaching**
   - Focus on meaningful communication and interaction
   - Emphasis on functional language use
   - Integration of all four language skills

2. **Literary Analysis Approach**
   - Critical examination of literary works
   - Analysis of themes, styles, and techniques
   - Comparative literature studies

3. **Creative Writing Workshops**
   - Hands-on practice in composing literary works
   - Peer review and feedback sessions
   - Development of individual writing styles

4. **Performance-Based Learning**
   - Dramatization of literary works
   - Oral presentations and recitations
   - Role-playing and simulations

5. **Technology-Enhanced Learning**
   - Use of digital tools and resources
   - Multimedia integration for literary studies
   - Online collaboration and communication

## Detailed Syllabus Content

### SENIOR FIVE - TERM 1

#### Topic 1.1: Nathari (Prose) (11 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses and creates various types of prose works considering literary elements to strengthen writing and communication skills.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate understanding of the concept of literature and its role in society
- Identify various types of prose and distinguish them from other literary genres like poetry
- Classify different types of prose and identify their characteristics
- Analyse stories and identify their characteristics
- Create various types of prose works considering literary elements

**Sub-topics:**
- Introduction to literature
- Types of literature
- Role of literature in society
- Types of prose
- Classification of prose

#### Topic 1.2: Ushairi (Poetry) (10 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses and composes various types of poems for communication, education, and entertainment.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate understanding of the concept of poetry considering meaning, meter (traditional and free), and its importance for cooperation
- Analyse the characteristics of recorded Kiswahili poetry
- Explain the usage of poetry terminology and analyze poems
- Compare and classify various types of poems based on the number of verses
- Demonstrate fluency, self-expression, and confidence when presenting

**Sub-topics:**
- Introduction to Kiswahili poetry
- Poetry terminology
- Types of poems
- Poetry structure (theme, stanza, verses)
- Poetic message
- Poetic techniques

#### Topic 1.3: Tamthilia (Drama) (21 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses, creates, and performs various types of dramas for entertainment, communication, promoting values, and generating income.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate understanding of the concept of drama considering meaning and characteristics
- Identify various types of drama and demonstrate their differences
- Analyze drama texts they have read and respect others' opinions during discussions
- Compare drama texts and identify different features
- Write drama works considering literary elements

**Sub-topics:**
- Introduction to drama
- Types of drama
- Classification of drama
- Drama elements (language, themes, characters)

#### Topic 1.4: Riwaya (Novel) (21 Periods)
**Competency:** The learner analyses and creates various types of novels considering literary elements.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Demonstrate understanding of the concept of novel
- Identify various types of novels and analyze their differences
- Analyze narrative techniques
- Analyze characters and themes
- Create novel chapters considering literary elements

**Sub-topics:**
- Introduction to novel
- Types of novels
- Novel elements (characters, themes)
- Narrative techniques
- Plot development

## Assessment Framework

### Assessment Philosophy

This Advanced Secondary Curriculum sets new expectations for learning, with a shift from Learning Objectives to Learning Outcomes that focus mainly on the application of knowledge and deeper learning that leads to the acquisition of skills.

### Formative Assessment Approaches

**Three Forms of Assessment:**

1. **Observation** – Watching learners as they work, which is effective for assessing skills, values, and attitudes
2. **Conversation** – Engaging in dialogue with learners through questioning and discussion, which is ideal for assessing knowledge and understanding
3. **Product** – Reviewing the learner's work (e.g., writing, presentations, performances, projects), which serves as a tangible, lasting piece of evidence of learning

**Triangulation:** When all three are used, the information gathered can be cross-checked to ensure reliability. Evidence from observation can be validated by comparing it with evidence from conversation and product.

### Assessment of Generic Skills

Generic skills are integrated into the syllabuses and reflected in the Learning Outcomes. As a result, there is no need to assess them separately. These skills develop progressively within the increasingly complex contexts of the subject content and are evaluated as part of the subject-specific Learning Outcomes.

### Assessment of Values and Attitudes

Values and attitudes cannot be assessed through traditional tests and examinations. These aspects are inherently personal, variable and long-term in nature. Instead, values and attitudes are best evaluated over time through careful observation and meaningful interactions with learners.

### Project-Based Learning Assessment

Project-based learning is assessed using a rubric and an observation checklist to evaluate how well learners engage with the project, their problem-solving abilities, and the final outcomes.

### Summative Assessment

- One school-based summative assessment at the end of the year
- Assessment focuses on continuous teacher evaluations during Activities of Integration
- End-of-cycle assessment for certification with comprehensive guidelines outlined in the assessment manual

### Record-Keeping

In competency-based learning, accurate and comprehensive record-keeping is crucial to track learners' progress and achievements. Various assessment tools and strategies are employed to capture learners' demonstration of abilities and achievements, including observation checklists, rubrics and scoring grids. The collected data and evidence are correctly recorded and maintained in learners' files, portfolios and anecdotal notes.

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Competency Curriculum:** A curriculum where learners develop the ability to apply their learning with confidence in a range of situations.

**Differentiation:** The design or adaptation of learning experiences to meet individual learners' needs, strengths, preferences and abilities.

**Formative Assessment:** The process of evaluating a learner's performance by interpreting responses to tasks to gauge progress and inform subsequent learning steps.

**Generic Skills:** Skills applicable across all subjects, enhancing the learning and preparing young people for work and life.

**Inclusion:** An approach to planning learning experiences that ensures each learner feels confident, respected, safe and equipped to learn to his or her full potential.

**Learning Outcome:** A statement specifying what a learner should know, understand or be able to do within a particular aspect of a subject area.

**Process Skill:** A capability acquired through a specific programme of study in a learning area, enabling learners to apply their knowledge and understanding.

**Sample Assessment Activity:** An activity providing the learner with the opportunity to demonstrate their achievement of Learning Outcomes, typically integrated into regular teaching and learning.

**Suggested Learning Activity:** An activity within the regular teaching and learning process that facilitates formative assessment.

**Fasihi:** Literature in Kiswahili, encompassing all literary genres and works

**Nathari:** Prose literature in Kiswahili, including stories, essays, and narratives

**Ushairi:** Poetry in Kiswahili, encompassing various poetic forms and techniques

**Tamthilia:** Drama in Kiswahili, including plays and theatrical works

**Riwaya:** Novel in Kiswahili, extended narrative fictional works
