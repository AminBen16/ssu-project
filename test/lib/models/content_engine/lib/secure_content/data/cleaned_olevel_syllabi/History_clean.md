# HISTORY AND POLITICAL EDUCATION SYLLABUS

**Subject:** History and Political Education  
**Level:** O-Level (Lower Secondary)  
**Years:** Senior 1-4  
**Source:** NCDC Uganda, 2019  

---

## SENIOR 1

### THEME 1: UNDERSTANDING OUR PAST

#### Topic 1.1: Finding out about our past (10 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands different methods of finding out about the past, and appreciate the importance of historical sites.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the meaning of History and political education (k)
- Appreciate the importance of learning about our past (v)
- Understand how people find out about the past (u)
- Know the historical sites in Uganda and the rest of East Africa (k)
- Understand the benefits of historical sites in Uganda (u)

#### Topic 1.2: The origin of man (16 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the theories about the origin of man using a series of accounts.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand traditional East African beliefs about the origin of man (u)
- Analyse the bible and the creation stories about the origin of man (a, s)
- Know scientific view about the origin of man (k)
- Analyse the concept of human evolution (u)
- Understand the out of Africa theory about the origin of man (u)
- Appreciate the multi-regional theory on creation and the origin of man (a, s)

#### Topic 1.3: Migration and settlement in East Africa since 1000 AD (8 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the origins of their own families, as a result of migration and settlement of people in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the groups of people who lived in East Africa before 19th century migrations (u)
- Know the origins of local people in different areas of East Africa (s, k)
- Understand the origins of Bantu, Luo and Ngoni people who came to East Africa (u)
- Know the reasons for the migration of these peoples into East Africa (k)
- Understand the results of Bantu, Luo and Mfecane migration into East Africa (k, u)

#### Topic 1.4: Culture and ethnic groups in East Africa (12 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the concept of culture and the people relations in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Appreciate the existence of cultural values in our families (k, u)
- Know the key cultural institutions and their leaders in East Africa (k)
- Know cultural centres that exist in East Africa (k)
- Know the ethnic groups in East Africa
- Analyse the contribution of cultural handcrafts in East Africa (u, a)
- Understand the need to respect the cultures of other ethnic groups to avoid conflicts (u, v)
- Appreciate the value of cultural traditional transitional justice mechanisms in conflict resolutions in East Africa (k, v, u)
- Understand the role of culture and gender in family matters

#### Topic 1.5: State formation in East Africa (14 periods)
**Competency:** The learner appreciates the organisation of centralised and non-centralised states in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the centralised and non-centralised states in East Africa (v, k)
- Know the characteristics of centralised and non-centralised societies (u)
- Understand the distinction between the centralised and non-centralized societies in East Africa (u)
- Explain the factors that contributed to the growth and decline of pre-colonial societies (u)

#### Topic 1.6: Religions in East Africa (10 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the influence and impact of religions in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the religions that existed in East Africa before the coming of foreign religions
- Know the Christian and Islamic religious groups that came to Uganda in the 19th century, why they came and where they had influence (k)
- Understand the impact of foreign religions in Uganda (v, u)
- Know the activities carried out by the European Christian Missionaries (v, s)
- Understand why Christians and some Muslims were killed at Namugongo (v, u)

#### Topic 1.7: Local and external trade contacts with East African communities (18 periods)
**Sub-topic 1.7.1: Indian Ocean Trade and slavery in East Africa (9 periods)
**Competency:** The Learner understands the pre-colonial trade contacts and connects them to the colonial trade systems in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand trade contacts that existed in East Africa before the pre-colonial time
- Understand the pre-colonial trade system in East Africa that began before 1800
- Understand the organisation of Indian Ocean trade in East Africa
- Know the relationship between slave trade and the Indian Ocean trade
- Know the organisation of trade and slavery (k)
- Understand slave trade and its impact in East Africa (u)
- Know the impact of slave trade abolition in East Africa

**Sub-topic 1.7.2: East African Long Distance Trade (9 periods)
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the background of East Africa's Long Distance trade (u)
- Know the reasons for growth and expansion of Long Distance trade in East Africa (v, k)
- Understand the organisation of Long Distance trade in East Africa
- Know the impact of East African Long Distance trade (s, u, v)
- Appreciate the contribution of Long Distance trade in the history of East Africa

#### Topic 1.8: Scramble, partition and colonisation of East Africa (10 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the causes, methods and impact of the colonisation of East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the term scramble, partition and colonisation of East Africa
- Understand the reasons why Europeans and Asians came to East Africa (u)
- Understand how Uganda and the rest of East Africa was colonised (u)
- Understand the results of colonisation of East Africa (s, u)
- Appreciate the impact of European and Asian influence in East Africa (s)
- Understand how the rest of Africa was colonised by the different European powers (u)

#### Topic 1.9: Response to the establishment of colonial rule in East Africa (10 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands how East African communities reacted against colonial rule in both primary and secondary responses.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the concept of collaboration and resistance (u)
- Analyse the factors that made East Africans either to collaborate or resist colonial rule (a, u)
- Know the key agreements made in East Africa with colonialists (k)
- Analyse the impact of the signing of the 1900 Buganda agreement to East Africans (a, s)
- Know the terms and the significance of 1923 Devonshire White Paper in Kenya (k)

## SENIOR 2

### THEME 2: COLONISATION AND STRUGGLE FOR INDEPENDENCE IN EAST AFRICA

#### Topic 2.1: The colonial administrative systems in East Africa (12 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the nature and impact of colonial administrative systems on the peoples of East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the colonial administration systems in Uganda (u)
- Know the reasons why different types of colonial administration was used in East Africa (u)
- Know the effects of indirect and direct rule used in East Africa (v, s, u)
- Appreciate the contribution of colonial administration systems used in East Africa (s, u)

#### Topic 2.2: The colonial economy in East Africa (12 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the impact of road network and general colonial economy in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand economic conditions before and after the introduction of colonial economy (u)
- Know the features of colonial economy in East Africa
- Understand the reasons why the colonial administration encouraged both peasant and plantation farming (v, u)
- Understand why industries were established in colonial East Africa (v, u)
- Know the impact of the development of transport system in colonial East Africa
- Understand the health and social problems that were faced during the colonial economy period (k, u)
- Appreciate the impact of colonial economy in East Africa (v, u)

#### Topic 2.3: World wars and their impact in East Africa (14 periods)
**Sub-topic 2.3.1: World War I (7 periods)
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the meaning of World War I (k)
- Understand the major countries or powers involved at World War I (u)
- Know the reasons why the East African countries were involved in World War I (a, v)
- Understand how the events of World War I influenced future history of East Africa (u)
- Appreciate the impact of World War 1 in East Africa (a, v)

**Sub-topic 2.3.2: World War II in East Africa, 1939-1945 (7 periods)
**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the background for the outbreak of World War II (u)
- Understand the reasons for East Africa's involvement in World War II
- Know some of the East African personalities who were involved in world war II (u)
- Appreciate the role of East African states in the World War II (u)
- Appreciate the impact of World War II towards the rise of independence in the East African states (v, k, u)

#### Topic 2.4: Struggle for independence in East Africa (14 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the causes and results of independence struggles in East Africa.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the reasons for the rise of nationalism in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania (u)
- Know the various approaches used by people of East Africa to demand for their independence from colonial rule (u)
- Appreciate the steps taken in the struggle for East African states independence (u, v)
- Compare the Ugandan struggle for independence with that of the East African states (k, u)

#### Topic 2.5: Post-independence socio-economic challenges in East Africa (14 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands and evaluates the challenges faced by East African states after independence.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the various European states that passed on independence to African leaders (k)
- Understand the social and economic challenges faced by East African states after the independence (u)

#### Topic 2.6: Civil society and non-governmental organisations in East Africa (12 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the civil society organisations (CSOs) and non-governmental organisations (NGOs) and their roles and challenges they face in promoting community welfare.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the origin and the background for CSOs and NGOs formation in East Africa (u)
- Know the difference between CSO's, and NGO's (k)
- Understand the role of a CSO, and NGOs in East Africa (u, k)
- Appreciate the challenges facing CSOs and NGOs in East Africa (u)
- Appreciate the role of CSO and NGOs in the development of the East African states (a, v)

#### Topic 2.7: Changing land tenure systems in East Africa (10 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands and evaluates issues of land ownership and management in Uganda.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the various types of land ownership in Uganda (v, k)
- Understand how land was traditionally owned in Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania (v, u)
- Know the main land reforms carried out in Uganda since independence
- Understand the historical development of land ownership in Uganda (u)
- Appreciate the value of land as a cornerstone to development in Uganda (u)

#### Topic 2.8: Key personalities in the history of East Africa before independence (18 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands and appreciates the nationalists who struggled for the independence of East African states.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the background and the East African problems caused by colonialists
- Understand key personalities who struggled for the independence of East African states
- Appreciate the impact of East African personalities in the struggle for independence

## SENIOR 3

### THEME 3: UGANDA

#### Topic 3.1: The structure of government in Uganda (15 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the structures for the governments of East Africa and how they operate.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the main differences between a country, nation, state and government (k)
- Understand and show the structure of Uganda government, with its three arms (v, k, s)
- Appreciate the best structure for a good government (s, u)
- Understand other systems of government in East Africa (u)
- Compare the Ugandan system of government with other partner states in the East African Community (u)

#### Topic 3.2: The local government systems in Uganda (16 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the relationship between the central government and the decentralised systems of government in Uganda.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the role and structure of Uganda's local government systems (k, u, v)
- Understand the contribution and challenges of the local government system (v, u)
- Know the levels of local courts in Uganda (k, u)

#### Topic 3.3: Constitutionalism in Uganda (15 periods)
**Competency:** The learners understands the rule of law in the constitution of Uganda roles, principles and issues related to parliament, the constitution, the judiciary, citizenship, political parties, elections, how they relate to a democratic government.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the importance of the national constitution, national symbols, and values as given in the constitution (v, k, s)
- Know series of constitutional reviews Uganda has had since 1962
- Understand the role of the citizen in the constitutional making process (k, u)
- Understand the structure of the Ugandan parliament, and the roles of the people and bodies within it (u)
- Appreciate the role of the police, prisons and the army (u, v)
- Know the role of the judiciary in Uganda

#### Topic 3.4: Democracy and leadership in East Africa (15 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the roles played by different types of leaders at all levels of society in helping communities and resolving conflicts.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the meaning of the term democracy
- Know the features of democracy
- Understand what a political party is, why it was formed, its role and the challenges (v, u)
- Appreciate the benefits of the most democratic countries in East Africa
- Understand the different types of leadership in our communities (u)

#### Topic 3.5: The United Nations Organisation and its impact on Uganda (16 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the role of UN in pursuing peace and development of member states.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the essential facts about the history, aims and structure of the UN (k)
- Know the role of the UN in maintaining world peace and development drawing examples from Uganda (k)
- Understand the roles and functions of the UN and its subsidiary bodies in detail with a focus on Uganda (u)
- Understand the contribution of the UN in maintaining peace and development in Uganda (s, u)
- Appreciate the role of the UN in promotion of human rights in Uganda

#### Topic 3.6: The evolution of human rights in Uganda (16 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the rule of law and basic human rights, and their importance to a peaceful society and their own lives in order to evaluate the benefits of the United Nations Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Appreciate the difference between "rights" and "responsibilities"
- Understand the concept of human rights, the rule of law and civil society (k)
- Know various ways of violating human rights and how they can be avoided (v, u, s)
- Appreciate the need to respect human rights and the rule of law in order to maintain a peaceful society (v, u)
- Appreciate the benefits to the people of Uganda in the government following the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (k, u)

#### Topic 3.7: The post-independence liberation struggles in Uganda (18 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands and evaluates the causes and the results of continued struggles of independence after 1962.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand the political situation that led to the 1967 Uganda Crisis (u)
- Know the key events that characterised the 1967 Crisis and their results (s, u)
- Know the events that proceeded the 1966 crisis and their impact on politics of Uganda (e.g. the 1971 coup, 1980 general elections, 1980-85 civil war in Luwero Triangle, etc.)

#### Topic 3.8: Patriotism in Uganda (16 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands the various meanings of patriotism and how it is practiced in Uganda.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the term "Patriotism" (k)
- Understand the purpose of patriotism (u)
- Know the personalities who did patriotic work for Uganda (u)
- Know patriotic events in Uganda since independence time
- Appreciate the impact of patriotism in Uganda since 1960

#### Topic 3.9: Key contributors to nation building in the post-colonial Uganda (17 periods)
**Competency:** The learner understands and evaluates the influence of key personalities in history of Uganda since independence.

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Know the background for the independence of Uganda
- Appreciate the personalities who have excelled in politics of Uganda
- Know the names of key Ugandans who have contributed to the promotion of social sector of Uganda
- Appreciate the efforts of key Ugandans in economic field since independence

## ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

### Formative Assessment
- **Observation:** Watching learners working (assessing skills)
- **Conversation:** Asking questions and talking to learners (assessing knowledge and understanding)
- **Product:** Appraising learner's work (written reports, presentations, practical outcomes)

### Summative Assessment
- End of year examinations focusing on application of learning
- Teacher assessment contributing 20% to final School Leaving Certificate grade
- Four-point achievement scale for each topic:
  - 0: No Learning Outcome achieved
  - 1: Some LOs achieved, but not sufficient for overall achievement
  - 2: Most LOs achieved, enough for overall achievement
  - 3: All LOs achieved – achievement with ease

### Key Assessment Areas
- **Historical Knowledge:** Understanding of historical events, periods, and personalities
- **Political Education:** Knowledge of governance systems, democracy, and civic rights
- **Critical Analysis:** Ability to analyze causes and effects of historical events
- **Research Skills:** Use of primary and secondary sources, field investigations
- **Communication:** Oral presentations, written reports, debates
- **Problem Solving:** Conflict resolution, peace building, and community development
- **Values and Attitudes:** Patriotism, respect for diversity, human rights awareness
